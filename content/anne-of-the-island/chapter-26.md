# Chapter 26 — Enter Christine

## Story Summary

In February, the students at Redmond are preparing for a reception that the Juniors are giving for the Seniors. At Patty’s Place, Anne and the other girls are getting dressed for the evening. Anne wears a cream silk dress covered with tiny embroidered rosebuds. Phil had taken the dress home during the Christmas holidays and added the embroidery herself, and the result has become one of Anne’s prettiest dresses.

Roy Gardner has sent Anne white orchids to wear to the reception. Phil admires Anne’s appearance but says the orchids do not really suit her. Anne agrees. She says she prefers flowers that feel more natural and familiar.

Phil has received pink rosebuds from Jonas Blake, but Jonas will not be at the reception because he has a prayer meeting to lead. Phil worries that he may not care for her as much as she hopes because he still has not proposed. Anne tells her that Jonas is probably holding back because he is poor and cannot offer Phil the wealthy life she has always known. Phil decides that if Jonas does not ask her, she may eventually ask him herself.

Then Phil casually mentions Gilbert Blythe. She tells Anne that Gilbert has been spending a great deal of time with Christine Stuart, the sister of Ronald Stuart. Christine is in Kingsport studying music, and Phil has heard that she is very attractive. She also says people think Gilbert is very interested in her.

Anne tries to react as if the news means nothing. However, she suddenly has trouble fastening the clasp of a small gold chain around her neck. She becomes quiet and impatient, even scolding Rusty when he sits on a cushion. Phil assumes that everything has worked out properly because Anne refused Gilbert and now has Roy.

Downstairs, Aunt Jamesina is warming the girls’ coats near the fire. Roy is already waiting for Anne. He is polite and charming, and everyone at Patty’s Place likes him. Aunt Jamesina often says that Anne is lucky because Roy is such an attractive young man.

Roy’s courtship has been exactly the kind of romantic attention Anne once imagined she wanted. He sends flowers, gives her compliments, and speaks to her in a soft and serious way. Yet that evening Anne does not respond to him as warmly as usual. She wishes the people around her would stop treating her future with Roy as if it were already decided.

Roy helps Anne with her coat and gives her another poetic compliment. During the short walk to Redmond, Anne is unusually quiet. Roy notices that she looks pale.

As soon as Anne enters the reception room, however, she becomes lively again. Roy thinks her mood has improved, but Anne’s attention has moved across the room. Gilbert is standing under the palms talking to a young woman. Anne immediately realizes that the woman must be Christine Stuart.

Christine is tall, dark-haired, and very handsome. She has large dark-blue eyes and the kind of elegant appearance Anne once wished she herself could have. Anne studies Christine carefully. She notices the smooth dark hair, clear complexion, and beautiful eyes and thinks that Christine looks almost exactly like one of Anne’s old romantic ideals.

Anne feels uncomfortable and jealous, although she does not call the feeling jealousy. She reminds herself that she refused Gilbert and that his friendship with Christine should not matter to her. She also tells herself that Roy is the man she has chosen to care about.

Still, her attention remains fixed on Gilbert and Christine. She even finds small comfort in deciding that Christine’s figure is not as good as hers and that Christine’s nose is not as attractive.

Nothing dramatic happens between Anne and Gilbert at the reception. The important change is in Anne’s reaction. She has Roy beside her, the romantic admirer she once believed she wanted, but the sight of Gilbert with Christine immediately affects her in a way she cannot ignore.

## Conversation 1 — Getting Ready for the Reception

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Phil: Roy sent you orchids again?
Anne: Yes, but I’m not sure they really **go with** me.
Phil: I agree. They’re beautiful, but they don’t feel like you.
Anne: I’d rather **stick with** simpler flowers.
Phil: Meanwhile, Jonas sent me roses and then **backed out of** the reception.
Anne: He didn’t back out. He has another responsibility tonight.
Phil: I know, but I wish he would finally say what he feels.
Anne: Give him time. He may be **holding back** for a reason.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- go with — match or suit something. Those shoes go well with your dress.
- stick with — continue choosing or using something. I think I’ll stick with the simpler option.
- back out of — decide not to do something you had agreed or planned to do. He backed out of the trip at the last minute.
- hold back — stop yourself from saying or doing something. She was holding back because she was nervous.

## Conversation 2 — Seeing Gilbert and Christine

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Anne: Is that Christine Stuart with Gilbert?
Phil: Yes. Why?
Anne: No reason. I was just asking.
Phil: You suddenly **went quiet**.
Anne: I’m fine.
Phil: You don’t have to **pretend not to care**.
Anne: Gilbert is free to spend time with anyone he likes.
Phil: Of course. But that doesn’t mean the news can’t **catch you off guard**.
Anne: Maybe it did, just a little.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- go quiet — suddenly stop talking or become less talkative. He went quiet when the subject came up.
- pretend not to care — act as if something does not affect you. She pretended not to care about the result.
- catch someone off guard — surprise someone when they are not prepared. The question caught me off guard.
- spend time with — be together with someone for a period of time. She spends a lot of time with her classmates.
