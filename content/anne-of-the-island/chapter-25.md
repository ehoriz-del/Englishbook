# Chapter 25 — Enter Prince Charming

## Story Summary

In November of Anne’s third year at Redmond, she has an afternoon with no classes or plans. She stands at a window in Patty’s Place and tries to decide whether to stay indoors or go to the park.

Inside, Patty’s Place is warm and comfortable. There is a fire, a plate of apples, the three cats, and the familiar china dogs Gog and Magog. Outside, however, the park offers open space, gray water, pine trees, and fresh air.

Aunt Jamesina encourages Anne to go outside because she thinks Anne has been looking pale. She also tells Anne to take an umbrella because her rheumatism makes her believe rain is coming.

Anne decides to go to the park. She is feeling restless and wants to be alone. Most students are attending a football game, so she expects the park to be quiet.

As she walks through the pines, the wind becomes stronger. Anne has been feeling less cheerful than usual since returning to Redmond. Life at Patty’s Place is still pleasant, but something feels different. Gilbert sometimes visits on Friday evenings, yet their old friendship is gone. He treats Anne politely but no longer speaks to her with the closeness they once had.

Anne tells herself that she is glad Gilbert seems to have recovered from her rejection. She does not want him to be unhappy because of her. At the same time, she notices that his distance troubles her more than she expected.

Several other young men have shown interest in Anne, but she refuses to encourage any of them. She still believes that if she ever falls in love, the man should match the romantic ideal she has imagined for years.

While Anne is walking, Aunt Jamesina’s prediction comes true. Heavy rain begins suddenly. Anne opens her umbrella, but a strong gust of wind turns it inside out.

At that moment a young man appears and offers to share his umbrella. Anne looks up and is immediately struck by his appearance. He is tall, handsome, and well dressed, with dark eyes and a pleasant voice. To Anne, he looks very much like the romantic hero she has always imagined.

The young man suggests that they hurry to a small pavilion nearby and wait for the shower to pass. Anne agrees.

Inside the pavilion, they begin to talk. Anne notices that he is wearing Redmond colors. He introduces himself as Royal Gardner. He recognizes Anne because he heard her read a paper on Tennyson at a college literary meeting.

Roy explains that he studied at Redmond for his first two years but then spent two years in Europe because his mother’s health was poor. He has now returned to finish his Arts degree. This makes him a Junior, the same year as Anne.

The rain continues for almost an hour. Anne and Roy talk easily, and the time passes quickly. When the weather clears, they walk back toward Patty’s Place together.

Before they reach the gate, Roy asks Anne whether he may call on her. Anne agrees. She enters the house feeling excited and distracted.

Later that evening a package arrives for Anne. Inside is a box containing a dozen long-stemmed roses. Phil finds the card and immediately recognizes Roy Gardner’s name.

Phil knows about the Gardner family and tells Anne that they are wealthy and socially important in Kingsport. She also knows that Roy left college to travel with his sick mother and says people admired him for doing it without complaining.

Phil quickly realizes that Anne is interested in Roy. She teases Anne about the roses and about the way Anne blushes when his name is mentioned.

Anne tries to dismiss the teasing, but that night she remains awake thinking about Roy. His appearance, voice, manners, and romantic entrance in the rain all match the picture of a “Prince Charming” she has carried in her imagination for years. Anne begins to wonder whether the person she has always dreamed of has finally appeared.

Roy’s appearance is not the only thing that attracts Anne. His manner is polished, he speaks easily, and he seems to understand the kind of romantic language Anne has always enjoyed. Their first conversation feels unusually natural to her, which makes the unexpected meeting seem even more important.

## Conversation 1 — The Rainy Meeting

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Roy: Your umbrella doesn’t look very useful anymore.
Anne: The wind **turned it inside out**.
Roy: You can **share mine** until the rain stops.
Anne: Thank you. That would help.
Roy: There’s a pavilion nearby. We can **wait it out** there.
Anne: Good idea.
Roy: I think I recognize you from Redmond.
Anne: Then maybe we should **introduce ourselves** properly.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- turn something inside out — reverse something so the inside faces outward. The wind turned my umbrella inside out.
- share something — use something together with another person. We shared an umbrella.
- wait something out — wait until an unpleasant situation ends. We waited the storm out in a café.
- introduce yourself — tell someone your name and who you are. He introduced himself after the meeting.

## Conversation 2 — After the Roses Arrive

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Phil: A dozen roses? Someone is **trying to impress** you.
Anne: Don’t start.
Phil: Roy Gardner certainly knows how to **make an entrance**.
Anne: I only met him this afternoon.
Phil: And he already **asked to call on** you.
Anne: Yes.
Phil: You’re blushing.
Anne: I’m not.
Phil: Anne, I think your Prince Charming has finally **shown up**.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- try to impress — make an effort to make someone admire you. He tried to impress her with an expensive gift.
- make an entrance — arrive in a noticeable or impressive way. She made quite an entrance at the party.
- call on someone — visit someone socially; a more formal expression. He asked permission to call on her.
- show up — arrive or appear. He showed up just when the rain began.
