# Chapter 34 — John Douglas Speaks at Last

## Story Summary

After the strange evening at prayer meeting, Anne hopes that Janet’s brief show of independence might finally make John Douglas explain himself. Nothing changes.

John continues visiting Janet, taking her driving, and walking home with her exactly as he has done for years. He still says nothing about marriage.

The summer moves toward its end. Anne teaches, studies a little, writes letters, and enjoys her walks to and from school through a beautiful green swamp crossed by a narrow stream.

Valley Road is pleasant but sometimes quiet enough to feel monotonous. One evening Sam Toliver, the tall hired boy from next door, provides an unexpected change.

Sam comes to Wayside and sits near Anne. After a long silence, he tells her he is thinking of renting a farm in Millersville. Then he explains that if he has a farm of his own, he will need a woman.

Anne does not understand where the conversation is going until Sam suddenly asks whether she will “have” him.

Anne is shocked to realize that Sam is proposing marriage.

She refuses immediately and tells him they barely know each other. Sam calmly replies that they could get acquainted after they were married. He also points out that he works hard and has money in the bank.

Anne refuses again. Sam tells her she is a likely-looking girl and says he wants a hardworking wife. He suggests that she think about his offer because he does not plan to change his mind immediately.

Then he leaves because he has cows to milk.

Anne has received enough surprising proposals by this point in her life that she is able to laugh about Sam’s. That evening she imitates the conversation for Janet, and they both laugh.

A much more serious event occurs near the end of Anne’s stay.

One afternoon Alec Ward arrives at Wayside in a hurry and asks for Janet. Mrs. Douglas is very ill. Alec says this illness seems different from her usual attacks because she is quiet instead of complaining loudly.

Janet goes immediately to the Douglas home.

When she returns at twilight, she tells Anne that Mrs. Douglas has died. Shortly before her death, Mrs. Douglas asked Janet if she supposed she would marry John now.

Janet is deeply hurt by the question. It makes her believe that even Mrs. Douglas thought Janet had been refusing John because she did not want to care for his mother.

Anne tries to comfort Janet and makes her a hot drink, accidentally using white pepper instead of ginger. Janet does not notice.

The evening after the funeral, Anne and Janet sit on the porch at sunset. Janet is still grieving when the gate opens and John Douglas walks into the garden.

He comes directly toward Janet, hardly noticing Anne.

Without any delay, John asks Janet to marry him.

Janet’s first response is to ask why he did not propose years earlier.

John finally explains everything. Nineteen years before, during one of his mother’s serious illnesses, Mrs. Douglas made him promise that he would not ask Janet to marry him while she was alive.

At the time, the doctor believed Mrs. Douglas had only about six months to live. John did not want to make the promise, but his sick mother begged him. She also forced him to promise that he would never tell anyone about it.

Mrs. Douglas recovered and lived for nineteen more years. During all that time, John remained bound by his promise. He says he repeatedly begged his mother to release him from it, but she refused.

John tells Janet that he has suffered too. He knew he was hurting her but could neither propose nor explain why he could not.

Anne realizes that this private conversation does not belong to her and quietly leaves.

The next morning Janet tells Anne that she and John will marry the following month.

John wanted to tell people the truth about his mother’s promise, but Janet refused. She does not want to damage the memory of the woman who was still John’s mother. She says she no longer cares what other people think because she finally knows the truth herself.

## Conversation 1 — Sam’s Unexpected Proposal

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Sam: I’m thinking about renting a farm.
Anne: That sounds like a big change.
Sam: I’ll need someone to **help out** around the place.
Anne: I suppose so.
Sam: Would you marry me?
Anne: Sam, that completely **caught me off guard**.
Sam: You could think it over.
Anne: No. I’m going to **turn you down**.
Sam: Well, I thought I’d ask.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- help out — give practical help. I help out on the farm on weekends.
- catch someone off guard — surprise someone unexpectedly. His question caught me off guard.
- think something over — consider something carefully before deciding. Think it over before you answer.
- turn someone down — reject an offer or request. She turned him down politely.

## Conversation 2 — John Finally Explains

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Janet: Why didn’t you ask me years ago?
John: I made a promise to my mother.
Janet: For nineteen years?
John: I tried to **get out of** the promise, but she refused.
Janet: Why didn’t you tell me?
John: She made me promise to **keep it to myself**.
Janet: I wish I had known.
John: I wanted to explain, but I had to **keep my word**.
Janet: At least I understand now.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- get out of — escape an obligation or responsibility. He tried to get out of the agreement.
- keep something to yourself — not tell other people something. She kept the news to herself.
- keep your word — do what you promised. He kept his word even when it was difficult.
- understand now — finally see the reason for something. I understand now why she acted that way.
