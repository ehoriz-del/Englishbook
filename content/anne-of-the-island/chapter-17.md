# Chapter 17 — A Letter from Davy

## Story Summary

One November evening, the girls at Patty’s Place are together while snow begins to fall outside. Philippa comes in after shopping and announces that the price of butter has gone up five cents a pound. Stella, who manages the household accounts, is not pleased. Phil jokes that simple household worries are a new experience for her and says that shopping is almost more interesting than flirting.

Aunt Jamesina says that although prices are rising, air and salvation are still free. Anne adds that laughter is free too, which is useful because she has received a letter from Davy that she expects everyone to enjoy. Before they begin their evening studying, Anne sits down to read it aloud.

Davy begins by saying that everyone at Green Gables is well. It is snowing in Avonlea, and Marilla has told him that the old woman in the sky is shaking her feather beds. Davy immediately asks Anne whether the old woman is God’s wife. As usual, he fills the letter with questions that he expects Anne to answer.

He reports that Mrs. Rachel Lynde has recently had an accident. She fell down the cellar stairs and grabbed a shelf as she fell. The shelf came down with her, along with milk pails and cooking pans. The noise was so great that Marilla first thought there had been an earthquake.

Mrs. Lynde hurt her ribs, and the doctor gave her medicine that was supposed to be rubbed on the injury. She misunderstood the instructions and swallowed the medicine instead. According to Davy, the doctor said it was a wonder that the medicine did not kill her. Mrs. Lynde survived and later claimed that doctors did not know very much anyway. One saucepan, however, was damaged beyond repair.

Davy then writes about Thanksgiving. There was no school, and Green Gables had a large dinner. He lists everything he ate, including turkey, mince pie, fruit cake, doughnuts, cheese, jam, and chocolate cake. Marilla warned him that he would make himself sick, but he proudly reports that he did not. Dora, on the other hand, had a stomachache afterward.

The new teacher at school is a man who enjoys giving unusual assignments. He asks the boys in Davy’s class to write about the kind of wife they would like and asks the girls to describe their ideal husbands. Davy includes his composition in the letter. He wants a fifteen-year-old wife with good manners, curly hair, a tidy house, and meals served on time. He also expects her to obey him and says that if he gets the right wife, he plans to be a very good husband.

Davy continues with local news. He has attended Mrs. Isaac Wright’s funeral and wonders why people are told not to speak badly of the dead. He has also offended Mrs. Lynde by asking whether she was alive in Noah’s time.

He writes about Mr. Harrison’s attempt to get rid of a dog. The first time Mr. Harrison tried to hang the dog, it recovered and ran to the barn while he was digging the grave. Mr. Harrison then repeated the attempt. Davy also reports on a new hired man, a lazy worker at the Barry farm, and the death of Mrs. Harmon Andrews’s prize pig.

Milty Boulter has been sick and has to take unpleasant medicine. Davy offered to drink it for him in exchange for a quarter, but Milty preferred to keep his money. Davy also says he asked Mrs. Boulter how a person catches a man, remembering the expression he heard before Anne left for college. Mrs. Boulter became angry and replied that she had never chased men.

The Avonlea Village Improvement Society plans to repaint the hall because people are tired of the blue color. Davy also describes the new minister coming to tea and eating three pieces of pie very quickly. Davy cannot understand why adults criticize him for eating that way when a minister can do it.

He ends the letter with kisses from himself and Dora. Then he adds one final question in a postscript, asking Anne who the devil’s father was. By the time Anne finishes reading, the household has received exactly the laughter she promised.

## Conversation 1 — Reading Davy’s Letter

Anne: Everything, including things nobody asked about.
Phil: That sounds like Davy.
Anne: He also **brings up** questions no one knows how to answer.
Aunt Jamesina: That boy certainly keeps people thinking.
Anne: Just wait until you hear his last question.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- read something out loud — read so that other people can hear. She read the letter out loud to the group.
- fill someone in on — tell someone details they do not know. He filled me in on the latest news.
- bring something up — introduce a subject into a conversation. She brought up a difficult question.
- keep someone thinking — make someone continue considering or wondering about something. His questions keep everyone thinking.

## Conversation 2 — Household Costs

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Stella: Butter has **gone up** again.
Phil: Only five cents, but I noticed it.
Stella: We’ll have to **cut back on** something else.
Phil: I’m learning more about budgets every week.
Anne: We’ll **work it out**.
Aunt Jamesina: At least laughter is still free.
Phil: Good, because we’re not **giving that up**.
Stella: Agreed.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- go up — increase in price, amount, or level. Food prices have gone up this month.
- cut back on — reduce the amount of something you use or spend. We need to cut back on unnecessary expenses.
- work something out — find a solution to a problem. We’ll work out a better budget.
- give something up — stop doing or having something. I’m not giving up coffee completely.
