# Chapter 39 — Deals with Weddings

## Story Summary

Anne returns to Green Gables after graduation and her painful break with Roy. At first, home does not feel as comforting as she expected.

She misses the constant friendship of Patty’s Place, and she is embarrassed when she thinks about how badly she misunderstood her feelings for Roy. Before leaving Kingsport, Dorothy Gardner visits Anne and assures her that their friendship will continue.

Dorothy also tells Anne not to believe that Roy’s life is permanently ruined. Roy has had two earlier romantic disappointments and recovered from both. Anne feels relieved, though slightly annoyed because Roy told her she was the only woman he had ever loved.

When Anne reaches Green Gables, she discovers another change. The old tree she called the Snow Queen has blown down during a March storm. Marilla explains that it had become rotten inside.

Anne also feels Diana’s absence. For years Diana had always been one of the first people Anne saw when she came home, but now Diana has a household and family of her own.

Mrs. Lynde and Marilla give Anne the latest Avonlea news. Jane Andrews has returned from the West and is engaged to a wealthy man named Mr. Inglis, who made his money in mining.

Mrs. Lynde is not impressed by the match and complains about Mrs. Harmon Andrews boasting about Jane’s wealthy future.

Marilla asks about Gilbert. Anne explains that he studied extremely hard, received High Honors in Classics, and won the Cooper Prize. She says he looks tired because of the pressure of the past year.

Anne later visits Mrs. Harmon, who proudly describes Jane’s future home, servants, jewelry, and trip to Europe. Mrs. Harmon then asks why Anne is not getting married and mentions the rumor that Gilbert is engaged to Christine Stuart.

Anne answers calmly, although the subject hurts her.

Jane’s wedding takes place at the end of May. Mr. Inglis is older, shorter, and less romantic-looking than Mrs. Harmon’s proud descriptions suggested. Anne still thinks he seems kind and genuinely devoted to Jane.

The following week Anne travels to Bolingbroke to be a bridesmaid at Philippa Gordon’s wedding to Jonas Blake.

Phil looks beautiful, and Jonas is so happy that no one notices whether he is handsome or not. Phil tells Anne that she and Jonas will travel through the land associated with Evangeline before beginning their married life on Patterson Street, where Jonas will serve his mission church.

Phil’s mother still wishes Jonas had chosen a more fashionable church, but Phil does not care. She is completely happy with the life she has chosen.

Anne is sincerely happy for Phil, but she also feels lonely. Many of the people closest to her are beginning new lives while Anne feels uncertain about her own future.

When she returns to Avonlea, she visits Diana and finds another major change. Diana has given birth to her first child, a baby boy named Fred.

Anne looks at Diana with surprise and tenderness. The young mother seems very different from the girl who once shared Anne’s childhood adventures.

Diana proudly asks whether Anne thinks the baby is beautiful. The baby strongly resembles Fred, and Anne cannot honestly call him beautiful, but she thinks he is sweet and lovable.

Diana says she originally hoped for a daughter so she could name her Anne, but now she would not exchange her son for any girl.

Mrs. Allan is visiting Avonlea and joins the conversation. Diana says she wants her child’s earliest memory of her to be a loving one. Mrs. Allan then tells them the only memory she has of her own mother, who died when Mrs. Allan was young.

Afterward Anne walks home through the Birch Path and Willowmere. The trees have grown, friends have married, Diana has become a mother, and the places of Anne’s childhood are no longer exactly the same.

Anne feels unusually alone. She begins to look forward to the end of summer and to having steady work again, hoping that a busy life will make the emptiness less noticeable.

## Conversation 1 — Back in Avonlea

Marilla: Green Gables must feel different this time.
Anne: It does. So much has **changed around here**.
Marilla: That happens when you’ve been away.
Anne: I keep expecting everything to **pick up where it left off**.
Marilla: Life rarely works that way.
Anne: I know. I’ll have to **get used to** the changes.
Marilla: You will, in time.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- change around here — become different in a familiar place. A lot has changed around here.
- pick up where something left off — continue from the point where something previously stopped. We picked up where we left off.
- get used to — become comfortable with something new. She is getting used to the new routine.
- be away — be absent from a place. I was away for several months.

## Conversation 2 — Phil’s Wedding

Phil: I still can’t believe I’m married.
Anne: You look completely happy.
Phil: I am. We’re going to **settle down** near Jonas’s church.
Anne: Your mother is still worried about the neighborhood?
Phil: Yes, but I’m not **giving up** Jonas for a nicer address.
Anne: I never thought you’d choose this life.
Phil: Neither did I. But everything else **fell into place** once I knew I loved him.
Anne: Then you made the right choice for yourself.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- settle down — begin a stable home or married life. They plan to settle down near the church.
- give up — stop having or doing something. She would not give him up for a richer life.
- fall into place — begin to make sense or develop successfully. Everything fell into place after the decision.
- choose for yourself — make your own decision. You have to choose for yourself.
