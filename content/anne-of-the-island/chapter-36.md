# Chapter 36 — The Gardners’ Call

## Story Summary

One day at Patty’s Place, Phil sorts the mail and hands Anne what looks like an ordinary circular. Anne quickly realizes that it is not a circular at all. It is a letter from the editor of The Youth’s Friend.

The editor has accepted a short sketch Anne sent two weeks earlier and has enclosed a check for ten dollars. He also says he would like to see more of her work.

Anne is thrilled. The accepted piece is the old garden sketch she found among her Story Club papers. She rewrote it and sent it away without expecting much because it did not have a strong plot. After the embarrassing experience of “Averil’s Atonement,” this acceptance feels very different. Anne is especially pleased because the money has come from writing she respects.

Priscilla jokes that Patty’s Place now has a real author living in it. Aunt Jamesina becomes serious and tells Anne that writing for publication is a responsibility. She repeats a rule once used by her daughter: never write anything you would be ashamed to hear read at your own funeral. The girls laugh, partly because Aunt Jamesina admits that her daughter always laughed when she said it.

Anne spends the day happily thinking about writing. Even seeing Gilbert walking with Christine Stuart cannot completely spoil her mood.

During a walking party, Roy Gardner asks whether Anne will be home on Saturday afternoon. When she says yes, he tells her that his mother and sisters are coming to call on her.

Anne understands what the visit means. She has never met Roy’s family, and she knows they are aware that he is serious about her. Their decision to visit suggests that they are considering Anne as someone who may become part of the Gardner family.

Anne tells herself that she will simply be herself, but she immediately begins thinking about which dress to wear and how to arrange her hair.

The next afternoon is Friday, not Saturday, and Patty’s Place is completely unprepared for formal visitors. Stella is surrounded by papers for a college assignment. Anne is sitting on the floor in ordinary clothes with cats in her lap. Priscilla is wearing a large kitchen apron and has flour on her nose because she has just finished icing a chocolate cake.

Then the door knocker sounds. Phil opens the door and discovers Mrs. Gardner and Roy’s two sisters, Aline and Dorothy. Roy has apparently given them the wrong day.

Everyone reacts quickly. Anne gets to her feet, Priscilla hides the chocolate cake under a sofa cushion and runs upstairs, and Stella tries to gather her papers. Phil and Aunt Jamesina remain calm and help the visit begin normally.

Mrs. Gardner is tall, elegant, and carefully polite. Aline is similar to her mother but more openly proud and patronizing. Dorothy is completely different. She is lively, friendly, and informal, and Anne likes her almost immediately.

The call goes reasonably well, although two accidents make it memorable. First, Rusty and Joseph suddenly race through the room and jump across Mrs. Gardner’s silk-covered lap. Mrs. Gardner makes it clear that she does not like cats. Dorothy, however, says she loves them.

The second accident happens when Aline goes to examine Gog and Magog, the china dogs. She sits down on the cushion under which Priscilla has hidden the chocolate cake. Anne and Priscilla can do nothing but watch while Aline remains seated on it.

When the Gardners finally leave, Dorothy stays behind for a moment. She warmly tells Anne that Roy has told her a great deal about her and says she hopes she and Anne can become friends. She asks if she may visit Patty’s Place often.

Anne gladly agrees. She knows she will probably never be close to Aline, and Mrs. Gardner will take time to know, but she genuinely likes Dorothy.

After the visitors leave, Priscilla lifts the cushion and discovers the completely flattened cake. Anne goes upstairs feeling strangely emotional. The visit has gone better than she feared, yet its meaning makes her future with Roy feel more serious and more difficult to ignore.

## Conversation 1 — Anne’s First Acceptance

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Phil: Anne, why are you smiling like that?
Anne: My story was **accepted for publication**.
Phil: Really? That’s wonderful!
Anne: They even sent me ten dollars.
Phil: So all that writing is finally **paying off**.
Anne: A little. I almost didn’t **send it in**.
Phil: Good thing you did.
Anne: The editor even asked to see more of my work.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- pay off — produce a good result after effort. All her practice finally paid off.
- send something in — submit something for consideration. I sent the article in last week.
- hear back from — receive a reply from someone. I finally heard back from the editor.
- work on — spend time improving or developing something. She is working on another story.

## Conversation 2 — The Unexpected Visit

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Priscilla: They’re here today? I thought they were coming tomorrow.
Anne: So did I. We were completely **caught off guard**.
Phil: Don’t panic. We can **pull this off**.
Stella: My papers are everywhere.
Priscilla: And I just hid a cake under a cushion.
Anne: Just **act normal**.
Phil: Exactly. We’ll get through the visit somehow.
Priscilla: I only hope no one sits on that cushion.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- catch someone off guard — surprise someone when they are not prepared. The early visit caught us off guard.
- pull something off — succeed at something difficult. Somehow we pulled the dinner off.
- act normal — behave as if nothing unusual is happening. Just act normal and keep talking.
- get through — successfully finish a difficult situation. We’ll get through the meeting.
