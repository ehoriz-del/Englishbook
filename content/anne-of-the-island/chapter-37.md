# Chapter 37 — Full-fledged B.A.’s

## Story Summary

Final examinations arrive at Redmond, and the students at Patty’s Place are exhausted from weeks of studying. Phil complains dramatically about her philosophy examination and worries that she may fail. Anne tells her she will survive, and the girls joke about whether four years at college have actually made them wiser.

Aunt Jamesina says that college cannot teach everything. She believes experience teaches practical sense in ways that books cannot. Phil, Anne, Priscilla, and Stella defend their education and talk about what they have learned beyond academic subjects.

After the examinations are finished, the results are announced. Anne receives High Honors in English. Priscilla earns Honors in Classics, Phil earns Honors in Mathematics, and Stella receives strong results across her subjects.

Gilbert also does extremely well. He receives High Honors in Classics and wins the Cooper Prize, an award that has not been given for five years.

Convocation finally arrives. Anne has waited for this day for years. On her table are two boxes of flowers. Roy has sent violets, and Gilbert has sent lilies-of-the-valley with his card.

At first Anne intends to carry Roy’s violets. Roy is the man everyone expects her to marry, and Anne herself assumes an engagement will soon be announced. Yet just before leaving Patty’s Place, she puts the violets aside and chooses Gilbert’s lilies instead.

The lilies remind Anne of Green Gables, Avonlea, and the old dreams she and Gilbert once shared about graduating from college. Long ago they imagined the day when they would both receive their degrees. Now that day has arrived, and Gilbert’s flowers feel connected to that history in a way Roy’s do not.

Anne receives her cap and diploma and officially becomes a Bachelor of Arts. Roy notices that she is carrying Gilbert’s flowers, and Gilbert also sees them.

For Anne, however, the strongest memory of Convocation is not the diploma or the academic honor. A strange feeling of sadness remains underneath the celebration.

That evening the Arts graduates hold a dance. While getting ready, Anne opens a small box she received at Christmas. Inside is a delicate gold chain with a tiny pink enamel heart that Gilbert had sent her as a gift from an “old chum.”

Anne has never worn it before. The heart reminds her of the childhood day when Gilbert called her “Carrots” and later tried to make peace by offering her a pink candy heart. That evening she puts the chain around her neck.

On the way to the dance, Phil casually tells Anne that she has heard a rumor: Gilbert and Christine Stuart are going to announce their engagement after Convocation.

Anne says very little. In the darkness she reaches for Gilbert’s gold chain, pulls hard enough to break it, and puts it in her pocket.

At the dance Anne acts cheerful. When Gilbert asks her to dance, she tells him her card is already full. She does not show anyone how strongly the rumor has affected her.

Later the girls return to Patty’s Place and sit near the fire, talking about the day. Anne joins in brightly.

Aunt Jamesina tells them that Moody Spurgeon MacPherson visited while they were gone because he did not know about the graduation dance. She makes one of her usual comments about his large ears. When Priscilla reminds her that Moody plans to become a minister, Aunt Jamesina immediately becomes more respectful.

The chapter ends with the girls now fully graduated. Their college work is complete, but their personal futures remain uncertain. Phil knows she will marry Jonas soon. Anne expects Roy to propose, yet her choice of Gilbert’s flowers and her reaction to the rumor about Christine show that her feelings are far less settled than she tells herself.

## Conversation 1 — Exam Results

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Phil: I can’t believe exams are finally **over with**.
Anne: And you did very well.
Phil: So did you. High Honors in English!
Anne: I’m happy, but it still hasn’t **sunk in**.
Priscilla: We’re actually graduating.
Phil: Four years **went by** unbelievably fast.
Anne: I know. It feels like we just arrived.
Phil: At least all that studying **paid off**.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- be over with — be completely finished. I’m glad the exams are over with.
- sink in — gradually become fully understood or real. The news still hasn’t sunk in.
- go by — pass, especially time. The semester went by quickly.
- pay off — lead to a good result after effort. Her hard work paid off.

## Conversation 2 — Choosing the Flowers

Phil: Aren’t those Roy’s violets?
Anne: Yes.
Phil: Then why are you carrying Gilbert’s lilies?
Anne: I don’t really know. They **bring back** so many memories.
Phil: Of Avonlea?
Anne: And of the plans Gilbert and I used to **talk about**.
Phil: Roy might notice.
Anne: I know, but somehow these flowers **feel right** today.
Phil: Sometimes that tells you more than you expect.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- bring back — cause memories or feelings to return. That song brings back college memories.
- talk about — discuss something. We used to talk about our future plans.
- feel right — seem emotionally or practically correct. This choice just feels right.
- notice something — become aware of something. He noticed the different flowers immediately.
