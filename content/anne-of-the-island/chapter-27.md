# Chapter 27 — Mutual Confidences

## Story Summary

March arrives gently in Kingsport, but the students at Patty’s Place are busy preparing for April examinations. Even Philippa, who usually treats life lightly, is studying seriously. She announces that she plans to compete for the Johnson Scholarship in Mathematics. She could probably do well in Greek too, but she wants to prove to Jonas that she is intelligent.

Aunt Jamesina asks Phil whether she can cook. Phil admits that the only thing she has ever tried to bake was a gingerbread that turned out badly. Aunt Jamesina says education is useful, but she still thinks practical household skills matter too.

The girls then receive good news from Miss Patty Spofford. Miss Patty and Miss Maria have decided to remain abroad for another year and plan to visit Egypt. This means Anne, Phil, Priscilla, Stella, and Aunt Jamesina can stay at Patty’s Place for another college year.

One mild March evening, Phil suddenly puts down her books and asks Anne to walk with her in the park. She says that when she is eighty, she will be glad she chose a beautiful walk instead of another hour of studying.

As they walk among the pines in the quiet evening, Phil tells Anne that she is the happiest girl in the world. Anne immediately understands that Jonas Blake has finally proposed.

Phil explains that she accepted almost before Jonas had finished asking. She had worried for months that he might never propose because he was poor and believed he could not offer her enough. Now she knows that he loves her, and she is completely certain that she wants to marry him.

Phil admits that she is still surprised by her own choice. Jonas is not handsome, wealthy, or socially impressive. He is very different from the kind of husband Phil once said she wanted. Yet she no longer cares about those things.

She has already told Alec and Alonzo that she cannot marry either of them. Both were upset, and Phil cried because she hated hurting them. However, once she understood that she loved Jonas, the decision became simple.

Jonas is going to graduate from St. Columbia and work in a small mission church on Patterson Street. Phil knows that marrying him will mean giving up many luxuries. She jokes about learning to cook, remake dresses, and live with less money. She says that she has learned how to shop for a household while living at Patty’s Place and believes she can learn the rest.

Anne tells Phil that she is deeply happy for her. Phil then turns the conversation toward Anne. She asks whether Anne plans to marry Roy Gardner.

Anne refuses to answer a proposal that Roy has not yet made. Phil points out that everyone at Redmond knows Roy is in love with Anne and asks directly whether Anne loves him.

Anne says she supposes she does. She believes Roy is her ideal. He is handsome, romantic, wealthy, and admired by many other students. On Anne’s birthday he sent her violets and a sonnet praising her beauty. The attention is exactly the kind of romance Anne used to imagine.

However, Anne notices a difference between her feelings for Roy and her reactions to Gilbert. She does not blush when Phil asks whether she loves Roy. Instead, she becomes embarrassed whenever Gilbert or Christine Stuart is mentioned.

Anne also remembers telling Roy a funny story that he did not understand. Gilbert would have understood immediately, and Anne remembers the easy laughter she and Gilbert once shared.

For the moment, Anne does not draw a clear conclusion from these differences. She continues telling herself that Roy is the man she loves because he matches the picture she has carried in her imagination for years.

Phil, meanwhile, has moved in the opposite direction. She once planned to choose a husband by appearance, wealth, and convenience. Now she is preparing to marry Jonas, who fits none of those old requirements but is the one man she is certain she wants.

## Conversation 1 — Phil’s Engagement

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Anne: You look unusually happy today.
Phil: Jonas finally **asked me to marry him**.
Anne: And you said yes?
Phil: Before he could almost finish the question.
Anne: What about Alec and Alonzo?
Phil: I already **turned them down** at Christmas.
Anne: So you finally **made up your mind**.
Phil: Completely. For once, I’m not **going back and forth**.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- ask someone to marry you — propose marriage. He asked her to marry him last weekend.
- turn someone down — reject an offer or request. She turned down the job offer.
- make up your mind — reach a decision. I finally made up my mind.
- go back and forth — repeatedly change between two choices or opinions. I kept going back and forth between the two options.

## Conversation 2 — Anne Talks About Roy

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Phil: Do you think you’re in love with Roy?
Anne: I suppose I am.
Phil: You don’t sound very sure.
Anne: He **checks all the boxes** I once imagined.
Phil: Is that enough?
Anne: I don’t know.
Phil: Maybe you should **pay attention to** how you actually feel around him.
Anne: I’m trying to **sort that out**.
Phil: You don’t have to figure it out tonight.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- check all the boxes — meet all the expected requirements. The apartment checks all the boxes for us.
- pay attention to — notice or focus on something carefully. Pay attention to how you feel after the meeting.
- sort something out — understand or solve something confusing. I need time to sort out my feelings.
- figure something out — understand something after thinking about it. She eventually figured out what she wanted.
