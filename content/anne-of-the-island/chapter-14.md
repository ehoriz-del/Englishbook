# Chapter 14 — The Summons

## Story Summary

As summer continues, Ruby Gillis becomes weaker. She is no longer teaching, and her family has decided that she should rest until at least the new year. Anne visits her often even though the visits are difficult. Ruby usually talks brightly about clothes, parties, and young men, while Anne can see that her health is getting worse.

Mrs. Rachel Lynde worries that Anne may catch Ruby’s illness and complains about the frequent visits. Marilla is also concerned because Anne often comes home looking tired. Anne explains that Ruby always seems to need her company, even though Ruby refuses to talk openly about what is happening.

One evening Anne gives up a moonlight drive to White Sands so she can spend time with Ruby. They sit together in the Gillis garden. Ruby is lying in a hammock with a white shawl around her shoulders. Her sewing is beside her, but she is too tired to work. Her hair is loose because the pins have been giving her a headache.

Unlike on earlier visits, Ruby does not talk about parties, clothes, or admirers. She is quiet. The moon rises, and from the garden they can see the church and the graveyard beyond it.

Ruby suddenly says that she knows she will soon be buried there. Anne is surprised because Ruby has spent the whole summer refusing to admit that she is dying. Ruby tells Anne that she has known the truth for a long time. She has only avoided speaking about it.

Ruby then admits that she is afraid to die. She says she believes she will go to heaven, but the thought still frightens her because it will be completely different from the life she knows. She wants to remain in Avonlea and continue living like other young women.

Anne listens carefully and tries to comfort her. Ruby says she wants the ordinary future she expected to have. She wants to marry, have children, and continue enjoying life. She also tells Anne that Herb Spencer is the young man she truly loves. The other men she talked about were not important to her in the same way.

Ruby begins to cry. Anne does not try to stop her or give quick answers. She stays beside her and holds her hand until Ruby becomes calmer.

Afterward Ruby says she feels better because she has finally spoken honestly. During the daytime she has been able to avoid thinking about death, especially when people visit and the house is busy. At night, however, she has been unable to escape the fear. She says she has wanted to talk to Anne about it during many of Anne’s visits but was afraid that speaking the truth would make death feel more certain.

Ruby asks Anne to come again soon. She says Anne has always been one of the school friends she trusted most. Ruby also mentions Em White, a former friend with whom she had quarreled years before. Em recently came to visit, and the two girls finally ended their old disagreement. Both discovered that they had wanted to make peace for years but had each believed the other would refuse.

Anne leaves the Gillis home late that night. Ruby asks her to return soon, and Anne promises that she will.

Anne never sees Ruby alive again. The following evening, while many people in Avonlea are attending a farewell party for Jane Andrews, Ruby dies quietly in her sleep.

The next morning the news spreads through the village. At the funeral, Ruby’s family and friends gather to say goodbye. Mrs. Gillis later calls Anne into another room and gives her the embroidered centerpiece Ruby had been working on. The needle is still in the cloth where Ruby left it the afternoon before she died. Mrs. Gillis tells Anne that Ruby would have wanted her to keep it.

After the funeral, Diana wants to talk about some of the details, but Anne is not very interested in discussing them. Davy asks whether Ruby will still laugh in heaven because she used to laugh so much in Avonlea. Anne answers him quietly.

## Conversation 1 — Ruby Finally Opens Up

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Ruby: Anne, I need to **open up about** something.
Anne: You can tell me anything.
Ruby: I’ve known for a while that I’m very ill.
Anne: I’m listening.
Ruby: During the day I can **push the thought away**, but at night it comes back.
Anne: That sounds frightening.
Ruby: I’m tired of **pretending everything is fine**.
Anne: You don’t have to pretend with me.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- open up about — begin talking honestly about a personal subject. She finally opened up about how worried she was.
- push something away — try not to think about a difficult thought or feeling. I kept trying to push the worry away.
- pretend everything is fine — act as if there is no problem. He smiled and pretended everything was fine.
- come back — return to your thoughts or memory. The same worry kept coming back at night.

## Conversation 2 — After a Difficult Talk

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Ruby: I’m glad I finally **said it out loud**.
Anne: Sometimes talking helps.
Ruby: I’ve been **holding it in** all summer.
Anne: That must have been exhausting.
Ruby: I kept hoping I could **get away from** the fear.
Anne: But it kept returning?
Ruby: Yes. Tonight I finally **let it out**.
Anne: I’m glad you didn’t have to carry it alone.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- say something out loud — speak words that you had previously kept in your thoughts. It felt strange to say the fear out loud.
- hold something in — keep an emotion or thought inside instead of expressing it. She had been holding her feelings in for months.
- get away from — escape or avoid something unpleasant. I went for a walk to get away from the noise.
- let something out — express an emotion or thought that has been kept inside. He finally let out everything he had been feeling.
