# Chapter 33 — “He Just Kept Coming and Coming”

## Story Summary

Three days after the tea at the Douglas home, Anne returns from school and finds Janet crying. Anne is immediately worried because Janet is usually cheerful and practical.

Janet first says she is crying because it is her fortieth birthday. Anne tries to comfort her by pointing out that she was almost forty the day before and nothing terrible happened.

Then Janet reveals the real reason. John Douglas has never asked her to marry him.

Anne is shocked. She had assumed that John and Janet’s marriage was already understood because Mrs. Douglas spoke about it so confidently.

Janet explains that John has been courting her for twenty years. He began visiting her before Janet’s mother died. He kept coming regularly, and after a while Janet began preparing quilts and other household things because she expected him to propose.

Eight years later Janet’s mother died. Janet thought John might finally ask her to marry him because she was now alone. John was kind and helpful during her grief, but he still said nothing about marriage.

The same pattern continued year after year. John visited, took Janet driving, walked her home from meetings, and behaved as though she were the most important woman in his life. But he never proposed.

People in Valley Road have developed their own explanation. They believe Janet refuses to marry John because his mother is often ill and Janet does not want the work of caring for her.

Janet has allowed people to believe this false story because she would rather be blamed than pitied. In reality, she says she would gladly care for Mrs. Douglas if she could marry John.

What hurts Janet most is not simply that John has not proposed. She cannot understand why. Mrs. Douglas herself often hints that she wants John to marry Janet, so Janet sees no obvious reason for the delay.

Anne becomes angry on Janet’s behalf. She tells Janet that after twenty years John should either speak clearly or stop behaving as though they are engaged.

Anne urges Janet to show more spirit. She asks why Janet never sent John away years ago.

Janet admits that she could not do it because she has always loved him. There was never another man she wanted. She was also afraid that if she told John to stop coming, he might take her seriously and disappear from her life.

Anne insists it is not too late to change the situation. She advises Janet to make it clear that she will no longer accept his endless indecision. She promises to support her.

The following evening, Janet and Anne attend prayer meeting. Janet has decided to take Anne’s advice.

After the meeting, John approaches as usual and asks whether he may walk Janet home.

Janet, nervous but determined, refuses. She tells him coldly that she has known the road home for forty years and can manage without him.

John says nothing. He turns and begins walking away.

Anne watches his face in the moonlight and immediately sees the same expression of pain she noticed at his mother’s house. Her confidence disappears. She suddenly feels that something much more serious is happening than simple indecision.

Without thinking about the people watching, Anne calls after John. When he does not return, she runs after him, catches his arm, and brings him back.

Anne tells John that the whole thing was her fault. She says she persuaded Janet to refuse him and begs him not to leave.

Janet silently takes John’s arm, and they walk home together. Anne follows at a distance and slips into the house through the back door.

Later Janet sarcastically tells Anne that she was not much help after promising to support her.

Anne admits that she could not watch John walk away. Janet confesses that she is actually glad Anne stopped him. When she saw John leaving, she felt as if all the happiness left in her life was going with him.

Still, nothing is explained. John does not ask Janet why she refused his arm, and he says nothing about marriage. The mystery remains.

## Conversation 1 — Janet Finally Talks

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Anne: You’ve known John for twenty years?
Janet: He’s been **coming around** that long.
Anne: And he has never proposed?
Janet: Not once.
Anne: How have you **put up with** that uncertainty?
Janet: I love him. I was afraid if I pushed him, he might **walk away**.
Anne: You deserve an explanation.
Janet: I know, but I’ve never known how to ask for one.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- come around — visit someone, especially regularly or informally. He has been coming around for years.
- put up with — tolerate a difficult situation. I don’t know how she put up with the uncertainty.
- walk away — leave a situation or relationship. She was afraid he might walk away.
- ask for — request something. You have every right to ask for an explanation.

## Conversation 2 — Anne Changes Her Mind

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Janet: You told me to stand firm.
Anne: I know, but when John started to leave, I **panicked**.
Janet: You ran after him!
Anne: I thought everything was about to **fall apart**.
Janet: I was trying to **follow through on** your advice.
Anne: I’m sorry. I couldn’t just **stand by** and watch him go.
Janet: I’m actually glad you stopped him.
Anne: Then maybe I didn’t ruin everything.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- fall apart — break down or fail completely. The plan started to fall apart.
- follow through on — do what you said or planned you would do. She followed through on her promise.
- stand by — remain present without taking action. I couldn’t stand by and do nothing.
- run after — chase or follow someone who is leaving. She ran after him before he reached the gate.
