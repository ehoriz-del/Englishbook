# Chapter 8 — Anne’s First Proposal

## Story Summary

The last night of the year is stormy at Green Gables. Snow blows across the fields and against the windows, and Jane Andrews has come to spend the afternoon and stay overnight. After the girls go to bed in Anne’s little porch room, Jane tells Anne that she has something important to discuss.

Anne is tired after a party at Ruby Gillis’s the night before and expects Jane to tell her about an engagement or some other piece of local news. Instead, Jane suddenly asks what Anne thinks of her brother Billy. Anne is confused because she has hardly ever thought about Billy at all. She knows him as a round-faced, good-natured young man who smiles a great deal, but she has never considered him in any personal way.

Jane asks whether Anne likes Billy. Anne says that she does, in the ordinary sense. Jane then asks whether Anne would like Billy as a husband. Anne is so surprised that she drops back onto her pillows and asks whose husband Jane means. Jane calmly explains that Billy wants to marry Anne.

Billy has not come to Anne himself because he is too shy. Instead, he has persuaded Jane to speak for him. Their father has recently given Billy the upper farm in his own name, so Billy believes he is now in a position to marry. Jane says Billy has always admired Anne and would like to know whether she will accept him.

Anne tells Jane immediately that she cannot marry Billy. The idea has never occurred to her, and she does not care for him in that way. Jane suggests that Anne could at least think the proposal over. She describes Billy as dependable, hardworking, and free of bad habits. She adds that he is willing to wait until Anne finishes college if necessary, although he would prefer to marry in the spring before planting begins.

Anne repeats that there is nothing to consider. She asks Jane to tell Billy clearly that her answer is no. Jane accepts the decision, although her tone becomes cooler. She had not really expected Anne to accept, but she is still slightly offended that Anne has rejected her brother. Jane’s reaction makes it clear that, although she considered Billy’s chances poor, she still expected Anne to recognize the proposal as an honor.

Anne says she hopes Billy will not be deeply hurt. Jane assures her that he will recover and says Billy has too much common sense to break his heart over the refusal. Billy also likes Nettie Blewett, and Mrs. Andrews would actually prefer him to marry Nettie because she considers Nettie an excellent manager and saver. Jane believes Billy will probably turn to Nettie once he knows Anne will not have him. Before going to sleep, Jane asks Anne not to tell anyone about the proposal, and Anne agrees.

Jane falls asleep easily, but Anne remains awake. The whole situation is too unexpected for her to stop thinking about it. The next morning, after Jane has gone home, Anne returns to her room, closes the door, and finally laughs over the strange experience.

Anne would normally tell Diana about something so unusual, but she has promised Jane to keep it private. She also believes Diana now shares almost everything with Fred, so Anne no longer feels able to confide in her in exactly the same way she once did.

Anne thinks about the fact that this was her first marriage proposal. In her imagination, she had always pictured such a moment as graceful and romantic, whether she accepted the man or refused him. Instead, her first proposal has arrived through Jane on behalf of Billy, partly because Billy now owns a farm and thinks it is time to marry.

The practical details make Anne laugh again. Billy has not declared lifelong devotion or made any romantic speech. He has simply asked his sister to find out whether Anne will marry him, and if Anne refuses, he is apparently prepared to consider Nettie Blewett instead. The proposal is completely different from anything Anne had imagined.

## Conversation 1 — Jane Delivers the Message

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Jane: I need to **bring something up** before we go to sleep.
Anne: That sounds serious. What is it?
Jane: Billy asked me to **speak for him**.
Anne: Speak for him about what?
Jane: He wants to know whether you would consider marrying him.
Anne: I didn’t **see that coming** at all.
Jane: You don’t have to answer this second.
Anne: Actually, I’ve already **made up my mind**. I can’t marry him.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- bring something up — introduce a subject into a conversation. She brought up the budget near the end of the meeting.
- speak for someone — communicate another person’s views or wishes on that person’s behalf. I can’t speak for the whole group, but I support the idea.
- see something coming — expect or predict that something is going to happen. I did not see that announcement coming.
- make up your mind — reach a decision. I made up my mind after reviewing all the options.

## Conversation 2 — After the Proposal

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Anne: I still can’t believe Billy had you ask me.
Jane: He couldn’t **work up the courage** to do it himself.
Anne: That makes the whole thing even stranger.
Jane: Are you sure you don’t want to **think it over**?
Anne: I’m sure. I don’t feel that way about him.
Jane: He’ll probably **get over** it quickly.
Anne: I hope so.
Jane: And please don’t **pass this on** to anyone else.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- work up the courage — gradually become brave enough to do something difficult. It took me a week to work up the courage to call.
- think something over — consider something carefully before deciding. I need a day to think the offer over.
- get over — recover emotionally from a disappointment or difficult experience. It took him a while to get over the rejection.
- pass something on — tell or give information to another person. Please don’t pass this information on yet.
