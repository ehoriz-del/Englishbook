# Chapter 40 — A Book of Revelation

## Story Summary

During July, the Irvings return to Echo Lodge, and Anne spends three happy weeks visiting them.

Miss Lavendar remains much as Anne remembers her. Charlotta the Fourth has grown into a young woman but still admires Anne deeply. Paul is now sixteen. His childhood curls are gone, and he is more interested in football than imaginary Rock People, although he and Anne remain close.

One evening Anne returns to Green Gables from Echo Lodge just as a violent summer storm is beginning. Paul has brought her home but has already left for Echo Lodge again.

Anne happily greets Marilla, Mrs. Lynde, Davy, and the familiar house. She asks Davy whether he has grown while she was away.

Davy proudly says he has grown another inch. Then, without warning, he asks Anne whether she knows that Gilbert Blythe is dying.

Anne becomes completely still and turns white.

Mrs. Lynde angrily tells Davy that he should not have announced the news so suddenly. She explains more carefully that Gilbert has typhoid fever.

He became ill shortly after Anne went to Echo Lodge. The case has been serious from the beginning because Gilbert was already exhausted after his intense college work. A trained nurse is caring for him, and the doctor has done everything possible.

Davy adds that Mr. Harrison heard there was almost no hope.

Marilla takes Davy out of the room. Mrs. Lynde tries to comfort Anne, reminding her that Gilbert is young and strong.

Anne cannot stay downstairs. She goes blindly to her old room and kneels by the window.

Outside, the storm becomes violent. Rain beats against the house, trees bend in the wind, and the sea can be heard in the distance.

Anne spends the night awake.

For the first time, she understands her feelings without confusion. The thought of Gilbert dying shows her immediately that she loves him and has always loved him.

She recognizes the difference between her feelings for Gilbert and her former attraction to Roy. Roy had matched the romantic picture Anne had built in her imagination. Gilbert, however, had become part of her actual life through years of friendship, competition, shared memories, arguments, laughter, and trust.

Anne is horrified because she believes she has understood this too late.

She remembers rejecting Gilbert when he proposed at Patty’s Place. If Gilbert dies, she thinks, he will never know that her feelings changed—or that perhaps they were always deeper than she understood.

Anne also realizes that she no longer believes Gilbert ever loved Christine Stuart. In this moment of fear, the rumors that once seemed so convincing no longer matter to her.

All night Anne remains by the window while the storm continues.

Near dawn the weather finally begins to clear. Anne goes downstairs and outside into the wet morning.

Then she hears someone whistling along the road. It is Pacifique Buote, who works for George Fletcher, Gilbert’s uncle and neighbor.

Anne knows Pacifique may have recent news from the Blythe household. She tries several times to call his name before her voice works.

When he comes near, Anne asks if he has heard how Gilbert is that morning.

Pacifique tells her that Gilbert is better. The crisis passed during the night, and the doctor now believes he will recover. He says it was a very close call, but Gilbert is going to be all right.

The news completely changes the world for Anne.

She watches Pacifique continue down the road, remembering that ordinary-looking young man as the person who brought her the best news she could have received.

Anne remains outside in the fresh morning after the storm. The fear that filled the night is gone. Gilbert is alive, and Anne now knows with complete certainty what he means to her.

## Conversation 1 — The Sudden News

Davy: Anne, did you know Gilbert is very sick?
Anne: What do you mean?
Mrs. Lynde: Davy, you shouldn’t have **blurted that out**.
Anne: Please tell me what happened.
Mrs. Lynde: He **came down with** typhoid fever.
Anne: Is he going to recover?
Mrs. Lynde: We don’t know yet, but everyone is doing what they can.
Anne: I need a moment alone.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- blurt something out — say something suddenly without thinking. He blurted the news out before anyone could stop him.
- come down with — become ill with a disease. She came down with the flu.
- do what you can — make every reasonable effort. We’re doing what we can to help.
- need a moment — need a short time to think or recover emotionally. I just need a moment.

## Conversation 2 — Morning News

Anne: Pacifique, have you heard anything about Gilbert?
Pacifique: Yes. He **pulled through** the worst of it last night.
Anne: He’s better?
Pacifique: The doctor thinks he’ll **get better** now.
Anne: Are you sure?
Pacifique: Yes. It was a close call, but he **made it through**.
Anne: Thank you. You have no idea how much that means to me.
Pacifique: I’m glad I had good news.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- pull through — survive a dangerous illness or difficult situation. The doctors believe he will pull through.
- get better — recover or improve. She is finally getting better.
- make it through — survive or successfully endure something difficult. He made it through the night.
- close call — a dangerous situation that almost ended badly. It was a close call, but everyone was safe.
