# Chapter 15 — A Dream Turned Upside Down

## Story Summary

Near the end of the summer, Anne has only one week left before returning to Redmond. She is looking forward to classes, friends, and the new life she and Priscilla, Stella, Philippa, and Aunt Jamesina will have at Patty’s Place.

One evening Anne is having tea with Mr. and Mrs. Harrison when Mr. Harrison asks whether she has written any more stories. Anne answers rather sharply because she still remembers the rejection of “Averil’s Atonement.”

Mr. Harrison then tells her that Mrs. Hiram Sloane recently saw a large envelope addressed to the Rollings Reliable Baking Powder Company. The company had announced a writing competition. Writers were asked to send in a story that mentioned their baking powder, and the best story would win a prize.

Anne says she would never enter such a competition. She considers the idea of using a story to advertise baking powder embarrassing and says she would never write something for that purpose.

That same evening Diana arrives at Green Gables carrying a letter for Anne. She is very excited and tells Anne to open it quickly. The letter is from the Rollings Reliable Baking Powder Company. It says that Anne’s story “Averil’s Atonement” has won the twenty-five-dollar prize. The company plans to publish the story in several Canadian newspapers and print copies for its customers.

Anne is completely confused. She has never entered the contest.

Diana then happily explains what happened. When she saw the competition announcement, she immediately thought of Anne’s story. She believed Anne would never send it herself because she had lost confidence after the magazine rejections. Diana therefore sent in the copy Anne had given her without telling Anne.

There was one problem. “Averil’s Atonement” did not mention baking powder. Diana solved this by changing two parts of the story. In the scene where Averil makes a cake, Diana added that she used Rollings Reliable Baking Powder and that this was why the cake turned out well. Diana also changed the final romantic scene so that Perceval promises that they will never use any other baking powder in their future home.

Diana expects Anne to be delighted. Instead, Anne is horrified. She tries not to hurt Diana because she understands that Diana acted out of friendship. Anne thanks her for trying to help, but she cannot feel proud of the prize.

Anne offers the twenty-five-dollar check to Diana, saying Diana deserves it because she entered the story and changed it. Diana refuses. She says she only wanted to help Anne and is happy simply because her friend won.

After Diana leaves, Anne becomes even more upset. She had written the story seriously and had cared about it, even after it was rejected. Now she imagines it being printed as an advertisement and feels that something personal has been changed into something ridiculous.

Gilbert arrives later and has already heard the news at Orchard Slope. He begins by congratulating Anne but stops when he sees how unhappy she is. Anne explains why the prize embarrasses her. She remembers lessons from Queen’s about writing for serious reasons and worries about what teachers and students at Redmond will think.

Gilbert does not see the situation in the same way. He tells Anne that most Redmond students do not have much money and that they will probably assume she entered the competition to help pay her expenses. He says there is nothing shameful about earning money honestly, even if writing great literature would be more exciting.

Gilbert’s practical view makes Anne feel a little better. She is still upset that Diana changed the story into an advertisement, but she becomes less worried about being laughed at by everyone at Redmond.

Soon the story appears in Island newspapers, and copies are printed as colorful advertising pamphlets. A store in Avonlea gives them to customers. Anne receives a bundle and burns them in the kitchen stove.

Most people in Avonlea, however, think winning the prize is an impressive achievement. Some of Anne’s critics make unkind comments, but many friends are pleased for her. By the time Anne is ready to return to Redmond, she is relieved to leave the whole incident behind and begin her second year of college.

## Conversation 1 — Diana’s Surprise

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Diana: I have some amazing news for you.
Anne: What happened?
Diana: Your story **ended up winning** the baking powder contest.
Anne: What? I never entered it.
Diana: I **sent it in** for you.
Anne: You did what?
Diana: I changed a few lines so it would **fit in with** the contest rules.
Anne: Diana, I don’t know how to **react to** this.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- end up — finally reach a result or situation, often unexpectedly. We ended up staying much later than planned.
- send something in — submit something to a company, school, contest, or organization. She sent her application in yesterday.
- fit in with — match or suit a rule, plan, or situation. The new design fits in with the rest of the room.
- react to — respond emotionally or physically to something. I didn’t know how to react to the news.

## Conversation 2 — Gilbert’s View

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Gilbert: You seem really upset about the prize.
Anne: I’m afraid people will **make fun of** me.
Gilbert: I don’t think they will.
Anne: The story has been turned into an advertisement.
Gilbert: Most students will just see it as a way to **earn some money**.
Anne: You think I’m worrying too much?
Gilbert: A little. Try to **look at it another way**.
Anne: Maybe I can **move on from** the embarrassment eventually.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- make fun of — laugh at or tease someone. I was afraid they would make fun of my mistake.
- earn some money — receive money for work or effort. She took a weekend job to earn some money.
- look at something another way — consider a situation from a different point of view. Try to look at the problem another way.
- move on from — stop focusing on a past problem and continue with life. It took him time to move on from the disappointment.
