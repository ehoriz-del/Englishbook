# Chapter 19 — An Interlude

## Story Summary

Anne turns twenty while living at Patty’s Place. On the evening of her birthday, she is curled up on the hearth rug in the living room with Rusty in her lap. Aunt Jamesina is sitting nearby in her favorite chair, reading. Stella and Priscilla have gone to a committee meeting, and Philippa is upstairs getting ready for a dance.

Anne tells Aunt Jamesina that it feels strange to leave her teenage years behind. She is a little sorry that they are over. She also feels dissatisfied with herself. Years earlier Miss Stacy told her that by the age of twenty a person’s character would have taken a definite direction. Anne worries that her own character still has many faults.

Aunt Jamesina is not concerned. She says everyone has faults and that her own character is cracked in a hundred places. She thinks Miss Stacy probably meant that by twenty a person’s basic direction is formed, not that the person is finished changing. Aunt Jamesina tells Anne not to worry too much about being perfect.

Their conversation then moves to Phil, who is preparing for a dance. Anne describes Phil’s new dress, made of creamy yellow silk and delicate lace. Aunt Jamesina says the words silk and lace still make her want to go dancing. She admits that she always wanted a yellow silk dress when she was young, but her mother and later her husband would never allow it. She jokes that when she gets to heaven, one of the first things she plans to get is a yellow silk dress.

Phil soon comes downstairs dressed for the dance. She stands in front of the long mirror and asks Anne whether she looks right. Anne tells her she looks beautiful. Phil checks whether her skirt is straight and whether the rose she is wearing is placed correctly. She complains that the mirror in her bedroom makes her look green and says a flattering mirror can improve a person’s mood.

Phil and Anne begin comparing their looks. Phil tells Anne that she likes the way Anne’s hair grows around her forehead and says one small curl is especially attractive. Aunt Jamesina joins in by saying that although Phil may be prettier, Anne has the better nose. Anne calmly says that her nose has always been a comfort to her.

The talk soon turns to marriage. Phil expects to marry someday, though she still has not chosen between Alec and Alonzo. Aunt Jamesina thinks this should not be such a difficult decision. Phil says both men are equally nice, which is exactly the problem.

Aunt Jamesina starts suggesting other young men. She mentions Will Leslie, but Phil says his eyes are too large and mild. George Parker looks too stiff and formal to her. Marr Holworthy seems acceptable, but Phil says he is poor. She announces that she wants to marry a rich and handsome man.

Phil then says that she would marry Gilbert Blythe if he were rich. Anne reacts sharply. Phil immediately notices Anne’s tone and teases her. She points out that Anne does not seem to want Gilbert for herself but also does not like the idea of Phil marrying him.

Aunt Jamesina says Phil should not marry anyone she does not love. Phil jokes about old-fashioned love and says she plans to delay marriage as long as possible. Her carriage arrives, and she leaves for the dance.

After Phil is gone, Aunt Jamesina asks Anne whether Phil is always quite sensible. Anne explains that Phil is kind, sweet, and intelligent, but she often talks in a playful way and does not mean everything literally.

Aunt Jamesina says she loves Phil but still finds her difficult to understand because she is unlike the girls Aunt Jamesina knew when she was young. Anne jokingly asks how many different girls Aunt Jamesina used to be. Aunt Jamesina replies that she was about half a dozen.

The evening remains a quiet one at Patty’s Place. Anne’s twentieth birthday passes without a large celebration in the original scene, but the conversations around the fire mark the change from her teenage years into her twenties.

## Conversation 1 — Turning Twenty

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Anne: It feels strange to **leave my teens behind**.
Aunt Jamesina: Are you sorry to be twenty?
Anne: A little. I thought I’d have everything **figured out** by now.
Aunt Jamesina: Nobody has everything figured out at twenty.
Anne: I still notice so many faults in myself.
Aunt Jamesina: Don’t **get hung up on** every little flaw.
Anne: That’s easier said than done.
Aunt Jamesina: You’ll keep changing as you **go along**.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- leave something behind — move beyond a period, place, or situation. She was ready to leave her school years behind.
- figure something out — understand or solve something. I still haven’t figured everything out.
- get hung up on — worry too much about something. Don’t get hung up on one small mistake.
- go along — continue through a process or period of time. You learn more as you go along.

## Conversation 2 — Phil Talks About Marriage

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Phil: I suppose I’ll have to **settle down** someday.
Anne: Only if you actually want to.
Phil: The problem is choosing between Alec and Alonzo.
Aunt Jamesina: You should **make up your mind** eventually.
Phil: I keep **going back and forth**.
Anne: Then maybe neither one is right.
Phil: Perhaps. For now, I’ll **put it off**.
Aunt Jamesina: Just don’t marry someone you don’t love.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- settle down — begin a more stable life, often including marriage. He says he wants to settle down in a few years.
- make up your mind — reach a decision. She finally made up her mind.
- go back and forth — repeatedly change between two choices or opinions. I keep going back and forth between the two options.
- put something off — delay doing something until later. I decided to put the decision off until next month.
