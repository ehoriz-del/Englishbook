# Chapter 1 — The Shadow of Change

## Story Summary

Late in the summer, Anne Shirley and Diana Barry rest in the Green Gables orchard after picking apples. The fields already show signs of autumn, and the two friends talk about the changes that have come to Avonlea. Miss Lavendar has recently married Stephen Irving, Mr. and Mrs. Allan have left the manse, and Anne and Gilbert are about to leave for Redmond College. Diana, now engaged to Fred Wright, says Avonlea will feel very quiet once Anne is gone.

Another change is about to take place at Green Gables. Mrs. Rachel Lynde is moving in the next day, and Marilla and Anne have cleared out the old spare room for her. Anne tells Diana that she disliked changing the room because she had regarded it with almost ceremonial respect as a child. The pictures of George Whitefield and the Duke of Wellington have been moved into the upstairs hall, and the room no longer looks as it once did. Diana says again that she will be lonely without Anne. Anne replies that she expects to be even lonelier at first because Diana will remain among familiar people while she will be surrounded by strangers in Kingsport.

The girls also talk about being eighteen and about how different adulthood feels from what they imagined when they were younger. Diana says that some parts are pleasant but others are confusing. Anne jokes that Diana will soon become a settled married woman while she herself may become an old-maid aunt who comes to visit. Diana insists that Anne will eventually marry someone handsome and successful, but Anne turns the conversation into a joke.

After leaving Diana, Anne walks to the post office and finds a letter from Priscilla Grant. Priscilla’s father has agreed to let her attend Redmond, and she and Anne will board together. Anne is delighted. On her way home, Gilbert Blythe catches up with her on the bridge over the Lake of Shining Waters, and Anne immediately tells him the news.

Anne and Gilbert stop on the bridge and look at the pond in the evening light. Gilbert says he thinks they will like Kingsport. Anne wonders whether any place there can be more beautiful than Avonlea. For a while they stand quietly, remembering the familiar surroundings. Gilbert suddenly places his hand over Anne’s and seems ready to say something serious. Anne quickly pulls her hand away, announces that she must go home, and begins talking rapidly about Marilla and the twins. Gilbert has little chance to say anything before they reach the Green Gables lane.

Inside the house, Anne finds Davy crying on the sofa. He explains that Dora has fallen down the outside cellar steps and scraped her nose, but he is not crying because she was hurt. He is upset because he was not there to see the accident. Anne has difficulty keeping a straight face. Davy then asks whether Mrs. Lynde will put him to bed after Anne leaves. He says he does not want to say his prayers in front of Mrs. Lynde because she still feels like a stranger to him.

Davy asks why Anne is leaving when she does not really want to go. Anne says that she feels she ought to go. Davy replies that when he grows up, he intends to do only what he wants. He then repeats something Milty Boulter heard from his mother: that Anne is going to college to “catch a man.” Anne is annoyed but tells Davy that she is going to Redmond to study, grow, and learn. Davy asks what she expects to learn, and Anne answers by quoting a playful list about “shoes and ships and sealing wax” and other things. Davy immediately asks how a woman would go about catching a man. Anne carelessly tells him to ask Mrs. Boulter, then realizes what she has said and orders him not to do it. Davy protests that Anne herself told him to ask, and Anne ends the discussion by sending him to bed.

After Davy goes to bed, Anne walks down to Victoria Island and sits alone beside the water. She listens to the brook and lets her imagination wander over the stories and places she has always loved.

## Conversation 1 — Anne and Diana Talk About Change

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Diana: Everything seems to be changing at the same time.
Anne: I know. I’m trying to **take it all in** without getting overwhelmed.
Diana: Are you having second thoughts about Redmond?
Anne: Not exactly, but leaving Green Gables is harder than I expected.
Diana: At least Priscilla will be there with you.
Anne: Yes. That news really **put me at ease**.
Diana: And we’ll still write to each other.
Anne: Absolutely. I don’t want distance to **get in the way of** our friendship.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- take something in — absorb or fully understand information or an experience. I needed a few minutes to take in everything they had told me.
- have second thoughts — begin to doubt a decision you have already made. I had second thoughts about moving, but I decided to go.
- put someone at ease — make someone feel calmer and less nervous. Her friendly manner immediately put me at ease.
- get in the way of — prevent something from happening or make it more difficult. We won’t let busy schedules get in the way of staying in touch.

## Conversation 2 — Anne and Gilbert at the Bridge

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Gilbert: You got quiet all of a sudden.
Anne: I was just **taking in** the view before we leave.
Gilbert: Kingsport will be different, but I think we’ll **settle into** it.
Anne: I hope so. I’m excited, but I’m also nervous.
Gilbert: That’s understandable.
Anne: I’m trying not to **overthink** everything that might happen.
Gilbert: Probably a good idea. We can deal with things as they **come up**.
Anne: That sounds much more manageable.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- settle into — gradually become comfortable in a new place or routine. It took me a few weeks to settle into university life.
- overthink — think about something so much that it becomes more stressful or complicated. I tend to overthink important decisions.
- come up — happen or arise unexpectedly. If any problems come up, call me.
- take in — look at, listen to, or mentally absorb something. We stopped for a moment to take in the view.
