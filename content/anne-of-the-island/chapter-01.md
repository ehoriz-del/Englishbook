# Chapter 1 — The Shadow of Change

## Story Summary

Late in the summer, Anne and Diana rest in the Green Gables orchard after picking apples. The fields around them already show signs of autumn, and both girls are conscious that their lives are changing just as clearly. Miss Lavendar has recently married Stephen Irving, Mr. and Mrs. Allan have left Avonlea, and the manse now stands empty. Diana is engaged to Fred Wright, while Anne and Gilbert are preparing to leave for Redmond College. Diana admits that Avonlea will seem very quiet once Anne is gone.

Anne has another change to think about at home. Mrs. Rachel Lynde is moving into Green Gables the next day. Anne is glad that Marilla will have company, but she still feels unexpectedly sad when the old spare room is cleared for Mrs. Lynde. As a child, Anne had regarded that room almost like a sacred place, and seeing it altered reminds her that even the most familiar parts of Green Gables cannot remain untouched forever. Diana says that she will be terribly lonely after Anne leaves. Anne replies that she will probably be the lonelier one because Diana will remain among old friends while she must begin again among strangers in Kingsport.

The girls talk about growing up. Diana, who keeps touching her engagement ring, admits that adulthood is not as simple or exciting as they once imagined. Anne agrees that they have reached a “parting of the ways.” They are both eighteen now, and the future no longer feels like something distant. Anne jokes that Diana will soon become a respectable married woman while she herself may turn into an old-maid aunt who visits on holidays. Diana insists that Anne will eventually marry someone splendid, but Anne laughs the idea away.

After leaving Diana, Anne goes to the post office and finds a letter from Priscilla Grant. Priscilla’s father has finally agreed to let her attend Redmond, and she plans to board with Anne. The news immediately lifts Anne’s spirits. On the way home, Gilbert catches up with her near the Lake of Shining Waters, and Anne excitedly tells him that she will have Priscilla beside her in Kingsport.

Anne and Gilbert stop on the bridge and look over the pond in the moonlight. The familiar place brings back memories of their school days, and for a while neither wants to break the quiet mood. Gilbert places his hand over Anne’s and seems about to say something serious. Anne quickly pulls her hand away and changes the subject. She has become increasingly aware that Gilbert’s feelings may be moving beyond ordinary friendship, and she does not want that to disturb the easy companionship they have always shared. She talks rapidly all the way back to Green Gables, making it difficult for him to say anything more.

At home, Anne finds Davy crying. At first she assumes he is upset because Dora has fallen down the cellar steps and hurt her nose, but Davy explains that he is actually crying because he missed seeing the accident. His strange logic makes Anne struggle not to laugh. He then asks whether Mrs. Lynde will put him to bed after Anne leaves. Davy says he would rather say his prayers alone than say them in front of someone who still feels like a stranger.

Davy does not understand why Anne must go away when she does not really want to leave. Anne tells him that throughout life people often have to do things they do not want to do. Davy also repeats a piece of neighborhood gossip: Milty Boulter’s mother has said Anne is going to college to “catch a man.” Anne is annoyed at first, then decides not to take the remark seriously. She tells Davy plainly that she is going to Redmond to study, grow, and learn.

Later, after Davy has gone to bed, Anne walks down to Victoria Island and sits alone by the water. The practical worries of college, Gilbert’s changing manner, and the comments of neighbors fade for a while. Surrounded by the familiar night landscape, Anne lets her imagination wander as it always has. She is still deeply attached to Green Gables, but the new stage of her life is already beginning.

## Conversation 1 — Anne and Diana Talk About Change

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Anne: I knew things would change eventually, but I didn’t expect everything to **come at once**.
Diana: Mrs. Lynde moving in, college, people getting married—it is a lot.
Anne: Clearing out the spare room really **brought it home to me**.
Diana: You mean it suddenly felt real?
Anne: Exactly. I’ve been trying to **hold on to** the way Green Gables used to feel.
Diana: You can still love it even if some things change.
Anne: I know. I just need time to **adjust to** the idea.
Diana: And we’ll still be part of each other’s lives.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- come at once — happen in a short period of time, creating pressure or emotional impact. Several major changes seemed to come at once.
- bring something home to someone — make someone fully understand or feel the reality of something. Seeing the empty office brought it home to me that she had really left.
- hold on to — keep something, especially a memory, feeling, or relationship. I wanted to hold on to the traditions we grew up with.
- adjust to — become comfortable with a new situation or condition. It took me a few weeks to adjust to living alone.

## Conversation 2 — Anne and Gilbert at the Bridge

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Gilbert: You got quiet all of a sudden.
Anne: I was just **taking in** the view. It feels different tonight.
Gilbert: Maybe because we’re about to leave.
Anne: That’s probably part of it. Everything familiar seems to **stand out** more.
Gilbert: Are you nervous about Redmond?
Anne: A little, but Priscilla going too has really **put me at ease**.
Gilbert: You’ll settle in quickly.
Anne: I hope so. I’m trying not to **overthink** what comes next.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- take in — absorb or fully notice something. We stood quietly and took in the view.
- stand out — be especially noticeable. Small details began to stand out once I knew I was leaving.
- put someone at ease — make someone feel less nervous or uncomfortable. Her friendly welcome immediately put me at ease.
- overthink — think about something so much that it becomes more complicated or stressful. Try not to overthink the interview.
