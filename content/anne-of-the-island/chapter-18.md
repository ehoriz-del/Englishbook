# Chapter 18 — Miss Josephine Remembers the Anne-girl

## Story Summary

When the Christmas holidays begin, the girls leave Patty’s Place and return to their homes. Aunt Jamesina decides to stay behind in Kingsport because she does not want to leave the three cats alone for several weeks. She says there are no suitable neighbors to feed them and promises to keep Patty’s Place warm until the girls return.

Anne goes back to Green Gables expecting a cheerful holiday, but the weather makes the visit very different from what she imagined. Avonlea is covered by one of the worst early winters anyone can remember. Huge snowdrifts surround Green Gables, and strong storms continue day after day. Even after the roads are cleared, the wind quickly fills them with snow again.

The Avonlea Village Improvement Society tries three times to hold a party for the college students who have come home, but each attempt fails because the weather is too severe. Anne loves Green Gables, but she begins to miss Patty’s Place, the open fire, the girls, Aunt Jamesina, and the usual Friday evenings when friends visit.

She also misses Diana. Diana has bronchitis and cannot leave Orchard Slope. Anne can rarely visit her because the usual route through the Haunted Wood is blocked by snow. The longer road over the frozen Lake of Shining Waters is difficult as well. Ruby Gillis is gone, and Jane Andrews is teaching far away in the west, so Anne’s old circle in Avonlea feels smaller.

Gilbert still comes to Green Gables whenever the weather allows. His visits, however, are not as easy as they once were. Anne often notices him looking at her in a serious way, and she becomes uncomfortable. Marilla seems to understand what is happening and usually leaves the room when Gilbert arrives, taking Davy and Dora with her. Anne is annoyed because this makes the situation even more obvious.

Davy is enjoying the winter. He likes shoveling paths through the snow and eating the special food that Marilla and Mrs. Lynde prepare during the holidays. He is also reading an exciting adventure story from the school library. His comments about the Bible, Mrs. Lynde, and marriage continue to embarrass Anne.

One evening Davy asks when Anne and Gilbert are going to marry because Mrs. Lynde has said it is certain to happen. Anne becomes angry and leaves the room. Sitting alone near the kitchen window, she begins to think about a more serious problem.

Anne is worried about money for her next year at Redmond. The scholarship available to second-year students is small. She refuses to use Marilla’s savings, and she is not sure she can earn enough during the summer. She thinks she may have to leave Redmond for a year, teach school again, and save enough money to finish college later.

Mr. Harrison arrives through the snow carrying mail that has been delayed for several days. Letters from Stella, Priscilla, and Phil cheer Anne. Aunt Jamesina also writes from Patty’s Place. She reports that the cats are well and that she has been letting them sleep indoors because the weather is cold.

Anne leaves a thin typewritten letter until the end because it looks unimportant. When she finally reads it, she becomes very still. Marilla asks what is wrong.

The letter is from a lawyer. Miss Josephine Barry has died after a long illness. Anne is sad because Miss Josephine had always treated her kindly. Then she reads the rest of the letter and discovers that Miss Josephine has remembered her in her will. She has left Anne one thousand dollars.

The amount is enough to solve Anne’s immediate financial problem. Davy is amazed and quickly remembers Miss Josephine as the woman whose spare-room bed Anne and Diana once jumped into by mistake years earlier. Anne does not want to joke about the news. She goes upstairs to her old porch room by herself.

Marilla and Mrs. Lynde continue discussing the inheritance downstairs. Davy, meanwhile, wonders aloud whether having enough money means Anne will never need to marry. Mrs. Lynde tells him sharply to stop talking.

## Conversation 1 — A Difficult Holiday

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Anne: I thought I’d be busy seeing everyone during the holidays.
Marilla: The snow has **kept everyone in**.
Anne: Even the A.V.I.S. party was cancelled three times.
Marilla: You’ll have to **make the best of** the weather.
Anne: I know, but I miss Diana.
Marilla: She’ll **get over** the bronchitis soon.
Anne: I hope so. I also miss Patty’s Place more than I expected.
Marilla: You’ve **grown attached to** it.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- keep someone in — prevent someone from going out. The storm kept everyone in all weekend.
- make the best of — accept a difficult situation and try to enjoy or use it well. We made the best of the rainy day.
- get over — recover from an illness or difficult experience. She got over the flu quickly.
- grow attached to — begin to care strongly about someone or something. I grew attached to the little house.

## Conversation 2 — Unexpected Mail

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Marilla: You suddenly look very serious. What did the letter say?
Anne: Miss Josephine has **passed away**.
Marilla: I’m sorry. She was always kind to you.
Anne: There’s more. She **left me** a thousand dollars.
Marilla: That will help with Redmond.
Anne: Yes. I was afraid I might have to **drop out** next year.
Marilla: Now you can **carry on with** your studies.
Anne: I’m grateful, but I’m sad that the news came this way.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- pass away — die; a softer and more respectful expression. Her elderly neighbor passed away last winter.
- leave someone something — give money or property to someone in a will. His aunt left him a small house.
- drop out — leave a school or course before finishing it. She almost dropped out because of money problems.
- carry on with — continue doing something. He carried on with his studies after the break.
