# Chapter 23 — Paul Cannot Find the Rock People

## Story Summary

Anne enjoys her summer in Avonlea, but she often feels that something is missing. She refuses to admit that the feeling is connected with Gilbert’s absence. Gilbert does not write to her, although Anne knows he sometimes writes to Diana. Anne does not ask Diana about him, and Diana assumes Anne hears from Gilbert herself.

Mrs. Blythe, Gilbert’s mother, makes the situation more uncomfortable by frequently asking Anne in public whether she has heard from Gilbert. Anne usually answers that she has not heard from him lately, which people misunderstand as modest secrecy rather than the truth.

Even so, Anne has many pleasant summer experiences. Priscilla comes to visit in June. Later Mr. and Mrs. Irving return to Prince Edward Island with Paul and Charlotta the Fourth for July and August. Echo Lodge becomes lively again.

Miss Lavendar is happy in her marriage, and Paul clearly loves her. He explains to Anne that he does not call Miss Lavendar simply “mother” because that name belongs to his own mother, who died when he was young. Instead, he calls her Mother Lavendar.

Paul is now thirteen and has grown taller. He is still imaginative, and he and Anne enjoy walking together through fields, woods, and along the shore. Charlotta the Fourth has also grown up and is now a young woman, although her lively personality has not changed much.

Paul spends his first two weeks with his grandmother in Avonlea. As soon as he arrives, he is eager to go to the shore and look for the imaginary Rock People he used to believe lived there. Years earlier he had imagined characters such as Nora, the Golden Lady, and the Twin Sailors.

When Paul returns from the shore, however, he is unusually quiet. Anne asks whether he found the Rock People. Paul says the Golden Lady and the Twin Sailors never appeared. He says Nora seemed to be there, but even she did not feel the same.

Anne tells him that the Rock People have not changed. Paul has changed because he is growing older. His imagination is developing in a different way, and the old imaginary companions no longer feel real to him as they once did.

Paul is sad about this. Anne explains that growing older means leaving some parts of childhood behind. Mrs. Irving overhears them and says they still talk as much nonsense as they always did. Anne jokingly says they are becoming much too wise.

Later in the summer, Anne spends two happy weeks at Echo Lodge. During that visit she helps move along the very slow courtship between Ludovic Speed and Theodora Dix. An older friend of the Irvings, Arnold Sherman, is also visiting, and the household is cheerful.

Near the end of her stay, Anne talks with Miss Lavendar about returning to Kingsport and Redmond. Anne says Patty’s Place now feels like a second home. She is surprised by how quickly the summer has passed.

Then Miss Lavendar asks Anne directly about Gilbert. She wants to know whether Anne and Gilbert are still as close as they used to be.

Anne says she is still Gilbert’s friend. Miss Lavendar is not satisfied with the answer and asks whether they have quarreled. Anne explains that there was no quarrel. Gilbert wanted more than friendship, and Anne told him she could not give him that.

Miss Lavendar asks whether Anne is absolutely sure. Anne says she is. Miss Lavendar replies that she is sorry and says that Anne and Gilbert are well suited to each other.

Anne becomes impatient and asks why everyone seems to believe she should marry Gilbert. Miss Lavendar simply says that they were made for each other.

Anne does not change her answer. She still believes that she does not love Gilbert in the way he wants, but the conversation brings the subject back into her mind just before she returns to Redmond.

Anne also notices that Paul is no longer exactly the small boy she once taught. He still loves stories and imagination, but he is beginning to understand the difference between the worlds he invented as a child and the real places around him. Their walks remain close and happy even though some of his old fantasies no longer return.

## Conversation 1 — Paul and the Rock People

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Paul: I went back to the shore, but the Rock People were gone.
Anne: Maybe you’ve **grown out of** seeing them the way you used to.
Paul: I didn’t want that to happen.
Anne: I know. Some things **fade away** as we get older.
Paul: Does that mean my imagination is disappearing?
Anne: Not at all. It may just **change into** something different.
Paul: I suppose I’ll have to **get used to** that.
Anne: We all do.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- grow out of — stop doing or enjoying something as you become older. He grew out of playing with toy cars.
- fade away — slowly disappear. The sound gradually faded away.
- change into — become something different. The old habit changed into a new routine.
- get used to — become comfortable with something new. It takes time to get used to change.

## Conversation 2 — Miss Lavendar Asks About Gilbert

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Miss Lavendar: Have you and Gilbert **fallen out**?
Anne: No. We didn’t argue.
Miss Lavendar: Then what happened?
Anne: He wanted our friendship to **turn into** something more.
Miss Lavendar: And you said no?
Anne: Yes. I’m sure of my answer.
Miss Lavendar: I still think you two **belong together**.
Anne: Everyone keeps saying that.
Miss Lavendar: Sometimes other people notice things before we do.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- fall out — have an argument and stop being friendly. They fell out over a small disagreement.
- turn into — change and become something else. Their friendship turned into a business partnership.
- belong together — be well suited to each other. Everyone thought the couple belonged together.
- keep saying — repeat the same idea many times. People keep saying I should change jobs.
