# Chapter 10 — Patty’s Place

## Story Summary

The next evening Anne and Priscilla return to Patty’s Place to ask about renting it. They walk through the little garden and ring the bell. An elderly servant lets them into a large living room, where two women are sitting beside a cheerful fire.

The older woman is Miss Patty Spofford, about seventy, and the younger is her niece, Miss Maria, about fifty. Both wear caps and gray shawls, both have large light-blue eyes behind steel-rimmed spectacles, and both are steadily knitting. Behind each woman sits a large white china dog decorated with green spots, a green nose, and green ears. Anne is immediately fascinated by the dogs.

At first the room is very quiet. Anne and Priscilla are nervous, and Miss Patty and Miss Maria simply look at them. Anne notices the old-fashioned braided rugs, the grandfather clock, the cupboards filled with china, the prints and silhouettes on the walls, and a window seat on the turn of the stairs. Another door opens directly toward the pine grove.

Finally Anne explains that they saw the sign saying the house was available to rent. Miss Patty says she had actually intended to remove the sign that day. Anne assumes someone else has already taken the house, but Miss Patty says that she and Maria have decided not to rent it at all.

Anne cannot hide her disappointment. She tells Miss Patty that she has loved the house since first seeing it the previous autumn and had hoped she and her college friends could live there. Miss Patty becomes interested when she hears Anne say that she genuinely loves the place. She explains that several other people have wanted to rent Patty’s Place, but she did not trust any of them to care for it properly.

Miss Patty suddenly tells Anne that if she truly loves the house, she may have it. She believes Anne and her friends will treat it well. The only remaining question is whether the students can afford the rent.

Miss Patty names her price, and Priscilla immediately realizes it is too high for them. Anne explains that they are college students with limited money and tells Miss Patty what they had expected to pay. To their surprise, Miss Patty accepts the lower amount.

She explains that the rent is not essential to finance her trip. She and Maria already have enough money to travel to Europe. Miss Maria wants to go, and Miss Patty says that a young person like Maria obviously cannot travel around the world alone. Although Miss Patty is seventy, she says she is not tired of living and expects that she may enjoy the trip as well. They plan to leave in June and may remain away for two or three years.

Most of the furniture will stay in the house. Anne timidly asks whether the two china dogs will remain too. Miss Patty is pleased by the question. She explains that the dogs are more than a hundred years old and were brought from London by her brother Aaron fifty years earlier. Their names are Gog and Magog. Gog looks to the right, while Magog looks to the left.

Miss Patty has one further condition: the name “Patty’s Place” must remain on the gate. Previous potential tenants wanted to remove it, which offended her. Anne and Priscilla assure her that they consider the name one of the best things about the house.

Miss Patty and Miss Maria then show the girls around. Downstairs are the living room, a kitchen, and a small bedroom. Upstairs are three rooms, one large and two small. Anne especially likes a small pale-blue room facing the pines, with an old-fashioned dressing table and a diamond-paned window with a seat beneath it.

That evening Philippa visits 38 St. John Street, tired from packing for the end of term. She notices at once that Anne and Priscilla are unusually excited. They tell her they have rented Patty’s Place and that Stella Maynard and Aunt Jamesina will be joining them.

Phil immediately begs to live with them too. Anne warns her that their household will be simple because Anne, Priscilla, and Stella have little money. Phil says she does not care. Priscilla adds that everyone will have chores and that visitors will be allowed only on Friday evenings. Phil accepts all the conditions. Anne and Priscilla tell her they must consult Stella first, but as far as they are concerned, Phil is welcome to join them.

## Conversation 1 — Asking About the House

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Miss Patty: I was actually about to take the sign down.
Priscilla: Has someone else **taken the place**?
Miss Patty: No. We had decided not to rent it.
Anne: That’s disappointing. We really **fell in love with** it.
Miss Patty: Do you mean that seriously?
Anne: Yes. We would **look after** it very carefully.
Miss Patty: In that case, I may reconsider.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- find out about — obtain information about something. We called the owner to find out about the apartment.
- take a place — rent or agree to occupy a house or apartment. Another student had already taken the place.
- fall in love with — become strongly attracted to a person, place, or thing. She fell in love with the neighborhood immediately.
- look after — take care of someone or something. They promised to look after the house while the owners were away.

Anne: We’ve rented Patty’s Place for next year.
Phil: Then I want to **move in with** you.
Priscilla: You know we’ll all have to **pitch in** with the work.
Phil: That’s fine. I can learn.
Anne: We’ll also have to **stick to** a fairly simple budget.
Phil: I can do that too.
Priscilla: We need to **check with** Stella, but I think she’ll agree.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- move in with — begin living in the same home as another person or group. She moved in with two friends near campus.
- pitch in — help with work that needs to be done. Everyone pitched in to clean the apartment.
- stick to — continue following a plan, rule, budget, or decision. We need to stick to our budget this month.
- check with — ask someone for approval, confirmation, or information. I need to check with my roommate before inviting guests.
