# Chapter 22 — Spring and Anne Return to Green Gables

## Story Summary

On a cool spring evening, Marilla sits alone by the fire at Green Gables and thinks about the past. She is nearly sixty and has begun to spend more time remembering her childhood, youth, and the long quiet years before Anne came into her life.

Marilla realizes how much Green Gables changed after Anne arrived nine years earlier. Anne brought energy, imagination, noise, affection, and many unexpected problems, but she also made Marilla’s life warmer and happier. Marilla thinks that the years since Anne came have been the fullest years of her life.

Anne is supposed to return home the following evening. Marilla expects to see Mrs. Lynde when the kitchen door opens, but instead Anne appears unexpectedly, carrying Mayflowers and violets.

Marilla is so surprised and happy that she forgets her usual reserve. She hugs Anne tightly and kisses her. Anne explains that she suddenly became homesick and decided to come home a day early. She walked from Carmody while the mailman agreed to bring her trunk the next day.

Anne tells Marilla about the spring evening she has just walked through. She passed the barrens, picked Mayflowers, and came through Violet Vale. She talks about the frogs singing in the marsh and says their sound always reminds her of the night she first arrived at Green Gables.

Although Anne has enjoyed Redmond and her visit to Bolingbroke, she says that Green Gables is still home.

Marilla then mentions that Gilbert will not be coming home that summer. Anne quickly changes the subject and begins arranging the violets she brought with her. Marilla notices the change in Anne’s voice but does not press her immediately.

When Marilla asks how Gilbert did in his examinations, Anne says he did extremely well and led his class. She then asks where Mrs. Lynde and the twins are. Mrs. Lynde and Dora are visiting Mr. Harrison, while Davy is at the Boulters’.

Davy soon bursts into the kitchen and is delighted to see Anne. He announces that he has grown two inches since the previous fall. He also proudly shows her that one of his front teeth is missing. Mrs. Lynde removed it by tying a string to the tooth and to a door, then shutting the door. Davy sold the tooth to Milty Boulter for two cents because Milty is collecting teeth to make a necklace for playing Indian Chief.

Marilla asks whether Davy behaved well at the Boulters’. He says yes but admits that he is tired of being good. Anne reminds him that being bad has consequences, even if being bad seems fun at first.

Davy then tells Anne about an echo behind the Boulter barn and asks what an echo is. Anne gives him an imaginative answer, describing Echo as a beautiful invisible nymph who laughs from the hills. Davy immediately asks whether this is true or whether Anne is telling a “whopper.” Anne becomes frustrated because he does not understand the difference between a fairy tale and a lie.

The conversation becomes even more surprising when Davy announces that he is going to be married someday. The girl is Stella Fletcher, a classmate whom he considers the prettiest girl he has ever seen. He even asks Anne to look after Stella if he dies before he is old enough to marry her.

Marilla tells Davy to stop talking nonsense and calls everyone to supper.

Anne’s first evening home is filled with familiar voices, questions, and family routines. Yet one important part of the old pattern is missing. Gilbert will not be in Avonlea during the summer, and Anne avoids discussing him whenever Marilla brings up his name.

Davy has many more small pieces of news for Anne. He talks about his friends, school, and the things he and Milty have been doing. His questions move quickly from one subject to another, and Anne finds herself answering him in the same mixture of patience and imagination she used when he was younger. The familiar conversation makes her return feel immediate, as if college life had suddenly moved far away.

## Conversation 1 — Coming Home Early

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Marilla: I wasn’t expecting you until tomorrow.
Anne: I know. I got homesick and decided to **come back early**.
Marilla: You walked all the way from Carmody?
Anne: Yes. I wanted to **take my time** and enjoy the spring evening.
Marilla: You must be tired.
Anne: A little, but the walk **brought back** so many memories.
Marilla: Well, I’m glad you’re home.
Anne: So am I. I’ve missed this place.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- come back early — return sooner than expected. She came back early because she missed home.
- take your time — do something without rushing. We took our time walking home.
- bring something back — cause a memory or feeling to return. That song brought back old memories.
- miss a place — feel sad because you are away from somewhere important to you. I really missed home while I was away.

## Conversation 2 — Davy’s Questions

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Davy: Anne, do you know I’m going to get married someday?
Anne: You’ve really **thought this through**, haven’t you?
Davy: Of course. Her name is Stella.
Anne: And does Stella know about this plan?
Davy: Not exactly.
Anne: Then maybe you shouldn’t **get ahead of yourself**.
Davy: I’m just planning ahead.
Anne: You certainly never **run out of** ideas.
Davy: I’ve got lots more.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- think something through — consider all parts of a plan carefully. You should think the decision through first.
- get ahead of yourself — act or plan too far in advance before basic things are settled. Don’t get ahead of yourself.
- run out of — use all of something so that none is left. Davy never runs out of questions.
- plan ahead — prepare for something before it happens. It helps to plan ahead.
