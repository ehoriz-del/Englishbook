# Chapter 41 — Love Takes Up the Glass of Time

## Story Summary

By September, Gilbert has recovered from typhoid fever and is visiting Green Gables again.

One afternoon he comes around the corner of the porch and asks Anne to take one of their old walks with him. He suggests Hester Gray’s garden, a place connected with many memories from Anne’s younger years.

Anne wants to go, but she has to attend Alice Penhallow’s wedding that evening and needs time to finish preparing her green dress. Gilbert asks whether she can go the next afternoon instead. Anne says yes.

They joke briefly about the number of weddings Anne has attended that summer. Jane Andrews, Philippa, and now Alice have all married.

After Gilbert leaves, Anne feels uncertain. Since his recovery, he has visited Green Gables often, and some of their old friendship has returned.

But friendship is no longer enough for Anne.

Now that she knows she loves Gilbert, every friendly conversation makes her wonder whether he still feels the same way. She worries that she may have destroyed her chance when she rejected him two years earlier.

The old rumors about Christine Stuart also continue to trouble her. Anne thinks it is possible that Gilbert really loves Christine or is even engaged to her.

The next afternoon Gilbert returns. Anne wears an old green dress he once told her he liked. As they walk through the woods, Anne notices that Gilbert looks older after his illness. He seems to have left part of his boyhood behind.

They reach Hester Gray’s garden and sit on the old bench. The garden is different from the first time Anne and her friends found it, but it is still beautiful. Goldenrod and asters grow around them, the brook can be heard below, and the sea is in the distance.

Anne says that the blue haze beyond the valley looks like the land where dreams come true.

Gilbert asks whether she still has any unfulfilled dreams.

His tone immediately makes Anne’s heart beat faster. She tries to change the subject by talking about the flowers and the autumn air, but Gilbert does not allow the conversation to move away.

He tells Anne that he has one dream he has continued to hold onto even when he believed it could never happen.

His dream is a home with a fire, a cat and dog, friends coming and going—and Anne.

Then Gilbert reminds her that he asked her an important question more than two years earlier. He asks whether, if he asks again now, her answer will be different.

Anne cannot speak. Instead, she looks at him, and Gilbert understands her answer.

They remain in Hester Gray’s garden until evening, talking about everything that has happened between them.

Anne admits that she believed Gilbert was in love with Christine Stuart.

Gilbert explains that Christine was already engaged to a man in her hometown. Christine’s brother had asked Gilbert to look after her when she came to Kingsport to study music because she did not know many people there.

Gilbert became Christine’s friend but never loved her. He knew people at Redmond were spreading romantic rumors about them, but after Anne rejected him, he did not care enough to correct the gossip.

Gilbert tells Anne that there has never been another woman for him. He has loved Anne since their school days, even since the time she broke her slate over his head after he called her “Carrots.”

He tried to stop loving her after Roy Gardner appeared because he believed Anne would marry Roy. For two years he repeatedly heard that Anne and Roy were about to announce their engagement.

During Gilbert’s recovery from typhoid, however, he received a letter from Phil—now Phil Blake. Phil told him that Anne and Roy were not going to marry and advised him to try again.

Anne tells Gilbert about the terrible night when she believed he was dying. That night she finally understood that she loved him.

Gilbert warns Anne that they will have to wait. He still has three years of medical training before he can begin his career. He cannot offer wealth, expensive jewelry, or a grand house.

Anne tells him she does not want those things. She wants Gilbert.

They agree that they can spend the coming years working, waiting, and making plans together.

At last, after years of friendship, misunderstandings, missed chances, and changing expectations, Anne and Gilbert are engaged. They leave Hester Gray’s garden together in the evening, knowing that the future they once imagined separately will now be shared.

## Conversation 1 — The Walk to Hester Gray’s Garden

Gilbert: Can you go for a walk tomorrow?
Anne: Yes. I’d love to.
Gilbert: Let’s go back to Hester Gray’s garden.
Anne: We haven’t been there in a long time.
Gilbert: I’ve been thinking about how much has **changed between us**.
Anne: So have I.
Gilbert: There’s something I want to **bring up** when we get there.
Anne: All right. I’ll listen.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- change between people — become different in a relationship. A lot has changed between us.
- bring something up — introduce an important topic. I need to bring something up.
- go back to — return to a place or subject. We went back to our old neighborhood.
- think about — consider something carefully. I’ve been thinking about the future.

## Conversation 2 — Gilbert Asks Again

Gilbert: I asked you once before, and you said no.
Anne: I know.
Gilbert: If I **ask you again**, will your answer be different?
Anne: Yes.
Gilbert: You really mean that?
Anne: I do. I finally **figured out** what I should have understood long ago.
Gilbert: I was afraid I had **missed my chance**.
Anne: So was I.
Gilbert: Then we can start planning our future together.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- ask again — repeat a question or request. He decided to ask again later.
- figure something out — finally understand something. She figured out what she truly wanted.
- miss your chance — lose an opportunity because you did not act in time. I thought I had missed my chance.
- plan a future together — make long-term plans as a couple. They began planning a future together.
