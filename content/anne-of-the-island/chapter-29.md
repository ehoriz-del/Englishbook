# Chapter 29 — Diana’s Wedding

## Story Summary

Diana’s wedding day arrives at Orchard Slope. Anne is upstairs with Diana in the west-facing bedroom, helping her get ready. Diana is dressed in white, with her dark curls under a wedding veil. Anne arranges the veil just as she once promised she would when they were children.

Anne also ties white ribbon around Diana’s bouquet of pink roses. The moment reminds both of them of the dreams they shared years earlier. When they were younger, Anne imagined that Diana’s marriage would break her heart. Now she can smile at those old ideas, although she still feels emotional about the change in their lives.

Diana says they are not really parting. She will still live nearby, and their friendship will continue. Anne agrees that they have kept their old promise of friendship faithfully. They have never had a serious quarrel or allowed coldness to come between them.

Still, Anne knows Diana’s life will be different after marriage. Diana will have a husband, a home, and new responsibilities. Anne jokes that Mrs. Rachel has promised to give her a quilt too when her own wedding day comes.

Diana suddenly becomes very nervous when she hears the minister arriving. She says she cannot go through with the ceremony and feels certain she will faint. Anne responds with practical humor and tells Diana that many people survive getting married.

Diana’s father soon comes upstairs. Before leaving the room, Anne asks Diana to kiss her goodbye as Diana Barry for the last time. Diana reminds her that Diana Wright will still be able to kiss her.

Anne then meets Gilbert at the top of the stairs. He has arrived from Kingsport that day and is Fred Wright’s best man. This is the first time Anne and Gilbert have seen each other since leaving college for the summer.

Gilbert greets Anne politely. Anne immediately notices that he looks thinner than before. Gilbert, in turn, is strongly affected by seeing Anne in her soft white bridesmaid’s dress with lilies-of-the-valley in her hair.

Following the custom of the wedding, Anne goes down to the parlor on Gilbert’s arm. The guests notice how good they look together. Mrs. Rachel quietly comments on it to Marilla.

Fred enters looking very nervous, and Diana comes in with her father. Diana does not faint, and the ceremony proceeds without any problem.

Afterward there is a large meal and celebration. Later, Fred and Diana leave by moonlight for their new home.

Gilbert walks Anne back toward Green Gables. During the informal happiness of the wedding, some of their old easy friendship has returned. Anne enjoys walking the familiar road with him again.

When they reach the bridge over the Lake of Shining Waters, Gilbert suggests taking a walk through Lovers’ Lane before Anne goes inside. Anne agrees.

The moonlit lane is beautiful and full of familiar memories. Years earlier, walking there alone with Gilbert would have felt emotionally dangerous to Anne. Now she tells herself it is safe because Roy Gardner is part of her life and she believes Gilbert is interested in Christine Stuart.

Anne has met Christine several times in Kingsport. Christine has always been pleasant to her, and Anne has been equally polite, but Anne does not feel a real friendship with her.

Gilbert asks whether Anne will stay in Avonlea all summer. Anne explains that she is going to Valley Road the next week. Esther Haythorne is not well, so Anne has agreed to substitute for her during the summer school term.

Anne tells Gilbert that Avonlea has started to feel different. Many of the children she once taught are now almost grown, and she sometimes feels like a stranger in the place where she grew up.

The wedding, the walk with Gilbert, and Diana’s departure all make Anne aware of how much their lives are changing. She is glad Diana is happy, but Avonlea already feels quieter to her without the old daily companionship she and Diana once shared.

## Conversation 1 — Before the Ceremony

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Diana: I don’t think I can do this.
Anne: You’re just nervous.
Diana: What if I faint?
Anne: You’ll be fine. Just **take a deep breath**.
Diana: Everything suddenly feels real.
Anne: I know. But you don’t have to **back out** because you’re scared.
Diana: I’m not going to back out.
Anne: Good. Now let’s **get you downstairs**.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- take a deep breath — breathe in slowly and deeply to calm yourself. Take a deep breath before you speak.
- back out — decide not to do something you had planned or agreed to do. She almost backed out of the trip.
- get someone somewhere — help or cause someone to reach a place. Let’s get everyone downstairs.
- feel real — begin to seem emotionally true or immediate. The move did not feel real until the last day.

## Conversation 2 — Walking Home with Gilbert

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Gilbert: Are you staying in Avonlea all summer?
Anne: No. I’m **filling in for** a teacher at Valley Road.
Gilbert: When do you leave?
Anne: Next week.
Gilbert: Avonlea must feel different now.
Anne: It does. Everyone seems to be **moving on**.
Gilbert: That happens.
Anne: I know. I’m just trying to **get used to** it.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- fill in for — temporarily do someone else’s job. I’m filling in for my manager this week.
- move on — continue to the next stage of life or a situation. Many of our old friends have moved on.
- get used to — become comfortable with something new. I’m still getting used to the new schedule.
- stay in — remain in a place. Are you staying in town this summer?
