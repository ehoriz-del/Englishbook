# Chapter 11 — The Round of Life

## Story Summary

Anne returns to Avonlea after her first year at Redmond with the Thorburn Scholarship. At first, Green Gables and the village seem almost unchanged. On her first Sunday back, however, Anne begins to notice how much has happened during the months she was away. There is a new minister in the pulpit, and several familiar people are missing from the church because they have died. She is reminded that Avonlea has continued to change even while she was at college.

One surprise is Billy Andrews. He has married Nettie Blewett and appears in church looking very pleased with himself. Anne remembers that only a few months earlier Jane Andrews had asked her to marry Billy on his behalf. Billy clearly did not remain unhappy for long after Anne refused him. Jane has also made a change in her plans. She has given up teaching in Avonlea and intends to go west in the fall.

The most disturbing change is Ruby Gillis. Anne sees Ruby sitting in the choir and is shocked by her appearance. Ruby is still beautiful, but she is much thinner than before. Her cheeks are brightly colored, her eyes look unusually bright, and her hands are very delicate. After church, Anne asks Mrs. Rachel Lynde whether Ruby is ill. Mrs. Lynde tells her directly that Ruby has consumption and that the illness is serious. Ruby and her family refuse to speak openly about it, and Ruby still talks as if she will return to teaching in the fall.

Anne is deeply upset. She and Ruby have not been especially close in recent years, but they were school friends for a long time. Ruby speaks cheerfully to Anne after church and invites her to visit the next evening. Diana agrees to go with Anne because she has also wanted to see Ruby but has found the situation difficult.

On the way to the Gillis home, Anne and Diana stop at Elisha Wright’s house to deliver a small dish of rhubarb jelly to Aunt Atossa. Aunt Atossa is an elderly relative who has recently come to live with the Wright family after her husband’s death. She is known for saying unpleasant things to people, and Anne quickly understands why.

Aunt Atossa complains about the jelly, criticizes Diana’s mother, comments on Diana’s engagement, makes remarks about Anne’s freckles and red hair, and complains that Marilla has not visited her. Anne and Diana remain polite but are glad to leave. Afterward, Diana tells Anne a funny story about Aunt Atossa at a prayer meeting. Aunt Atossa once stood up and criticized nearly everyone in the church, but the minister was very deaf and did not hear her speech. When she finished, he calmly said that he hoped God would answer their sister’s prayer.

As they continue toward Ruby’s house, Anne tells Diana that she has been thinking about writing a short story for publication. She is nervous about trying because she would be embarrassed if the story were rejected. Diana immediately encourages her. Anne says she has already chosen the name Averil Lester for the heroine but has not yet found the right plot.

At the Gillis home, the rooms are bright and full of visitors. Ruby is dressed in white and talks rapidly about dresses, parties, young men, and her plans to teach at White Sands. Two young men downstairs are competing for her attention, while Ruby says Herb Spencer is the one she really likes.

Later Ruby takes Anne upstairs to show her new clothes. For a moment, her cheerful manner changes. She asks Anne to visit often and to come alone. When Anne asks if she is feeling well, Ruby insists that she is perfectly healthy and says her winter illness only made her a little weak. Her voice becomes defensive, and she quickly returns downstairs to her guests.

Anne and Diana leave soon afterward. Anne has now seen both the lively way Ruby behaves in front of others and the brief moment when Ruby quietly asked her to come back alone.

## Conversation 1 — Back in Avonlea

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Diana: Does Avonlea feel the same after a year away?
Anne: At first it did, but then I started to notice what had **changed around** here.
Diana: Some things happened very quickly.
Anne: Billy getting married really **caught me off guard**.
Diana: I thought you might laugh when you saw him.
Anne: I almost did, but seeing Ruby made everything else **fade into the background**.
Diana: Her illness is hard to think about.
Anne: I know. I still haven’t fully **taken it in**.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- change around — become different in several small ways. A lot has changed around the neighborhood since I left.
- catch someone off guard — surprise someone when they are not expecting something. The announcement caught me off guard.
- fade into the background — become less important or less noticeable. My small worries faded into the background after I heard the news.
- take something in — fully understand or absorb information. I needed time to take in what she had told me.

## Conversation 2 — Anne Talks About Writing

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Anne: I’ve been thinking about writing a short story.
Diana: You should **go for it**.
Anne: I’m worried an editor might reject it.
Diana: That could happen, but you can still **give it a try**.
Anne: I’ve chosen the heroine’s name, but I haven’t **come up with** a plot yet.
Diana: You always have ideas.
Anne: I hope one of them will **work out**.
Diana: Start writing and see where it goes.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- go for it — decide to try something you want to do. If you really want the job, go for it.
- give something a try — attempt something to see whether you can do it. I decided to give painting a try.
- come up with — think of an idea, answer, or plan. We need to come up with a better title.
- work out — develop successfully or have a good result. I hope the plan works out.
