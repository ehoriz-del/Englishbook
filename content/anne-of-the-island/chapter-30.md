# Chapter 30 — Mrs. Skinner’s Romance

## Story Summary

Anne leaves Avonlea to teach the summer term at Valley Road. Esther Haythorne, the regular teacher, is not well, so Anne has agreed to substitute for her through July and August.

When Anne gets off the train at Valley Road station, she looks around for the person who is supposed to meet her. She will be staying with Miss Janet Sweet, but no one at the station looks like the woman Anne has imagined from Esther’s description.

The only person waiting nearby is a large, cheerful older woman sitting in a small wagon filled with mailbags. The woman calls to Anne and asks whether she is the new Valley Road schoolteacher.

Anne says yes. The woman introduces herself as Mrs. Amelia Skinner and explains that Janet Sweet asked her to bring Anne to the house. Mrs. Skinner rearranges the mailbags to make room, and Anne climbs into the crowded wagon.

Mrs. Skinner is delivering the mail that day because her husband, Thomas, wants to work in his turnip field. She repeatedly urges the black mare to keep moving while she talks almost without stopping.

Soon Mrs. Skinner tells Anne that she and Thomas have been married for only a month. Thomas had courted her for a long time, but he was not the only man who wanted to marry her.

Mrs. Skinner had been a widow for many years. After her daughter moved west to teach school, she became lonely and began to think that marrying again might not be a bad idea.

Two men started visiting her. One was Thomas Skinner. The other was William Obadiah Seaman, whom she usually calls W.O.

W.O. was wealthy. He owned a fine home and lived comfortably. From a practical point of view, he looked like the better choice.

Thomas, on the other hand, was poor. His house was small and not in especially good condition. Mrs. Skinner jokes that if the roof did not leak in dry weather, that was about the best anyone could say for it.

Anne naturally asks why Mrs. Skinner did not choose the richer man.

Mrs. Skinner answers very seriously that W.O. did not love her. His sister had been keeping house for him, but after she married, W.O. needed another woman to manage his home. Mrs. Skinner believes that was the main reason he wanted a wife.

Thomas was different. He loved Mrs. Skinner, and she loved him.

Mrs. Skinner admits that she still struggled with the decision. W.O.’s house and money were tempting. She finally had a serious conversation with herself and decided that a comfortable house would not make her happy if there was no love in it.

She chose Thomas. While preparing for the wedding, she even avoided driving past W.O.’s fine property because she was afraid the sight of the house might make her doubt her decision again.

Now that she is married to Thomas, she says she does not regret the choice. They are comfortable and happy together even though they are not rich.

Anne asks how W.O. reacted. Mrs. Skinner says he was upset for a while but has already started visiting another woman in Millersville.

She then tells Anne about W.O.’s first wife, whom he had not really wanted to marry, and about a cousin named Jane Ann who married a wealthy man she did not love. Jane Ann now envies Mrs. Skinner because she would rather live in a small house with a man she cared about than in a large house without affection.

As the wagon continues down the country road, Mrs. Skinner’s story keeps Anne entertained. Eventually they reach Janet Sweet’s home, called Wayside.

Mrs. Skinner points out a shortcut through the marsh to the school but warns Anne to be careful because the mud is dangerous. Anne sincerely tells Mrs. Skinner that she has enjoyed the ride and the conversation.

Mrs. Skinner is delighted by the compliment. Then she drives away, still talking to the black mare, while Anne begins her summer at Valley Road.

## Conversation 1 — Meeting Mrs. Skinner

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Mrs. Skinner: Are you the new teacher?
Anne: Yes. I was wondering who would **pick me up**.
Mrs. Skinner: Janet asked me to.
Anne: Thank you.
Mrs. Skinner: We’ll have to **make room for** you between these mailbags.
Anne: I can squeeze in.
Mrs. Skinner: Good. We’ll **head over to** Janet’s place now.
Anne: This is already more interesting than I expected.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- pick someone up — collect someone and take them somewhere. I’ll pick you up at the station.
- make room for — create space for someone or something. We moved the bags to make room for her.
- head over to — go to a place. Let’s head over to the house.
- squeeze in — fit into a small or crowded space. We managed to squeeze in one more chair.

## Conversation 2 — Choosing Thomas

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Anne: So W.O. had more money, but you chose Thomas?
Mrs. Skinner: That’s right.
Anne: Was it hard to **make up your mind**?
Mrs. Skinner: Very hard at first.
Anne: What finally helped you decide?
Mrs. Skinner: I realized money couldn’t **make up for** a lack of love.
Anne: And you’re happy with Thomas?
Mrs. Skinner: Very. I’m glad I **went with** the person I truly cared about.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- make up your mind — reach a decision. It took her weeks to make up her mind.
- make up for — compensate for something that is missing or disappointing. Money cannot always make up for lost time.
- go with — choose one option. In the end, I went with the simpler plan.
- care about — feel affection or concern for someone or something. She cares deeply about her family.
