# Chapter 16 — Adjusted Relationships

## Story Summary

Three weeks after the girls move into Patty’s Place, the new household already feels comfortable. Anne, Priscilla, Stella, Philippa, and Aunt Jamesina have settled into the old house with the three cats, Rusty, Joseph, and the Sarah-cat. Gog and Magog, the two china dogs that belong to the house, still stand in their usual places. In the evenings the girls gather in the large living room, where the fire, books, flowers, and old furniture make the house feel warm and welcoming.

Anne had been glad to return to Redmond. Her last days in Avonlea had been uncomfortable because the baking-powder version of “Averil’s Atonement” had appeared in local newspapers and advertising pamphlets. Most people in Avonlea thought winning the prize was impressive, but Anne still felt embarrassed by the way her story had been used. Returning to college gave her a chance to leave the incident behind.

The second year at Redmond begins with familiar faces. Gilbert, Priscilla, Stella, Charlie Sloane, Philippa, and Moody Spurgeon MacPherson are all there. Moody has finally come to college after teaching school. Unfortunately, some older students play a joke on him and shave half of his head, forcing him to appear in public that way until his hair grows back.

At Patty’s Place, the girls spend their first weeks arranging the rooms, dividing the work, and learning to live together. Philippa, who comes from a wealthy family and has never done much housework, has to learn simple tasks. She discovers that washing dishes, shopping, and helping with the daily routine are new experiences for her.

One day Anne notices a very unattractive rusty-brown cat following her on the street. She tries to send him away, but he follows her all the way to Patty’s Place. Anne shuts the gate in his face, thinking she has finally escaped him. A short time later, however, Phil opens the door and finds the cat sitting on the step. He immediately runs inside and jumps onto Anne’s lap.

The girls feed the hungry cat but do not intend to keep him. The next morning he is still there. For several days they continue to put him outside, and each time he returns. He ignores everyone except Anne and clearly decides that she belongs to him. As he receives regular meals, he begins to look healthier.

The girls become worried because Aunt Jamesina is planning to arrive with her own cat, the Sarah-cat. Stella says they cannot keep two cats, especially because the rusty cat is clearly a fighter. Phil suggests that the most humane solution is to chloroform him. She says she has done it before and knows the method.

Anne dislikes the idea but agrees because she believes they have no other choice. The cat is given a good breakfast, then placed under a box with chloroform. Anne cannot watch and leaves Phil to handle it. Later the girls prepare to bury him in the orchard.

When Phil lifts the box, however, they hear a small meow. The cat is still alive. By the next morning he is completely awake and jumps onto Anne’s shoulder. Phil discovers that there is a knothole in the box, which allowed fresh air to enter.

Phil says they will have to try again, but Anne immediately refuses. She announces that the cat is now hers and names him Rusty. From then on, he becomes a permanent member of the household.

When Aunt Jamesina finally arrives, she brings not only the Sarah-cat but another cat named Joseph, which belonged to a friend who moved to Vancouver. At first the cats do not get along. Rusty fights with Joseph and acts as if Patty’s Place belongs to him. Aunt Jamesina calmly says they should be allowed to work things out.

Her prediction is correct. After some time, Rusty and Joseph stop fighting and eventually become close companions. The members of the household also adjust to one another. Phil learns more household tasks, Aunt Jamesina becomes an important part of the home, and Patty’s Place settles into its new routine.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- settle in — become comfortable in a new place or routine. It took us a few weeks to settle in.
- get used to — become familiar or comfortable with something. She got used to the new schedule quickly.
- pitch in — help with work that needs to be done. Everyone pitched in after dinner.
- figure something out — understand or solve something. We’re still figuring out how to divide the chores.

Phil: I think he has **picked you out** as his person.

Anne: I know, but he looks much better now.
Phil: We need to **deal with** the situation somehow.

Stella: Then Rusty is officially part of the household.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- come right back — return immediately or very soon. I sent the dog outside, but he came right back.
- pick someone out — choose one person from a group. The puppy picked me out right away.
- keep someone or something around — continue to have someone or something nearby. We decided to keep the old table around.
- deal with — handle a problem or situation. We need to deal with this before tomorrow.
