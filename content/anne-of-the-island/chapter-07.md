# Chapter 7 — Home Again

## Story Summary

After the first few weeks at Redmond, the rest of the term passes quickly. By December, Anne and the other students are busy with Christmas examinations. Anne, Gilbert, and Philippa compete closely for the highest marks in the freshman classes, Priscilla does well, and Charlie Sloane manages to pass respectably.

On the night before the students leave for the holidays, Philippa tries once more to persuade Anne to spend Christmas with her in Bolingbroke. Phil promises dances, drives, and a lively social season, but Anne refuses. She wants to return to Green Gables. When Phil calls Avonlea dull, Anne describes the old farmhouse, the orchard, the brook, the fir woods, Marilla, Mrs. Lynde, Dora, Davy, her room over the porch, and the feather bed waiting for her. Phil stops joking and hugs her.

The following evening Diana meets Anne at Carmody station and drives her home. Green Gables is brightly lit, and Davy has built a bonfire in the yard to celebrate Anne’s return. He has also learned a loud war-whoop from Mr. Harrison’s hired boy and has been practicing it until Mrs. Lynde’s nerves are nearly exhausted. As soon as Anne arrives, Davy rushes to her and hugs her knees, while Dora holds her hand.

Marilla appears in the kitchen doorway with Mrs. Lynde behind her. Marilla is so happy that she is afraid she may cry, although she tries not to show it. Everyone sits down to a large supper, and Diana stays overnight. Before Anne and Diana go upstairs to talk, Davy insists that Anne put him to bed because he has missed saying his prayers with her.

Davy then admits that he has not prayed for several nights. He explains that he heard Mr. Harrison’s hired boy use a bad word and has wanted to say it ever since. The word keeps entering his thoughts even when he tries to pray. Anne tells him to say the word aloud. Davy is shocked but finally whispers it. Once he hears himself say it, he decides he never wants to use it again. Anne tells him it would be better not to spend so much time with people who fill his mind with language he does not want there. Davy then says his prayers.

Anne and Diana stay up late talking and sharing the news of the months they have spent apart. By morning snow has begun to fall, and Avonlea has a white Christmas. Letters and gifts arrive from Miss Lavendar and Paul Irving. Miss Lavendar is happily settled in her new home, while Charlotta the Fourth is homesick for Prince Edward Island. Miss Lavendar asks Anne to visit Echo Lodge during the holidays, light a fire, air the rooms, and make sure the cushions have not become moldy while the house is closed. Anne decides that Diana can go with her and also hopes to visit Theodora Dix during the outing.

Anne also receives a long letter from Philippa. Phil reports that she is enjoying her holiday with Alec and Alonzo but still cannot decide which one she prefers. She also describes an embarrassing streetcar incident in which she thought she had lost the coin for her fare. After searching every pocket and becoming increasingly desperate in front of the other passengers, she suddenly remembers that she had pushed the coin into the finger of one of her gloves.

During the holiday Anne and Diana visit Echo Lodge. They walk through the beech woods carrying a lunch basket. The little stone house has been closed since Miss Lavendar’s wedding. They open it, let in fresh air, and light a fire. The rooms still remind them strongly of Miss Lavendar, Charlotta the Fourth, and Paul.

Before leaving, Anne finds the old horn that used to hang behind the kitchen door. She and Diana go outside and call across the frozen landscape to hear the familiar echoes. The echoes answer clearly over the white river. Afterward the girls close Echo Lodge again and walk away together in the fading winter light.

## Conversation 1 — Back at Green Gables

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Diana: You looked so happy when we **pulled up** to Green Gables.
Anne: I was. The lights and Davy’s bonfire made everything feel familiar again.
Diana: Did college seem far away for a moment?
Anne: Completely. I just wanted to **settle back into** home life.
Diana: I’m sure Davy plans to keep you busy.
Anne: He already has. He’s been trying to **catch me up on** everything I missed.
Diana: And Mrs. Lynde?
Anne: She has plenty of news too. I’ll never **run out of** updates here.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- pull up — arrive and stop in a vehicle. We pulled up in front of the house just after dark.
- settle back into — become comfortable again in a familiar routine. It took only a day to settle back into life at home.
- catch someone up on — tell someone the important things that happened while they were away. She caught me up on all the office news.
- run out of — use or finish all of something so that none remains. We never run out of things to talk about.

## Conversation 2 — A Visit to Echo Lodge

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Anne: It feels strange to be here with the house empty.
Diana: I know. So many memories **come back** the minute we walk in.
Anne: Let’s open the windows and **air the place out** first.
Diana: Then we can light the fire.
Anne: Before we leave, I want to **look around for** the old horn.
Diana: Do you think it’s still here?
Anne: It should be. If we find it, we can **try it out** by the river.
Diana: Just like we used to.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- come back — return to your memory or awareness. The details came back as soon as I entered the room.
- air something out — let fresh air move through a room or object. We opened every window to air the cottage out.
- look around for — search in different places for something. I looked around for my keys before leaving.
- try something out — use or test something to see how it works. We tried out the old camera during the trip.
