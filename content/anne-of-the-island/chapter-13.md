# Chapter 13 — The Way of Transgressors

## Story Summary

One Sunday morning, Davy and Dora are preparing to go to Sunday School without an adult. Mrs. Rachel Lynde usually goes with them, but she has hurt her ankle and must stay home. Anne has gone to Carmody for the weekend, and Marilla has a headache.

Dora is ready and waiting in the hall. Davy has prepared himself, carrying his Bible and Sunday School paper. He has a cent for the Sunday School collection and five cents for church. He also knows his lesson, his Golden Text, and his catechism question.

Even so, Davy is in a bad mood. He is still angry with Mrs. Lynde because she recently persuaded Marilla not to let him go fishing with the Cotton children. Before Davy and Dora leave, Mrs. Lynde gives them a long list of instructions. She tells them not to walk in the dusty road, not to stop and talk in the church porch, not to whisper, not to forget their collections, and not to misbehave during the service.

As soon as Davy is out of the lane, he decides to do everything Mrs. Lynde told him not to do. He steps into the deep road dust and walks through it on purpose. Then he throws both collection coins over Mr. Barry’s fence. Finally, he decides not to go to Sunday School or church at all.

Dora is horrified and refuses to join him. Davy threatens to tell Marilla that a boy named Frank Bell kissed Dora at school unless Dora comes with him. Dora unwillingly follows. They take a shortcut to the Cotton house, where Pete, Tommy, Adolphus, and Mirabel Cotton are alone because the older members of the family are away.

The children decide to go fishing. They cannot use the pond because people on their way to church might see them, so they fish in the brook behind the Cotton house. Davy changes into Tommy Cotton’s overalls so that he will not ruin his Sunday clothes. The children catch many trout. Dora takes no pleasure in the outing and wishes she were at Sunday School.

After fishing, the children play around the Cotton farm. They climb onto the pig-house roof, cut their initials into the wood, and jump from another roof into a pile of straw. Davy acts as if he is having a wonderful time, but when the sound of church traffic tells him that people are returning home, he knows the visit must end.

On the way back, Dora tells Davy she does not think he really enjoyed himself. Davy strongly denies this, but he is already beginning to feel uncomfortable. He remembers that Mrs. Lynde has sometimes been kind to him, including the time she repaired his torn school trousers without telling Marilla.

At dinner Mrs. Lynde begins asking Davy about Sunday School and church. Davy tells several lies. He says he attended, gave his collection, and answered his lesson. When Mrs. Lynde asks about the sermon and announcements, Davy gives uncertain answers and finally repeats an old Golden Text as if it were the minister’s text.

Davy becomes so uncomfortable that he can hardly eat. Later Dora asks him how many lies he told. He tells her to be quiet and goes off alone behind the woodpile.

Anne returns late that night and goes straight to bed. Soon Davy enters her room in tears. He says he cannot sleep because he has been so bad. He confesses everything: skipping Sunday School, going fishing with the Cottons, lying to Mrs. Lynde, using a bad word, and saying disrespectful things about God.

Davy expects Anne to punish him, but she tells him that he has already been unhappy because of what he did. She explains that this unhappy feeling is his conscience. Davy says he wishes he did not have one, because he thinks life would be more fun without it.

Anne does not tell Marilla or Mrs. Lynde. Davy says he is sorry and promises not to repeat the same behavior. He says a short prayer and feels much better. Anne finally sends him back to bed.

A moment later, Davy returns with one last question. He asks whether, with enough practice, he could learn to spit like Mr. Harrison. This time Anne firmly orders him to go back to bed and stay there.

## Conversation 1 — Davy Changes the Plan

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Dora: We’re going to be late for Sunday School.
Davy: I’ve decided to **skip out on** it.
Dora: Davy, you can’t just **walk away from** the plan.
Davy: I’m tired of Mrs. Lynde telling me what to do.
Dora: That doesn’t mean you should **break every rule**.
Davy: I’m going fishing with the Cottons.
Dora: I’m not going.
Davy: Come on. Don’t **back out** now.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- skip out on — avoid or fail to attend something you are expected to attend. He skipped out on practice without telling anyone.
- walk away from — leave or abandon a plan or responsibility. You can’t just walk away from your responsibilities.
- break a rule — do something that a rule says you should not do. He knew he was breaking a rule.
- back out — decide not to do something you had agreed or planned to do. She backed out of the trip at the last minute.

## Conversation 2 — Davy Confesses

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Davy: Anne, I need to **own up to** something.
Anne: What happened?
Davy: I skipped Sunday School and lied about it.
Anne: I’m glad you’re telling me now.
Davy: I tried to **cover it up**, but I felt worse and worse.
Anne: That usually makes things harder.
Davy: I want to **make up for** what I did.
Anne: Start by telling the truth and not repeating it.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- own up to — admit that you did something wrong. He finally owned up to breaking the window.
- cover something up — hide the truth about something. She tried to cover up the mistake.
- make up for — do something good to balance a mistake or problem. I stayed late to make up for arriving late.
- tell the truth — say what really happened. It was difficult, but he finally told the truth.
