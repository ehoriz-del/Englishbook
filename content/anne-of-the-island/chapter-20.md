# Chapter 20 — Gilbert Speaks

## Story Summary

Spring examinations are over at Redmond, and the school year is nearly finished. One quiet day at Patty’s Place, Phil complains that nothing interesting has happened. Anne is reading Pickwick Papers and tells Phil that the day may have been ordinary for them, but somewhere else someone may have had an important experience. Phil prefers not to think about serious or unpleasant things.

The two friends talk for a while. Phil says she does not see any reason to become more serious because enough people in the world are serious already. She believes her role is to make life more amusing for others. Anne admits that Patty’s Place has been brighter because Phil has lived there.

Phil is tired because she stayed awake late the night before reading a frightening ghost story. After finishing it, she was too afraid to get out of bed and turn off the lamp. She waited until Stella came home and asked her to do it.

Their conversation turns to summer plans. Aunt Jamesina intends to remain at Patty’s Place, partly because of the cats. Phil asks Anne what she is reading, then decides that talking about Pickwick Papers has made her hungry. Anne tells her there is lemon pie in the pantry. Phil immediately goes to look for it.

Anne leaves the house and walks into the orchard with Rusty. It is an early spring evening. Some old snow still lies under the pines near the harbor road, but grass is beginning to grow in protected places. The air is cool and damp.

Anne sits on a large gray boulder in the orchard. She is lost in one of her daydreams when she sees Gilbert coming toward her. Recently she has tried not to be alone with him because she knows he wants to speak about their relationship. This time she cannot avoid him.

Gilbert has found a bunch of Mayflowers and gives them to Anne. Their scent immediately reminds her of Avonlea and the picnics of their school days. They talk about their summer plans. Anne tells him she will spend two weeks with Phil in Bolingbroke before returning to Green Gables.

Gilbert then says he will not be in Avonlea that summer. He has been offered work at the Daily News office and plans to take the job because the money will help with his expenses next year. Anne is surprised. She had expected Gilbert to be in Avonlea and suddenly realizes that a summer without him feels strange.

Anne tries to keep the conversation light. She talks about the evening and about white violets she found earlier. She suggests going to look for more and says she can call Phil to join them.

Gilbert stops her. He takes Anne’s hand and says there is something he must tell her. Anne immediately knows what is coming and asks him not to say it.

Gilbert continues. He tells Anne that he loves her and asks whether she will promise to become his wife someday.

Anne refuses. She says she cares deeply for Gilbert, but only as a friend. Gilbert asks whether she might come to love him differently in the future. Anne says no and insists that he must never speak to her about marriage again.

Gilbert asks whether there is someone else. Anne says there is not. She tells him that she likes him better than anyone else in the world and wants them to continue being friends.

Gilbert says friendship is no longer enough for him. He had believed there were times when Anne cared for him in the same way he cared for her, but now he realizes he was mistaken. He releases her hand and says goodbye.

Anne returns to her room and cries. She is upset because she believes she has lost Gilbert’s friendship. Her earlier ideas about refusing proposals had always involved graceful speeches and romantic situations, but the real experience has been painful and awkward.

Phil comes into the room and quickly guesses what has happened. She says Anne has refused Gilbert and calls her foolish. Anne replies that she cannot marry a man she does not love.

Phil tells Anne that she may not understand her own feelings. Anne asks her to leave because she wants to be alone. At that moment, Anne feels that something important in her life has changed, even though she still believes she made the only answer she could give.

## Conversation 1 — Gilbert’s Summer Plans

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Anne: So you won’t be in Avonlea this summer?
Gilbert: No. I **got offered** a job at the Daily News.
Anne: Are you going to take it?
Gilbert: Yes. It will **help me out** with expenses next year.
Anne: I thought we’d all be home.
Gilbert: Plans sometimes **work out differently** than we expect.
Anne: That’s true.
Gilbert: There’s something else I need to talk to you about.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- get offered — receive an offer for a job or opportunity. I got offered a summer position.
- help someone out — make a situation easier by giving help, money, or support. The extra job helped me out with tuition.
- work out differently — happen in a different way from what was expected. The trip worked out differently than we planned.
- talk to someone about — discuss an important subject with someone. I need to talk to you about something.

## Conversation 2 — After Gilbert Speaks

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Gilbert: I can’t **go on** pretending friendship is enough for me.
Anne: I never wanted to hurt you.
Gilbert: I know, but I thought you might **feel the same way** someday.
Anne: I care about you, but not in that way.
Gilbert: Then I need to **step back**.
Anne: Does that mean we can’t stay friends?
Gilbert: Not right now. I have to **come to terms with** your answer.
Anne: I’m sorry, Gilbert.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- go on — continue doing something. I can’t go on pretending nothing has changed.
- feel the same way — have the same feelings as another person. I liked him, but I didn’t feel the same way.
- step back — create some distance from a situation or relationship. I need to step back for a while.
- come to terms with — gradually accept a difficult reality. It took her time to come to terms with the decision.
