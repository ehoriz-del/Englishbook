# Chapter 32 — Tea with Mrs. Douglas

## Story Summary

During Anne’s first week in Valley Road, Janet asks her to attend the Thursday evening prayer meeting. Janet dresses much more carefully than Anne expects. She wears a pale-blue flowered dress with many ruffles and a white hat decorated with pink roses and feathers.

At the meeting, Anne notices that nearly everyone present is female. There are thirty-two women, two teenage boys, the minister, and only one other adult man.

Anne studies the man because he seems important to Janet. He is not handsome or graceful. He is tall, stooped, and rather untidy, with very long legs, large hands, hair that needs cutting, and an untrimmed moustache.

Still, Anne likes his face. She sees kindness, honesty, patience, and a quiet sense of humor in it. She also has the strange impression that he has suffered for a long time and learned to endure it.

After the meeting, the man approaches Janet and politely asks if he may walk her home. Janet suddenly becomes shy and formal, almost like a girl on her first walk with a young man. She introduces him as John Douglas.

John tells Anne that he noticed her during the meeting and thought she looked like a nice little girl. Normally Anne would dislike being called a little girl, but John says it so sincerely that she accepts it as a compliment.

Anne walks a little behind Janet and John on the moonlit road. She is delighted to discover that Janet has a beau. Janet is kind, practical, cheerful, and a wonderful cook, and Anne thinks she would make an excellent wife.

The next day Janet tells Anne that John’s mother wants to meet her. Mrs. Douglas is often confined to the house because of poor health and enjoys having visitors. Soon John himself comes to invite Janet and Anne to tea on Saturday.

When Saturday comes, Anne is surprised that Janet chooses a heavy black dress instead of the pretty blue one she wore to prayer meeting. Janet explains that Mrs. Douglas would consider the blue dress too frivolous, although John likes it.

The Douglas home is large and prosperous, surrounded by orchards, maples, and well-kept barns. John meets them at the door and takes them to his mother.

Mrs. Douglas is very different from what Anne expected. She is a tiny, delicate-looking elderly woman with soft pink cheeks, mild blue eyes, white hair, and a fashionable black silk dress. She welcomes Anne warmly and speaks affectionately to Janet.

However, the atmosphere is uncomfortable. Mrs. Douglas talks easily, but Janet blushes constantly, and John is unusually serious. At tea, Mrs. Douglas asks Janet to pour, treating her almost as if she already belonged in the household.

The table is covered with food: meat, chicken, preserves, biscuits, several pies, cakes, cookies, and tarts. Anne eats far more than usual, yet Mrs. Douglas worries that she has hardly touched anything.

After tea, Mrs. Douglas sends John and Janet into the garden to gather roses. Anne remains with her.

Mrs. Douglas tells Anne that she has suffered from poor health for more than twenty years. She says there have been many nights when everyone thought she might die before morning.

Then she speaks about Janet. She calls Janet a wonderful woman and excellent housekeeper and says she is thankful John has made such a wise choice. She talks as though John and Janet’s marriage is already certain.

Anne does not know how to respond. She cannot understand why the conversation feels so uncomfortable.

When John and Janet return, Mrs. Douglas affectionately tells Janet to visit more often and suggests that John may soon bring her there to stay permanently.

Anne looks at John at that exact moment and is shocked by his expression. Instead of looking happy or embarrassed, he looks deeply distressed.

As Anne and Janet walk home, Janet praises Mrs. Douglas and explains that her health worries John constantly. Anne says little. She keeps thinking about John’s face and wondering why a conversation about marrying Janet caused him so much pain.

## Conversation 1 — After Prayer Meeting

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Anne: So that was John Douglas.
Janet: Yes.
Anne: You never **mentioned him before**.
Janet: There wasn’t much to say.
Anne: He seems very kind.
Janet: He is.
Anne: I noticed you two **walked home together**.
Janet: He usually sees me home.
Anne: I think there’s more to this than you’re **letting on**.
Janet: Anne, don’t start imagining things.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- mention someone before — speak about someone at an earlier time. You never mentioned him before.
- walk someone home — accompany someone to their home on foot. He walked her home after the meeting.
- let on — reveal information, especially something you were hiding. She knew more than she let on.
- see someone home — accompany someone safely home. He offered to see her home.

## Conversation 2 — Leaving the Douglas House

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Janet: Mrs. Douglas likes you.
Anne: She was very friendly.
Janet: She’s been ill for years, so John worries about her.
Anne: I noticed something when she **brought up** marriage.
Janet: What?
Anne: John looked terribly upset.
Janet: You may be **reading too much into** it.
Anne: Maybe, but I can’t **shake off** the feeling that something is wrong.
Janet: I wouldn’t worry about it.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- bring something up — introduce a subject in conversation. She brought up the wedding during dinner.
- read too much into — give something more meaning than it really has. Don’t read too much into one comment.
- shake something off — get rid of a feeling or thought. I couldn’t shake off the feeling that something was wrong.
- worry about — feel anxious about something. He worries about his mother constantly.
