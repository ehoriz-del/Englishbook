# Chapter 20 — A Good Imagination Gone Wrong

## Story Summary — A2–B1 Level

Spring returns, and Anne happily explores the countryside with her friends. She and Diana use their imaginations to make ordinary places more exciting. They invent frightening stories about a wooded area between their homes and begin calling it haunted.

After repeating the stories, Anne starts to feel genuinely afraid. One evening, Marilla sends Anne through the woods on an errand. Anne begs to wait until morning, but Marilla insists that she go.

Anne runs through the woods in terror and returns safely. She realizes that she and Diana allowed their imagination to go too far. Anne does not lose her imagination, but she learns that it should add beauty to life rather than create unnecessary fear.

## Conversation 1 — The Haunted Wood

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Anne: Diana and I made up ghost stories.
Marilla: And now they're getting to you.
Anne: I don't want to go through the woods.

### Phrasal Verbs

- make up — invent.
- get to someone — begin to upset someone.
- go through — travel through a place.

## Conversation 2 — Afterward

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Marilla: You made it through.
Anne: Barely!
Marilla: Maybe you'll cut back on ghost stories.
Anne: We definitely went too far.

### Useful Expressions

- make it through — successfully survive or complete something.
- cut back on — reduce.
- go too far — do more than is reasonable.
