# Chapter 31 — Where the Brook and River Meet

## Story Summary — A2–B1 Level

Anne is about fifteen and clearly moving from childhood into young adulthood. Even Mrs. Rachel admits that she has become attractive. Marilla feels proud but also a little sad.

She misses the endlessly talkative child who first came to Green Gables. Anne still has imagination, but she keeps more thoughts inside. Anne continues preparing for the Queen's examination.

She works hard but sometimes becomes anxious about whether she will succeed. Her writing is changing too. Under Miss Stacy's guidance, she becomes less interested in exaggerated romance and more interested in honest, clear writing. Anne and Diana know that Anne may soon leave Avonlea for school.

Their friendship remains strong, but childhood is ending.

## Conversation 1 — Growing Up

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Marilla: You've certainly grown up.
Anne: I still think just as much.
Marilla: You don't chatter away like before.
Anne: I keep more to myself now.

### Phrasal Verbs

- grow up — develop into an adult.
- chatter away — talk continuously.
- keep to yourself — not share everything with others.

## Conversation 2 — Exam Worries

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Anne: I don't want to fall short.
Diana: You need to settle down and trust yourself.
Anne: You're right. I shouldn't wear myself out.

### Useful Expressions

- fall short — fail to reach a desired level.
- settle down — become calm and focused.
- wear yourself out — make yourself extremely tired.
