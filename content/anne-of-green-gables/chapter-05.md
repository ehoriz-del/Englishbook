# Chapter 5 — Anne's History

## Story Summary — A2–B1 Level

On the way to Mrs. Spencer’s house, Marilla asks Anne about her past. Anne explains that her parents, Walter and Bertha Shirley, were teachers. They died when Anne was still a baby. A woman named Mrs. Thomas took Anne in.

Anne lived with the Thomas family and helped care for their children. Although she was very young, she had many responsibilities. After Mr. Thomas died, Mrs. Thomas could no longer keep Anne.

Anne then went to live with Mrs. Hammond, who had many children. Anne again spent much of her time caring for younger children. When Mr. Hammond died, Anne could no longer stay there either.

Eventually, she was sent to an orphanage, where she lived for several months before Mrs. Spencer arranged for her to come to Prince Edward Island. As Marilla listens, she begins to understand Anne better. Anne has never had a stable, loving home of her own.

She often helped adults and cared for children, but rarely had someone who truly cared for her. Anne uses imagination to make difficult experiences easier to bear. Her story affects Marilla, who begins to question whether sending Anne away is really the right thing to do.

## Conversation 1 — Anne’s Childhood

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Marilla: Who looked after you after your parents died?
Anne: Mrs. Thomas took me in at first.
Marilla: And after that?
Anne: I eventually ended up with another family.

### Phrasal Verbs

- look after — take care of someone.
- take someone in — allow someone to live in your home.
- end up — finally arrive at a situation or place, often unexpectedly.

## Conversation 2 — A Difficult Past

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Marilla: You’ve had to put up with a lot.
Anne: I suppose so.
Marilla: How did you get through it?
Anne: My imagination helped me get away from unhappy thoughts.

### Useful Expressions

- put up with — accept an unpleasant situation.
- get through — survive or deal with a difficult experience.
- get away from — escape or take a break from something unpleasant.
