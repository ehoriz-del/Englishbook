# Chapter 7 — Anne Says Her Prayers

## Story Summary — A2–B1 Level

That evening, Marilla begins teaching Anne the habits she expects at Green Gables. She tells Anne to keep her room tidy and to say her prayers before bed. Anne surprises Marilla by admitting that she has never really learned how to pray.

Anne knows a formal description of God from the orphanage, but she does not feel close to Him. She even says that she once blamed God for giving her red hair. Marilla is shocked and decides that Anne needs religious instruction.

Marilla first tries to teach Anne a traditional children's prayer. Then she lets Anne pray in her own words. Anne thanks God for the beautiful things she has seen at Green Gables and asks to be allowed to stay there.

She also hopes she might become pretty when she grows up. Her prayer is unusual but sincere. Marilla is both worried and touched. She decides that Anne should attend Sunday school and learn more about religion and proper behavior.

For Marilla, raising Anne is clearly going to require patience.

## Conversation 1 — Bedtime

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*
Marilla: Before you turn in, you need to say your prayers.
Anne: I don't really know how.
Marilla: Then we'll go over it together.
Anne: Could I say what I'm really thinking?
Marilla: Yes, but try not to get carried away.

### Phrasal Verbs

- turn in — go to bed. I'm tired. I think I'll turn in early.
- go over — review or explain carefully. Can we go over the instructions again?
- get carried away — become too excited. I got carried away and talked for an hour.

## Conversation 2 — Learning Something New

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Anne: What if I mess up?
Marilla: Then you learn from it and try again.
Anne: I think I can keep at it.
Marilla: Good. You'll pick things up little by little.

### Useful Expressions

- mess up — make a mistake. I messed up the recipe.
- keep at it — continue trying. It's difficult, but keep at it.
- pick up — learn gradually. She picked up English quickly.
