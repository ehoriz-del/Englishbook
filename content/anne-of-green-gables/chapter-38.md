# Chapter 38 — The Bend in the Road

## Story Summary — A2–B1 Level

After Matthew's death, Marilla visits an eye specialist and receives frightening news. Her eyesight is failing, and she must avoid strain or she may become blind. Anne realizes that Marilla cannot manage Green Gables alone.

Marilla even considers selling the farm. Anne makes a major decision. She gives up the Avery Scholarship and her plan to attend Redmond College. Instead, she will stay near Green Gables, become a teacher, and take care of Marilla.

At first, Anne plans to teach at Carmody because Gilbert has been offered the Avonlea school. Then Mrs. Rachel brings surprising news: Gilbert has given up the Avonlea position so Anne can have it and stay close to Marilla. He will teach farther away. Anne is deeply touched.

When she meets Gilbert, she thanks him and finally ends their long silence. They agree to become friends. Anne's future is very different from the one she imagined. Yet she does not see her new path as the end of her dreams.

She thinks of life as a road with a bend in it: she cannot see everything ahead, but the unknown future can still be meaningful and beautiful. The novel ends with Anne at Green Gables, surrounded by love, responsibility, friendship, and hope.

## Conversation 1 — A New Decision

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Marilla: You shouldn't give up Redmond because of me.
Anne: I'm not giving up my future. I'm changing course.
Marilla: What will you do?
Anne: Teach nearby and look after you.

### Phrasal Verbs

- give up — stop pursuing something.
- change course — choose a different direction or plan.
- look after — take care of.

## Conversation 2 — Anne and Gilbert

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Anne: I heard you gave up the Avonlea school for me.
Gilbert: I knew you needed to stay near home.
Anne: I'd like to start over.
Gilbert: So would I.
Anne: Then let's put the past behind us.
Gilbert: Friends?
Anne: Friends.

### Useful Expressions

- give up — let someone else have something or stop having it.
- start over — begin again in a new way.
- put the past behind you — stop allowing an old conflict to control the present.
