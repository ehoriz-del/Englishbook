# Chapter 12 — Hazel’s Letter

## Story Summary

A week after Hazel’s angry visit to Windy Poplars, Anne receives a letter from her.

The letter is written on pale blue paper with a silver edge. Hazel begins by announcing that all the misunderstanding between herself and Terry has been cleared away.

She says that she and Terry are now deeply and wonderfully happy. According to Hazel, they have decided that they can forgive Anne for what she did.

Hazel explains that Terry has told her he was temporarily confused when he spoke romantically to Anne. Hazel says his heart never truly stopped belonging to her.

She also says Terry prefers sweet and simple girls and has no interest in women who are clever or designing. The description is clearly meant as a criticism of Anne.

Hazel tells Anne that neither she nor Terry understands why Anne interfered in their relationship. She suggests that perhaps Anne only wanted material for a story and used their romance for that purpose.

At the same time, Hazel claims that Anne’s actions accidentally helped them. She says the experience revealed Terry and Hazel to each other more clearly and made their love stronger.

Hazel describes herself and Terry as soul-mates who can understand each other’s thoughts. They have promised to remain faithful no matter how many jealous people or false friends try to cause trouble.

She then announces that they are going to marry as soon as her wedding clothes are ready.

Hazel plans to travel to Boston to buy her trousseau because she does not think Summerside shops have enough suitable choices. She describes her wedding dress and traveling suit in detail.

Although Hazel is young, she says she wants to marry while she is still young and before the freshness of life has faded.

Hazel insists that Terry is everything she ever dreamed of and that every thought in her heart now belongs to him.

She ends the main letter by saying she once believed all her friends would be happy about her happiness, but she has now learned a bitter lesson about the world.

The letter includes two postscripts.

In the first postscript, Hazel rejects Anne’s warning that Terry has a temper. She says Terry’s sister has told her he is a perfect lamb.

The second postscript is much more personal. Hazel tells Anne that she has heard lemon juice can bleach freckles and suggests that Anne try it on her nose.

After reading the letter, Anne turns to Dusty Miller and remarks that the second postscript is the last straw.

## Vocabulary & Nuance

- **allegiance** — loyalty or continued faithfulness to a person or cause.
- **designing** — clever in a dishonest or manipulative way.
- **trousseau** — a collection of clothes and personal items prepared for a bride.
- **constancy** — continued loyalty and faithfulness.
- **postscript** — an extra message added after the main ending of a letter.
- **clear up** — resolve a misunderstanding or confusion.
- **make up** — become friendly again after a quarrel.
- **swear off** — decide firmly to stop doing something.
- **rub something in** — make someone feel worse by repeatedly mentioning something unpleasant.
- **be the last straw** — be the final annoyance that makes a situation intolerable.
