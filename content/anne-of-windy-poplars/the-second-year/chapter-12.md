# Chapter 12 — A Letter of Forgiveness

## Story Summary

A week after Hazel's angry visit, a pale blue envelope edged with silver arrived at Windy Poplars. Anne recognized the handwriting at once and placed the letter beside her work for several minutes before curiosity defeated discipline. Hazel rarely wrote anything that could be described as brief, and the thickness of the envelope suggested that reconciliation had produced almost as much language as despair.

When Anne unfolded the pages, she discovered that the emotional weather had changed completely. Hazel announced that every misunderstanding between herself and Terry had been cleared away. They were happier than ever and intended to marry. In a gesture Hazel clearly considered generous, the couple had also decided they could forgive Anne.

Anne paused over that sentence. Only a week earlier she had been accused of jealousy, betrayal, and deliberate interference. Now the same events had been rearranged into a romantic crisis that Hazel and Terry had nobly survived. Anne occupied the role of misguided outsider, but at least she was no longer the villain of an active tragedy. She kept reading with the resigned interest of someone watching a familiar play take an unexpected turn.

Hazel explained that Terry had never truly stopped loving her. His brief fascination with Anne had been the result of moonlight, confusion, and the emotional strain caused by the engagement. Hazel accepted this explanation with surprising ease. Terry had also decided that he preferred a sweet, uncomplicated girl to a clever woman who might try to manage him. Anne raised her eyebrows at that part but resisted the temptation to argue with a letter.

According to Hazel, the crisis had proved the depth of their bond. They understood one another perfectly now. Their love had survived interference and misunderstanding, which meant it must be stronger than before. Hazel described the reconciliation in language so absolute that Anne could almost hear her speaking. Happiness became rapture, loyalty became eternal devotion, and an argument of several days became a test worthy of a legendary romance.

Practical details followed the declarations. The wedding would take place as soon as Hazel's clothes were ready. Summerside shops could not possibly provide everything required, so she planned a trip to Boston. There would be a white wedding dress, carefully chosen accessories, and a traveling suit elegant enough to begin married life properly. The girl who had recently felt trapped by the engagement now wrote as if marriage were the only future she had ever seriously considered.

Anne found herself relieved despite the absurdity. Hazel and Terry had chosen each other again, and this time Anne had played no role in the decision. If the couple wanted to interpret the confusion as proof of destiny, they were entitled to do so. She had no desire to improve their version of events, correct the emotional timeline, or remind Hazel of the plans for nursing and Mediterranean adventures.

Hazel also wrote that she wanted to marry while she was still young enough to feel the full brightness of life. Terry, she now insisted, was everything her imagination had ever asked for, and all the earlier doubts seemed to have vanished so completely that Anne wondered whether Hazel remembered how convincing they had sounded. The certainty was almost impressive. What had been a prison in May had become destiny in June, and Hazel saw no need to explain the change.

Hazel was not finished. A postscript defended Terry's temper and reported that his sister considered him extremely gentle. Hazel insisted Anne had worried unnecessarily about his character. Then came another piece of advice that had nothing to do with romance. She had heard that lemon juice could make freckles less noticeable and thought Anne might find the information useful.

Anne lowered the letter and stared at Dusty Miller. Anne could forgive the accusations and tolerate the grand language, and even Hazel's generous decision to forgive her for a problem Hazel herself had helped create was almost funny. Unrequested advice about her freckles required a deeper level of patience.

Irritation rose and then collapsed under the weight of the situation's absurdity. Hazel was not calculating enough to be cruel in any organized way. She was sincere in every emotional version of reality while she occupied it. When unhappy, she had truly believed herself trapped. When angry, she had truly believed Anne was a designing rival. Now that she was happy again, she truly believed the entire conflict had been a test that proved the greatness of her love.

Anne remembered the moonlit garden where Terry's gratitude started becoming admiration, and the earlier evening when Hazel begged someone else to end the engagement for her. Those moments had felt urgent and final at the time. Hazel's letter now treated them as temporary confusion. Anne understood more clearly why interfering had been such a mistake. A person who changed emotional certainty so quickly could not safely hand decisions to someone who assumed words had stable meaning.

She folded the silver edged pages and put them away. She still liked Hazel. The girl's vanity, imagination, insecurity, affection, and self-dramatizing habits were tangled together too closely to separate into simple virtues and faults. Life with Hazel would probably continue to produce crises, reconciliations, and strong opinions about other people's complexions.

What had changed was Anne's sense of responsibility. Hazel and Terry had chosen their own future, and Anne was genuinely glad if they were happy, but she no longer believed their happiness was something she had to manage for them. If new complications appeared, the couple would have to work through them themselves. Anne had learned enough from being temporarily cast as the villain. She looked at Dusty Miller and decided that Hazel's final postscript was, in Rebecca Dew's language, the last straw. Dusty offered no comment, which made him the most sensible participant in the whole affair.

## Vocabulary & Nuance

- **reconciliation** — the restoration of a friendly or loving relationship after conflict.
- **reinterpret** — understand or explain a past event in a new way.
- **absolute** — expressed as complete and without qualification.
- **entitled** — having the right to do or believe something.
- **calculating** — deliberately planning behavior for personal advantage.
- **clear something up** — resolve confusion or misunderstanding.
- **work through** — deal with a problem gradually until it becomes manageable.
- **move past** — continue beyond a difficult event.
- **make up** — become friendly again after an argument.
- **brush something aside** — dismiss something without much consideration.

## Conversation 1 — Reading Hazel’s Letter

Anne: Apparently Terry and Hazel have cleared everything up.
Rebecca: And what part have they assigned you in the new version?
Anne: The person they have generously decided to forgive.
Rebecca: That is convenient.
Anne: I am going to **move past** it before the explanation gets any stranger.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- clear something up — resolve confusion or misunderstanding.
- move past — continue beyond a difficult event.
- make up — become friendly again after conflict.
- brush aside — dismiss with little attention.
- leave well enough alone — avoid changing a situation that is acceptable as it is.

## Conversation 2 — Anne Draws a Boundary

Friend: Would you give Hazel advice again?
Anne: Only if she wanted perspective, not someone to take over the decision.
Friend: So you would help her **work through** it rather than solve it for her.
Anne: Exactly. Advice is not the same thing as ownership.
Friend: That is a useful distinction.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- work through — deal with a difficult issue gradually.
- take over — assume control.
- talk through — discuss carefully in order to understand.
- leave with — allow responsibility to remain with someone.
- step back — reduce involvement.
