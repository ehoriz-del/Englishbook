# Chapter 12 — A Letter of Forgiveness

## Story Summary

A week after Hazel's visit, a letter arrived for Anne on pale blue paper edged with silver. She recognized Hazel's handwriting before she opened it. The quarrel in the tower room was still recent, so Anne could reasonably have expected another complaint. Instead, the first lines announced a complete change in the situation. Hazel and Terry had cleared up every misunderstanding and were now intensely happy together.

Hazel also announced, with great generosity, that she and Terry had decided they could forgive Anne. Terry had explained his brief interest in Anne by saying that moonlight had confused him, just as moonlight had earlier confused both him and Hazel about their engagement. According to Hazel, his heart had never truly left her. He now knew exactly what kind of woman he preferred, and his description was not especially flattering to Anne. He liked sweet, simple girls and had no use for women he considered designing or too clever.

The letter still blamed Anne for interfering. Hazel said she and Terry would never understand why Anne had behaved as she did and even suggested that perhaps Anne had wanted material for a story. Yet the accusation was followed almost immediately by thanks. By interfering, Hazel claimed, Anne had forced them to discover the deeper truth about their relationship. The crisis had revealed them to themselves, and therefore everything had somehow happened for the best.

Hazel described their renewed love in the largest possible terms. She and Terry understood each other's thoughts. Nobody understood him as she did, and she intended to inspire him for the rest of his life. They considered themselves soulmates and had promised eternal loyalty no matter how many jealous people or false friends tried to cause trouble. The doubts Hazel had poured out in the tower room had disappeared completely from her version of the future.

They planned to marry as soon as Hazel's trousseau was ready. She would go to Boston because Summerside could not provide everything she wanted. Her wedding dress would be white moire, and her traveling suit would be dove gray with accessories in delphinium blue. Hazel admitted that she was young, but now youth was a reason to marry rather than a reason to wait. She wanted to begin married life before, as she put it, the bloom went off life.

The letter closed with another declaration that Terry was everything her dreams had pictured. Hazel expected rapturous happiness, although she added that recent events had taught her a bitter lesson about false friends and worldly wisdom. Anne could only read on. There was no practical question left for her to answer. Hazel and Terry had made their decision, and the elaborate explanation belonged to them.

Two postscripts remained. In the first, Hazel defended Terry against Anne's earlier warning about his temper and cited his sister's opinion that he was perfectly gentle. The second postscript was much shorter and far more personal. Hazel had heard that lemon juice might lighten freckles and suggested that Anne try it on her nose.

Anne lowered the letter and looked at Dusty Miller. She had managed to read accusations, forgiveness, eternal devotion, wedding clothes, and Terry's revised character without comment. The advice about her freckles was enough. Borrowing one of Rebecca Dew's favorite expressions, Anne decided that the second postscript was the last straw.

## Vocabulary & Nuance

- **reconciliation** — the restoration of a friendly or loving relationship after conflict.
- **reinterpret** — understand or explain a past event in a new way.
- **rapturous** — expressing extremely strong happiness or enthusiasm.
- **constancy** — the quality of remaining loyal and dependable.
- **postscript** — an additional message added at the end of a letter after the signature.
- **clear something up** — resolve confusion or misunderstanding.
- **brush something aside** — dismiss something without much consideration.
- **leave well enough alone** — avoid interfering further in a situation that no longer needs your help.
- **read too much into** — find more meaning in something than is really there.
- **have the last word** — make the final remark in a disagreement or exchange.

## Conversation 1 — Reading Hazel’s Letter

Anne: Apparently Terry and Hazel have cleared everything up.
Rebecca: And what part have they assigned you in the new version?
Anne: The person they have generously decided to forgive.
Rebecca: That is convenient.
Anne: I am going to **move past** it before the explanation gets any stranger.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- clear something up — resolve confusion or misunderstanding.
- move past — continue beyond a difficult event.
- make up — become friendly again after conflict.
- brush aside — dismiss with little attention.
- leave well enough alone — avoid changing a situation that is acceptable as it is.

## Conversation 2 — The Last Postscript

Rebecca: You survived the accusations and the wedding plans.
Anne: I was doing quite well until the advice about my freckles.
Rebecca: So that was the part you could not **brush aside**?
Anne: Exactly. I think I will leave well enough alone from now on.
Rebecca: At least Hazel managed to have the last word.
