# Chapter 13 — A Fortnight at Green Gables

## Story Summary

Anne leaves Summerside for her second summer vacation with mixed feelings. Gilbert will not be in Avonlea because he has gone west to work on a new railroad. Anne is disappointed that she will not see him, but she is still happy to return to Green Gables and the familiar places of Avonlea.

This summer Anne has an important companion. She has persuaded Mrs. Campbell to allow Little Elizabeth to spend two weeks at Green Gables.

Elizabeth is overwhelmed with excitement. As she and Anne drive away from Windy Poplars, she asks Anne to introduce her as Miss Elizabeth because the title makes her feel grown up. Anne agrees, remembering how seriously she herself once wanted to be called Cordelia.

The trip from Blight River toward Green Gables is almost magical for Elizabeth. The June countryside is bright and green, and every turn in the road seems to promise something new. She is wearing a new pink gingham dress and new brown boots, and she is free from The Evergreens for fourteen whole days.

As the Green Gables lane comes into view, Elizabeth feels as if she has nearly reached Tomorrow, the wonderful future she has imagined for so long.

Life changes for her as soon as she arrives.

Green Gables feels friendly in a way The Evergreens never has. Marilla, Mrs. Lynde, Davy, and Dora welcome her. Davy and Dora quickly become devoted to her, and the older women approve of her good manners.

Elizabeth sleeps with Anne in the little porch gable. On the first night she does not want to sleep because she is afraid of wasting even a minute of her visit.

She lies awake listening to the distant sound of the sea and the wind around the house. At The Evergreens, darkness has always frightened her. At Green Gables, the night no longer seems dangerous.

Anne has promised that they will go to the shore the next day. Elizabeth falls asleep thinking about the waves beyond the dunes.

During the following days, Elizabeth explores the places Anne has loved for years. She spends time in the gardens, orchards, fields, and woods.

She is allowed to make noise if she wants to, which feels strange after the quiet rules of The Evergreens. Once she has permission to be noisy, however, she no longer feels such a strong need to rebel.

Elizabeth learns to laugh freely.

She dances in the twilight garden among June lilies. She climbs into the big Duchess apple tree and reads fairy tales. She wanders through fields of buttercups and chases moths and fireflies.

She listens to bees among the flowers, gathers pink roses, and watches the moon over the brook valley.

Dora gives her strawberries and cream in the pantry. The two girls eat red currants in the yard, and Elizabeth says they are like eating jewels.

Elizabeth helps with ordinary household activities as well. She helps Anne frost cupcakes, watches Mrs. Lynde work on a quilt, helps Dora polish brass candlesticks, and learns from Marilla how to cut tiny biscuits.

Even simple tasks are exciting because she is doing them in a place where she feels wanted.

One afternoon she falls asleep on the kitchen sofa with Dora’s kittens curled around her.

At another time she laughs so hard at the wind blowing the hens’ tails over their backs that the people at Green Gables can hardly believe this is the same quiet, careful child Anne brought from Summerside.

The visit passes quickly.

Elizabeth sometimes lies awake thinking about the difference between Green Gables and The Evergreens. She cannot understand why one home feels full of freedom and warmth while the other requires everyone to move and speak softly.

She wishes ordinary life could feel more like these two weeks.

When the fortnight ends, Elizabeth is heartbroken to leave. The road back toward the station is just as beautiful as it was when she arrived, but she often cannot see it clearly because she is crying.

The people at Green Gables miss her after she goes. Mrs. Lynde admits that she never expected to become so attached to a child.

The house and grounds seem full of reminders of Elizabeth. Everyone remembers seeing her in the garden, the apple tree, the fields, the pantry, and the kitchen.

After Elizabeth returns to Summerside, Katherine Brooke comes to Green Gables with her dog and stays for the rest of the summer.

Katherine has resigned from her position at Summerside High. She has decided that she does not want to spend the rest of her life teaching.

In the fall, she plans to attend Redmond University and take a secretarial course. Anne has encouraged the decision because Katherine has never enjoyed teaching and now has the freedom to choose another direction.

One evening Anne and Katherine sit together in a corner of a clover field and watch the sunset.

Katherine says she feels younger than she did a year earlier. She is ready to pursue a different kind of life and wants more than the years of debt, boarding houses, and work she has already experienced.

Anne is happy for her but knows she will miss Katherine in Summerside.

She thinks about the evenings they have spent in the tower room talking, arguing, and turning ordinary things into jokes.

The second year ends with Elizabeth carrying memories of Green Gables back to Summerside and Katherine preparing to leave teaching behind. Anne will soon begin her third and final year at Summerside High without Katherine beside her.

## Vocabulary & Nuance

- **fortnight** — a period of two weeks.
- **ecstatic** — extremely happy and excited.
- **sojourn** — a temporary stay in a place.
- **unhindered** — free to act without being blocked or restricted.
- **resign** — formally leave a job or position.
- **look forward to** — feel happy or excited about something in the future.
- **settle in** — become comfortable in a new place.
- **take in** — notice, experience, or understand something fully.
- **grow attached to** — gradually develop strong affection for someone.
- **move on from** — leave a situation behind and begin a new stage.

## Conversation 1 — A Place That Feels Different

Elizabeth: Why does everything feel easier here
Anne: Because you feel welcome.
Elizabeth: Even the night feels different.
Anne: Places can feel very different when the people in them make us feel safe.
Elizabeth: I wish two weeks could last forever.
Anne: Then we will fill them with enough memories to last much longer.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- settle in — become comfortable in a place.
- take in — experience or notice something fully.
- look forward to — anticipate with pleasure.
- hold on to — keep a memory or feeling.
- head back — return to a previous place.

## Conversation 2 — Katherine Chooses a New Direction

Katherine: I have resigned from the High School.
Anne: And you are certain
Katherine: Completely. I have never liked teaching.
Anne: Then the secretarial course sounds like a good next step.
Katherine: I feel as if I am finally moving instead of standing still.
Anne: I will miss you in Summerside.
Katherine: I will miss our tower-room arguments too.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- move on from — leave one stage and begin another.
- start out — begin a new activity or career.
- give up — stop doing something.
- look ahead — think about the future.
- set out to — begin with the intention of achieving something.
