# Chapter 1 — Back at Windy Poplars

## Story Summary

Anne returns to Windy Poplars in early September for her third and final year as principal of Summerside High School. The summer has ended, and she has seen Gilbert only once since May. She writes to him from the tower room and admits that coming back feels different this time because Katherine Brooke is no longer in Summerside. Katherine spent part of the summer at Green Gables and is now preparing for a new life. Anne misses the evenings they used to spend talking, arguing, and joking together.

A new junior teacher has joined the High School staff. Anne describes her as cheerful, chubby, rosy, and friendly. She likes the young woman immediately, but she already knows that the friendship will not have the same depth as her friendship with Katherine. The new teacher is pleasant and easy to understand, while Katherine had always seemed to contain new and unexpected sides of herself.

Windy Poplars itself is mostly unchanged, although Anne notices several differences as soon as she returns. The old red cow has died. Aunt Kate, Aunt Chatty, and Rebecca Dew have decided not to replace her. Instead, they will buy milk and cream from Mr. Cherry. Rebecca reports the news sadly because the cow had been part of the household routine for years.

The change also affects Little Elizabeth. She used to come regularly to the Windy Poplars garden gate for fresh milk. That small errand gave her a convenient reason to visit Anne. Anne is sorry that the excuse has disappeared, but she is relieved that Mrs. Campbell has become somewhat more willing to let Elizabeth come over simply because she wants to. Elizabeth no longer needs to hide every visit behind a household task.

Anne is more troubled by another change. Aunt Kate tells her that the family plans to give Dusty Miller away when they can find a good home for him. Rebecca Dew has complained about the cat so often during the summer that Aunt Kate and Aunt Chatty have decided giving him away may be the only way to restore peace.

Anne protests immediately. She is very fond of Dusty Miller and thinks of him as a comfortable, prowling, purring part of Windy Poplars. She cannot imagine the tower room and household without him. The decision has not yet been carried out, but the possibility makes Anne unhappy.

Her attention soon shifts to a new responsibility. Mrs. Raymond has asked Anne to look after her twins on Saturday while she travels to Charlottetown for a relative’s funeral. Mrs. Raymond moved to Summerside the previous winter. Some people think she considers herself a little too important for the town, but Anne remembers that she was generous and helpful with the Dramatic Club. Anne therefore feels that helping her for one day is only fair.

The twins are named Gerald and Geraldine. They are eight years old and extremely beautiful. Their pale golden hair, blue eyes, and innocent expressions make them look almost angelic. Anne expects to enjoy spending the day with them because she has always liked children.

Rebecca Dew does not share Anne’s confidence.

When Anne tells her about the plan, Rebecca reacts with open doubt. She warns Anne that Gerald and Geraldine may look like angels, but their behavior is famous throughout the neighborhood. Rebecca calls them little terrors and explains that Mrs. Raymond does not believe in punishment. She wants her children to live what she calls a natural life and seems almost proud of their high spirits.

Rebecca has heard many stories from the neighbors. On one occasion the minister’s wife visited Mrs. Raymond. When the woman was leaving, a shower of Spanish onions came flying down the stairs. One of them knocked her hat off. Mrs. Raymond apparently treated the incident as another example of children being naturally lively at exactly the wrong time.

Anne still believes she can manage them. She tells Rebecca that she loves children. Rebecca answers that loving children is not the issue. According to her, Gerald and Geraldine have fooled many adults because their faces look so sweet and harmless.

Rebecca also points out that they are from the United States, speaking as though that fact might explain some of their behavior. Anne recognizes the familiar local prejudice in the remark and does not take it seriously.

Even after all these warnings, Anne does not cancel the arrangement. She has taught children, handled difficult students, dealt with the Pringles, and faced many unexpected situations in Summerside. She sees no reason to be frightened by two eight-year-olds.

At the same time, the warnings stay in her mind. She knows Rebecca is not easily impressed by appearances and that the twins have already gained a reputation in town. Anne goes into the weekend expecting an active day, but she still thinks patience, affection, and sensible guidance will be enough.

As she finishes her letter to Gilbert, her third year in Summerside is only beginning. Katherine is gone, the household routine has shifted, Little Elizabeth is growing older, Dusty Miller may be sent away, and Anne has promised to spend the next day caring for two children everyone else seems to fear.

She remains hopeful. Saturday will show whether Rebecca Dew’s warnings are exaggerated or whether Anne has agreed to a much more difficult task than she realizes.

## Vocabulary & Nuance

- **reconciled** — willing to accept a situation that cannot easily be changed.
- **protest** — express strong disagreement or objection.
- **suitable** — right or appropriate for a particular purpose.
- **permissive** — allowing a great deal of freedom and setting few limits.
- **unmanageable** — very difficult to control.
- **settle back into** — become comfortable again in a familiar routine.
- **give away** — transfer something to another person without payment.
- **look after** — take care of someone.
- **take someone in** — deceive someone by appearing different from what you really are.
- **make light of** — treat something as less serious than it really is.

## Conversation 1 — Returning to a Familiar Place

Mina: Does coming back feel strange after the summer?
Anne: A little. I have **settled back into** the tower room quickly, but Summerside feels different without Katherine.
Mina: At least Windy Poplars is familiar.
Anne: Yes. I know the house so well now that even the smallest changes stand out.
Mina: Such as the cow being gone?
Anne: And Dusty Miller possibly being given away. I am not happy about that at all.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- settle back into — become comfortable again with a familiar routine.
- stand out — be especially noticeable.
- get used to — become accustomed to something.
- miss out on — lose the chance to experience something.
- hold on to — keep something rather than let it go.

## Conversation 2 — A Warning About the Twins

Rebecca: Those children may look like angels, but do not let that fool you.
Anne: I have looked after plenty of children.
Rebecca: Not Gerald and Geraldine.
Anne: You think they will be impossible?
Rebecca: I think you will **find out** soon enough.
Anne: I still believe patience will help.
Rebecca: Then I hope your patience can **hold up** until supper.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- find out — discover the truth.
- hold up — remain strong or effective.
- fool someone — deceive someone.
- get away with — avoid punishment for something wrong.
- wear someone out — make someone extremely tired.
