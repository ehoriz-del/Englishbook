# Chapter 6 — A Plan to Elope

## Story Summary

Not long after the High School commencement, Jarvis Morrow walked home with Anne and finally spoke with the impatience he had been trying to control. For more than a year he had accepted secret meetings, delays, and repeated promises from Dovie that disappeared as soon as she returned to Elmcroft. His love for her had not weakened, but his willingness to remain suspended between hope and disappointment had reached its limit.

Anne listened while he explained the plan he had proposed. In Jarvis's view, it barely deserved the dramatic name of elopement. Dovie would leave home on an evening when Franklin was away in Charlottetown, go to the house of Jarvis's sister Julia, and marry him there. A minister would be present, along with members of Jarvis's family. The ceremony would be legal, respectable, and private. Afterward they could leave for a short honeymoon with his aunt in Kingsport. Nothing about the plan was reckless except that it required Dovie to act without her father's permission.

That, of course, was the very obstacle she could not overcome. Jarvis said he could almost persuade her when they were together. She would listen, cry, agree that they could not continue indefinitely, and come close to promising. Then she would return home and send word that she could not go through with it. Years of living under Franklin's authority had trained her to retreat the moment a decision might provoke him.

Anne understood the fear, but she also saw what it was doing to Jarvis. He had begun to feel foolish. Love could survive obstacles, but it was harder to survive the sense that only one person was taking risks. Anne told him that he had to stop begging for small promises and ask for a real decision. Dovie must choose whether she intended to build a life with him or remain under her father's control.

Jarvis disliked the advice because it introduced the possibility he feared most. Dovie might choose Franklin. Anne thought that unlikely, but she refused to pretend there was no risk. If Dovie did not care enough to act, Jarvis needed to know before more years passed. Poetry about unreachable roses would not solve the problem. He needed plain language, a clear boundary, and the courage to accept whatever answer came back.

A few evenings later Dovie appeared at Windy Poplars looking as if the decision had followed her through the door. Jarvis had taken Anne's advice and forced the issue. Franklin would be attending a Masonic banquet in Charlottetown the following week. Jarvis wanted Dovie to come to Julia's house that evening and marry him.

Dovie did not ask Anne for information. She asked Anne to decide. Her fear made every possible consequence feel enormous. Franklin had forbidden Jarvis even to come near the house and had threatened to set the bulldog on him. Dovie was certain her father would never forgive her if she married without his consent. Yet when Anne asked whether she could live without Jarvis, Dovie's answer was equally certain. She could not.

Anne refused to make the choice in the way Dovie wanted. She did not say that nothing could go wrong. She did not promise Franklin would eventually forgive her. Instead she reduced the decision to its essential shape. Dovie could remain where she was and accept the possibility of losing Jarvis, or she could marry Jarvis and face her father's anger. There was no version in which everyone stayed pleased and nothing changed.

Dovie cried, hesitated, and tried to hand the responsibility back to Anne. Anne kept returning it. She could advise Dovie to be honest about what she wanted, but the final act had to belong to Dovie herself. At last Dovie said she would go through with the plan. She would tell Jarvis to obtain the license, and she would meet him on the evening Franklin was away.

Jarvis arrived later in a state of relief that made him almost boyish. Tuesday had been chosen. He would wait at the end of the Elmcroft lane so that Aunt Maggie would not see him near the house. From there they would go directly to Julia's, where the minister and family members would be ready. He repeated the details with the confidence of a man who had spent so long waiting that a firm date felt like victory.

Anne shared his relief, but not without uneasiness. She had done more than listen. She had urged Jarvis to set a limit and had helped Dovie see that avoiding a choice was itself a choice. If the marriage succeeded, perhaps no one would remember how much influence Anne had exercised. If it went badly, she knew she would remember every word.

As Tuesday approached, Anne tried not to imagine all the ways Dovie might lose her courage. The plan itself was simple. The difficult part was not the minister, the license, or the journey into Summerside. The difficult part was whether Dovie could remain loyal to her own decision once she was back inside Elmcroft, surrounded by the habits of fear that had shaped her for years.

For the first time, Anne fully understood that courage did not become permanent simply because someone had felt it for an hour. Dovie would have to choose Jarvis again when the appointed evening came. No advice, however sensible, could do that final piece of work for her.

## Vocabulary & Nuance

- **suspended** — left in an uncertain state without a final decision.
- **obstacle** — something that blocks progress.
- **retreat** — move back from a position, often because of fear or pressure.
- **boundary** — a clear limit that defines what one will accept.
- **essential** — most basic and important.
- **consequence** — a result that follows from a choice or action.
- **accountability** — responsibility for decisions and their results.
- **uneasiness** — a feeling of worry or discomfort.
- **resolve** — firm determination to do something.
- **agency** — the ability to make and act on one’s own choices.

## Conversation 1 — Jarvis Reaches His Limit

Jarvis: I cannot keep having the same conversation and ending up in the same place.
Anne: Then stop asking for another vague promise. Ask for a decision.
Jarvis: And if she chooses her father
Anne: That would hurt, but at least you would know where you stand.
Jarvis: I suppose uncertainty has been dragging this out long enough.

### Phrasal Verbs & Expressions

- **drag something out** — make a difficult situation continue longer than necessary.
- **know where you stand** — understand your position in a relationship or situation.
- **draw a line** — set a clear limit.
- **follow through on** — complete something one has promised or decided.
- **back out of** — withdraw from an agreement or plan.

## Conversation 2 — Dovie Wants Anne to Decide

Dovie: Please just tell me what to do. I trust your judgment more than mine right now.
Anne: I can help you think it through, but I cannot make the call for you.
Dovie: Every option feels awful.
Anne: Then ask which consequence you can live with and which choice reflects what you actually want.
Dovie: I want Jarvis, but I am terrified of what comes after.
Anne: Being afraid does not mean the decision is wrong. It means the decision matters.

### Phrasal Verbs & Expressions

- **think something through** — consider a problem carefully from beginning to end.
- **make the call** — make the final decision.
- **live with** — accept the consequences of something.
- **come after** — happen later as a consequence.
- **follow through** — carry out a decision after making it.
