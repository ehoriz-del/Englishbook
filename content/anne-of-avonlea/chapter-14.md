# Chapter 14 — A Danger Averted

## Story Summary

One Friday evening, Anne walks home from the post office with Mrs. Rachel Lynde. Mrs. Lynde talks about preparations for a church fair and old-fashioned supper. She also asks about Marilla’s eyesight. Anne says the specialist is pleased with the improvement and no longer expects Marilla to lose her sight completely, although she will never be able to do much reading or fine handwork.

Anne is carrying a letter from British Columbia that appears to be from Davy and Dora’s uncle. At Green Gables, she opens it with Marilla. The uncle says that he has been ill and his wedding has been postponed. He cannot take the twins in the spring as planned and asks whether Anne and Marilla can keep them until fall. Marilla agrees, and Anne is pleased. They both recognize that they have become used to having the children at Green Gables.

Davy, however, is still capable of getting into trouble. Anne recently found him secretly eating Marilla’s special yellow plum preserves, which he had been forbidden to touch. After Anne explains why taking the jam was wrong, Davy promises not to do it again. He then creates another confusion by misunderstanding a line in his catechism and thinking it says that God “makes preserves.” Anne has to explain how punctuation changes the meaning.

That evening, the Avonlea Village Improvement Society meets at Harmon Andrews’s house. The society has already made visible progress. Several roadside areas have been cleared and improved, and the triangle at the crossroads has been planted.

The meeting is interrupted by disturbing news. Gertie Pye reports that Judson Parker plans to rent the long roadside fence on his farm to a patent medicine company, which will cover it with advertisements. The members are horrified because the fence runs beside an important road through Avonlea and would damage the appearance of the area they have been trying to improve.

The society decides to send a committee to protest. Anne agrees to do most of the talking, while Diana and Jane go with her for support. Judson Parker listens politely and compliments them, but refuses to change his plan. He says business is business and even jokes that he will ask the company to use attractive colors.

Later, Anne happens to overhear Judson speaking with Jerry Corcoran about the coming election. The conversation makes it clear that Judson is willing to benefit from a political deal involving his vote. Anne says nothing about what she has heard.

Soon afterward, Judson approaches Anne. Worried that she might repeat the conversation, he tells her that he has decided not to rent the fence to the advertising company after all. He also asks her not to mention what she overheard. Anne makes clear that she never intended to use the information to bargain with him, but she is able to tell the Improvement Society that the advertising plan has been abandoned.

## Conversation 1 — The Twins Will Stay Longer

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Anne: We finally **heard back from** their uncle.
Marilla: And his wedding has been **put off**, so he can’t take the twins yet.
Anne: Then they’ll stay with us until fall.
Marilla: I suppose we’ve **gotten used to** having them here.
Anne: I think so too — even if Davy still manages to get into trouble.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- hear back from someone — receive a reply or response from someone. I emailed the office on Monday but haven’t heard back from them yet.
- put something off — postpone something until a later time. We had to put the meeting off until next week.
- get used to something — become familiar or comfortable with something after time. It took me a few weeks to get used to the new schedule.
- get into trouble — become involved in a situation that causes problems or punishment. He got into trouble for ignoring the safety rules.

## Conversation 2 — The Advertising Plan

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Jane: Judson says he’s going to **go through with** the advertising deal.
Anne: Then we have to **speak out against** it before the signs go up.
Diana: Do you think he’ll listen?
Anne: Probably not, but we can’t let money **stand in the way of** everything we’ve worked for.
Jane: I just hope he eventually **backs down**.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- go through with something — do something you planned or promised, especially when it is difficult or controversial. She decided to go through with the move despite her doubts.
- speak out against something — publicly express strong disagreement with something. Several residents spoke out against the proposed development.
- stand in the way of something — prevent or make it more difficult for something to happen. We shouldn’t let one setback stand in the way of progress.
- back down — stop opposing someone or abandon a position after pressure or reconsideration. The company initially refused, but eventually backed down.
