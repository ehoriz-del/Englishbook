# Chapter 23 — Miss Lavendar’s Romance

## Story Summary

By December, Anne has visited Echo Lodge many times. Sometimes Diana goes with her, but Anne also walks there alone. A warm friendship has grown between Anne and Miss Lavendar. Anne enjoys finding another person who understands imagination, while Miss Lavendar enjoys the energy and companionship that Anne and Diana bring into her quiet life.

One Friday afternoon, Anne decides to walk to Echo Lodge and stay overnight. Snow is beginning as she reaches the lane. Charlotta the Fourth has gone home because her mother is ill, so Miss Lavendar is especially glad to see Anne.

They spend the first part of the evening cooking, eating, laughing, and pretending that they are much younger than they really are. Later, when they are tired, they sit near the fire while snow strikes the windows. The quieter setting leads Miss Lavendar to speak openly about the past.

She tells Anne that she and Stephen Irving had known each other since childhood and had once been engaged. There was no great betrayal or dramatic event behind the end of their relationship. They simply quarreled. Miss Lavendar admits that she was vain, quick to take offense, and inclined to sulk. Stephen was sensitive and proud as well.

After the quarrel, Stephen returned sooner than Miss Lavendar wanted. She was still angry and refused to listen or forgive him. Stephen left again. When he did not come back, Miss Lavendar became angry about that too. Neither of them was willing to make the next move, and the relationship ended.

Miss Lavendar tells Anne that she never stopped caring for Stephen and never wanted to marry anyone else. She also explains that heartbreak in real life is not exactly like heartbreak in romantic stories. A person can be deeply hurt and still continue living, laughing, eating, working, and finding pleasure in ordinary days.

Miss Lavendar remembers that she and Stephen had expected to marry the following spring and that her wedding dress had already been made. They had known each other since childhood; when Stephen was nine and Miss Lavendar was six, he had already said he intended to marry her someday.

She also tells Anne that when she finally understood Stephen was not coming back, the pain was real, but life did not stop. She continued to enjoy food, friendship, humor, and ordinary pleasures. Anne listens closely because Miss Lavendar’s description is very different from the dramatic heartbreak Anne has often imagined from books.


Anne asks about Paul. Miss Lavendar admits that hearing Stephen Irving’s son mentioned during Anne’s first visit had affected her strongly. She has wanted to meet Paul but is afraid that seeing him may bring back painful memories.

Anne offers to bring Paul to Echo Lodge. Miss Lavendar agrees but asks Anne to wait a month so that she can get used to the idea.

When Anne finally brings Paul, Miss Lavendar is deeply moved because he resembles Stephen. For a moment, Anne worries that the meeting will be awkward. Instead, Miss Lavendar quickly regains control and treats Paul naturally.

Paul also feels comfortable. He likes Miss Lavendar, Charlotta the Fourth, the house, and the food. By the end of the visit, he asks Miss Lavendar to become one of his “particular friends” and allows her to kiss him goodbye. Anne walks home relieved that the meeting has gone so well.

## Conversation 1 — How a Small Quarrel Became a Long Separation

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Anne: I always imagined that something terrible must have happened between you and Stephen.
Miss Lavendar: No. We simply **fell out over** something small and let pride make it worse.
Anne: Did he try to **make things right** afterward?
Miss Lavendar: He came back once, but I was still angry and refused to listen.
Anne: Did you ever think about contacting him later?
Miss Lavendar: Many times, but I kept waiting for him to **reach out** first.
Anne: So neither of you made the next move.
Miss Lavendar: Exactly. Over time, we **drifted apart**, even though the feelings never completely disappeared.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- fall out over something — have an argument that damages a relationship. They fell out over a misunderstanding and stopped speaking.
- make things right — repair a problem or relationship after something has gone wrong. I apologized because I wanted to make things right.
- reach out — contact someone, especially to reconnect, offer help, or repair a relationship. She finally reached out after years of silence.
- drift apart — gradually become less close over time. We stayed friends for years but eventually drifted apart.

## Conversation 2 — Meeting Paul for the First Time

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Anne: Were you nervous before Paul arrived?
Miss Lavendar: Very. I wasn’t sure how I would **react to** seeing Stephen’s son.
Anne: I noticed that the resemblance surprised you.
Miss Lavendar: It did, but once Paul began talking, I **settled down**.
Anne: He seemed comfortable almost immediately.
Miss Lavendar: We really **took to each other**.
Anne: Are you glad you agreed to the visit?
Miss Lavendar: Very glad. I nearly **backed out of** it because I was afraid of how I would feel.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- react to something — respond emotionally or practically to something that happens. I wasn’t sure how she would react to the news.
- settle down — become calmer after being nervous, excited, or upset. I was anxious at first, but I settled down after a few minutes.
- take to each other — begin to like each other quickly. The two children took to each other right away.
- back out of something — decide not to do something you had already agreed or planned to do. He backed out of the trip at the last minute.
