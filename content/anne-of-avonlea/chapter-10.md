# Chapter 10 — Davy in Search of a Sensation

## Story Summary

One November afternoon, Anne walks home from school through the Birch Path in a cheerful mood. The day at school has gone unusually well, and Davy has also been relatively well behaved for nearly two weeks. Anne meets Diana and talks about how little time she now has for daydreaming because teaching, studying, and helping Marilla with the twins keep her busy.

When Anne reaches Green Gables, she immediately discovers that something is wrong. Marilla tells her that Dora is missing. Marilla had been called to the Lynde home because Thomas Lynde suddenly became ill. When she left, Dora was playing in the kitchen and Davy was outside. After returning, Marilla searched the house but could not find Dora anywhere.

Anne questions Davy. He insists that he has not seen Dora since dinner. Anne and Marilla search the house, yard, barns, orchards, and Haunted Wood. Davy joins the search and suggests several places where Dora might be.

Davy finally suggests that Dora may have fallen into the well. The possibility terrifies Anne and Marilla. Anne brings Mr. Barry, who searches the deep well with a rope and a metal tool. After checking carefully, he concludes that Dora is not there.

Marilla then wonders whether Dora might have gone to Mr. Harrison’s because she has been interested in his parrot. Anne crosses the fields to his house. Mr. Harrison is away, but while Anne calls for Dora, she hears a faint cry coming from his toolhouse.

Anne finds Dora locked inside, cold, hungry, frightened, and crying. Dora explains that she and Davy had gone to see Ginger. Davy later took her to the toolhouse, shut her inside, and ran away. Anne carries Dora home safely.

The situation becomes more serious when Anne realizes that Davy knew exactly where Dora was throughout the search and repeatedly said that he did not. Marilla eventually finds him hiding in the stable and brings him into the kitchen.

At first Davy says he created the whole situation because things had been too quiet and he wanted to give everyone a big scare. He does not understand why Anne is especially upset that he lied. When Anne begins to cry, Davy realizes how deeply he has hurt her and becomes genuinely sorry. He explains that the Sprott children regularly told lies and that nobody had taught him clearly that it was wrong.

Anne asks Marilla to forgive that part of the incident because Davy did not understand the rule. Marilla agrees that they will have to begin teaching him many things from the beginning. Davy is still punished for locking Dora in the toolhouse and is sent to bed.

Later, Davy asks Anne whether lying is wrong for adults too. He accuses Marilla of having told him that something dreadful would happen if he did not say his prayers. He had deliberately stopped praying for more than a week to see what would happen. Anne carefully explains that telling a lie itself is something bad that has happened to him.

Afterward, Anne and Marilla discuss Davy’s behavior. Marilla is discouraged, but Anne argues that Davy is mischievous rather than hopeless. They both admit that, despite all the trouble he causes, they have grown very fond of him.

## Conversation 1 — Dora Is Missing

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Marilla: We’ve **looked everywhere**, and I still can’t find her.
Anne: Let’s not panic. We’ll **check around** the barns and the orchard again.
Marilla: What if something serious has happened?
Anne: We need to **rule out** the obvious places first.
Marilla: I wish we could **track her down** quickly. She must be frightened.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- look everywhere — search in all possible places. I looked everywhere for my keys before finding them in my coat.
- check around — look in several nearby places or ask several people for information. I’ll check around and see whether anyone found your bag.
- rule something out — decide that something is not possible after checking the evidence. The technician ruled out a problem with the battery.
- track someone or something down — find someone or something after searching. It took us an hour to track down the missing document.

## Conversation 2 — Davy Tells the Truth

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Anne: Davy, you knew where Dora was and **made up** a story instead.
Davy: I only wanted to give everyone a scare.
Anne: But you **kept it from** us while we were searching.
Davy: I didn’t understand how serious it was.
Anne: You need to **learn from** what happened and tell the truth next time.
Davy: I will. I don’t want to **let you down** again.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- make something up — invent a story, explanation, or piece of information that is not true. He made up an excuse for being late.
- keep something from someone — deliberately not tell someone information. She kept the problem from her family for several weeks.
- learn from something — use an experience to improve future behavior or decisions. We made mistakes, but we learned from them.
- let someone down — disappoint someone by failing to do what they expected or needed. I promised to help, and I don’t want to let you down.
