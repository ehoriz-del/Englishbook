# Chapter 26 — Around the Bend

## Story Summary

Thomas Lynde becomes weaker and dies quietly after a long illness. During his final days, Mrs. Rachel Lynde cares for him patiently and faithfully. The morning after his death, Marilla wakes Anne early to tell her the news and then goes to help Mrs. Lynde.

After the funeral, Marilla seems unusually thoughtful. The next evening, after visiting Mrs. Lynde, she goes to Anne’s room while Anne is correcting school exercises. Marilla first asks whether Gilbert Blythe is going to college in the fall. Then, rather suddenly, she asks Anne how she would feel about going to Redmond College too.

Anne is astonished. She admits that she would love to go, but she immediately says it is impossible. She does not want to leave Marilla alone with Davy and Dora, and she believes the twins still need too much care.

Marilla then explains the idea she has been considering. Mrs. Lynde is not in a strong financial position after Thomas’s illness and the mortgage on their farm. The farm will have to be sold, and Mrs. Lynde expects that she may need to leave Avonlea and live with one of her children.

Marilla does not want that to happen. She has known Mrs. Lynde for forty-five years and says she would miss her badly. She proposes that Mrs. Lynde move into Green Gables instead. Mrs. Lynde could use the north gable as her bedroom and the spare room as a small private kitchen. She would still have her independence, while Marilla would have company and help with the twins.

If Mrs. Lynde accepts, Marilla says there is no longer any practical reason for Anne to give up college. Anne has already saved enough money for one year at Redmond, and other money can help with another year. Scholarships may also be possible later.

Anne is overwhelmed by the sudden change. She is deeply happy that college may now be possible, but she also knows that going away means leaving many things she has come to love during the past two years. She thinks especially about her school, her pupils, Green Gables, and the ordinary responsibilities that have become important to her.

The next day Anne sends in her resignation from the Avonlea school. Mrs. Lynde gratefully accepts Marilla’s offer, though she plans to stay in her own house until the farm is sold in the fall.

The new arrangement also becomes a favorite subject for Avonlea gossip. Some neighbors predict that Marilla and Mrs. Lynde will never manage to live peacefully under one roof because both women have strong opinions. Marilla and Mrs. Lynde are not worried. They agree clearly on their separate rooms and responsibilities before the move takes place.

News of Anne’s resignation spreads quickly. Her pupils are very upset. Annetta Bell cries, Barbara Shaw is miserable, Anthony Pye gets into fights, and Paul Irving tells his grandmother that he cannot even eat his usual porridge because he is so unhappy about losing his teacher.

Diana is sad too. She worries that Avonlea will feel empty when Anne, Gilbert, and the Allans are gone. One evening she asks Anne whether college and new friends will change their friendship. Anne assures her that no new friend could replace Diana. She is excited about the road ahead, but she knows that leaving Avonlea will be difficult.

## Conversation 1 — A New Possibility

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Marilla: I’ve been thinking about your plans for the fall.
Anne: I thought they were already **settled**. I’m staying here.
Marilla: They may not be. Mrs. Lynde could **move in with** me after her farm is sold.
Anne: Then you wouldn’t be alone with the twins?
Marilla: Exactly. She could help me **look after** them.
Anne: Are you saying college might still be possible?
Marilla: Yes. I don’t think you should **give up on** it just because you stayed home these two years.
Anne: I hardly know what to say. I had stopped expecting this.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- be settled — be decided or arranged so that no more uncertainty remains. We thought the travel plans were settled.
- move in with someone — begin living in the same home as someone. My aunt moved in with us for a few months.
- look after someone — take care of someone. She looks after her grandchildren after school.
- give up on something — stop believing that something will happen or succeed. Don’t give up on your plan too quickly.

## Conversation 2 — Leaving Something You Love

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Diana: I’m happy for you, but I’m going to miss you terribly.
Anne: I know. I’m excited, but part of me is finding it hard to **let go of** my life here.
Diana: You’ve **grown attached to** the school and the children.
Anne: Very much. I didn’t expect teaching to mean so much to me.
Diana: Are you worried that everything will change while you’re away?
Anne: A little. But I suppose I have to **move forward**.
Diana: And we’ll still write to each other.
Anne: Of course. Going away doesn’t mean I’m **leaving you behind**.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- let go of something — accept that you must leave or stop holding on to something. It was hard to let go of the old house.
- grow attached to someone or something — gradually develop strong affection for someone or something. She grew attached to the neighborhood.
- move forward — continue toward the future after a change or difficulty. We made a decision and now we need to move forward.
- leave someone behind — go somewhere without someone, or make someone feel excluded as you move on. She worried that her friends would leave her behind.
