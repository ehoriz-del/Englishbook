# Chapter 29 — Poetry and Prose

## Story Summary

During the next month, Anne becomes extremely busy. She has her own preparations for Redmond College, but Miss Lavendar’s approaching wedding takes even more of her attention. Echo Lodge is full of planning, dressmaking, decisions, and excitement.

Anne and Diana spend a great deal of time helping Miss Lavendar. They discuss her traveling dress, the gray silk dress she will wear, and many small details of the wedding. Charlotta the Fourth is delighted by everything that is happening and is especially excited because she will later go to Boston to live with Stephen and Miss Lavendar.

Paul Irving is also very happy. As soon as his father tells him about the engagement, Paul goes to Green Gables to talk to Anne. He says he knew he could trust his father to choose a good second mother. He loves Miss Lavendar and is pleased that she will become part of his family.

Anne explains to Marilla how the reunion happened. If Anne and Diana had not taken the wrong road months earlier, they would never have found Echo Lodge. If Anne had not become Miss Lavendar’s friend, she might never have taken Paul there. Paul then wrote about Miss Lavendar to his father at exactly the time Stephen was preparing for a business trip.

Stephen had believed for years that Miss Lavendar had married someone else. After reading Paul’s letter, he changed his plans, sent his business partner on the trip instead, and came home.

Marilla listens but says the story does not seem as romantic to her as it does to Anne. In Marilla’s plain version, two young people quarreled, Stephen went away, married someone else, became a widower, and later returned to see whether his first love still wanted him. Anne admits that the story sounds much less romantic when Marilla puts it that way.

The wedding is planned for the last Wednesday in August. Miss Lavendar and Stephen will marry under the honeysuckle trellis where he first proposed to her twenty-five years earlier. Only a small group of family and close friends will attend. Afterward, the couple will travel to the Pacific coast, then return to Boston. Paul and Charlotta will live with them there. Echo Lodge will remain closed during the winter, but they plan to return every summer.

While Anne is thinking about one romance, she unexpectedly discovers another. One evening she walks through the Barry garden and sees Diana and Fred Wright standing together under a large willow tree. Fred is holding Diana’s hand and speaking to her quietly.

Anne realizes that they are in love. The sight shocks her because it makes Diana suddenly seem much more grown up. Anne quietly leaves without being noticed and returns to Green Gables feeling strangely lonely.

The next evening Diana comes to tell Anne that she and Fred are engaged. Diana is very happy and asks Anne to be her bridesmaid when they marry. The wedding will not happen for at least three years because Diana’s mother will not allow her to marry before she is twenty-one, and Fred must also prepare financially.

Anne teases Diana a little about planning her future household, then apologizes when Diana is hurt. Later, Anne begins imagining her own future home and is surprised that Gilbert keeps appearing in the picture, even though he does not fit the romantic hero she has always imagined.

## Conversation 1 — How One Event Led to Another

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Anne: It’s strange how everything **worked out**.
Marilla: You mean Stephen and Miss Lavendar?
Anne: Yes. One wrong turn **led to** our meeting her, and that changed everything.
Marilla: You certainly **had a hand in** bringing them together again.
Anne: Paul’s letter mattered too. His father changed his travel plans after reading it.
Marilla: So several small events **added up to** one big result.
Anne: Exactly. None of us planned it that way.
Marilla: Sometimes things simply **fall into place**.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- work out — develop or end successfully. I wasn’t sure the plan would work out, but it did.
- lead to something — cause or produce a result. One conversation led to a new opportunity.
- have a hand in something — be partly responsible for making something happen. She had a hand in organizing the event.
- add up to something — combine to produce a particular result. Several small delays added up to a serious problem.
- fall into place — begin to happen or become organized naturally and successfully. Once we found a location, the rest of the plan fell into place.

## Conversation 2 — Diana’s News

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Diana: I wanted to tell you myself before the news **gets around**.
Anne: You and Fred are engaged, aren’t you?
Diana: Yes. We’ve been **seeing each other** more often this summer.
Anne: I knew you liked him, but I didn’t realize it had become so serious.
Diana: It happened gradually. We just **grew closer**.
Anne: And now you’re planning a wedding?
Diana: Not yet. We’re going to **hold off on** that for at least three years.
Anne: Then I have plenty of time to get used to having an engaged best friend.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- get around — spread from person to person, especially news. The news got around before lunch.
- see someone — spend time with someone romantically. They’ve been seeing each other for several months.
- grow closer — gradually develop a stronger relationship. We grew closer after working together.
- hold off on something — delay doing something until later. We decided to hold off on buying a house.
