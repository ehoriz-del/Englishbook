# Chapter 8 — Marilla Adopts Twins

## Story Summary

Late in the autumn, Marilla returns from Mary Keith’s funeral with Davy and Dora. Mrs. Rachel Lynde watches the wagon pass and immediately notices the contrast between the twins. Dora sits quietly and neatly, while Davy moves constantly and nearly gives Marilla a fright several times during the ten-mile trip from East Grafton.

Davy has difficulty sitting still, but he is affectionate and quickly hugs Marilla whenever she threatens to punish him. He talks openly about his mother’s death and says she told him to be good, to be kind to Dora, and to protect her. His idea of kindness is imperfect, however, because he pulls Dora’s hair almost immediately afterward.

Anne welcomes the children at Green Gables. At supper, Dora behaves politely, but Davy eats quickly, asks for more cake, and steals Dora’s piece after Marilla refuses to give him a third serving. Anne tells him that a gentleman would not behave that way. Davy replies that he plans to become a gentleman when he grows up, not before.

The twins look quite different despite being the same age. Dora is quiet, tidy, and obedient. Davy is lively, curious, affectionate, and almost constantly involved in some kind of mischief. During their first week at Green Gables, Marilla and Anne discover how much attention he requires.

Two days after his arrival, Davy goes to church with Anne and Marilla. Anne persuades him to wash and dress properly by mentioning that Paul Irving behaves well. Davy has already become jealous of Paul because Anne praises him so often. During the service, Davy finds a caterpillar and drops it down Lauretta White’s neck. Lauretta screams in the middle of Mr. Allan’s prayer, creating a disturbance throughout the church.

Marilla punishes Davy by sending him to bed for the rest of the day and limiting his food. When Anne speaks with him, Davy admits that Paul Irving probably would not have done such a thing and says he is somewhat sorry, although he still remembers the incident as exciting.

A few days later, the Aid Society meets at Green Gables. Marilla allows Davy to make mud pies outside because she hopes it will keep him occupied. Davy persuades Dora to walk along the pigpen fence by calling her afraid. Dora falls into the pigpen. Davy then tries to wash her clothes by making her stand under the pump while he pours water over her. She returns to the house wet, dirty, and crying.

After Anne talks seriously with him, Davy admits another piece of mischief: he has put a toad in Marilla’s bed. Anne quietly removes it with a fire shovel before Marilla discovers it. By the end of the chapter, Anne is exhausted by the combination of Anthony Pye at school and Davy at home.

## Conversation 1 — Life with Davy

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Marilla: I can’t leave that boy alone for five minutes. He’s always **getting into** something.
Anne: He has a lot of energy. It takes him a while to **settle down**.
Marilla: Dora never causes this kind of trouble.
Anne: Davy also **looks up to** people he admires. Mentioning Paul sometimes helps.
Marilla: I just hope he doesn’t think he can **get away with** everything because he’s charming.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- get into something — become involved in something, often trouble or mischief in this context. The children got into trouble while the adults were outside.
- settle down — become calmer, quieter, or more controlled. It took the class several minutes to settle down after lunch.
- look up to someone — admire and respect someone. She has always looked up to her older sister.
- get away with something — do something wrong without being caught or punished. He thought he could get away with copying the report.

## Conversation 2 — Another Problem

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Anne: Davy, you need to **clean up** the mess you made outside.
Davy: I didn’t mean for Dora to fall.
Anne: You still have to **take responsibility for** what happened.
Davy: Are you going to tell Marilla everything?
Anne: First, you need to **calm down** and tell me exactly what you did.
Davy: All right. There’s one more thing I should tell you.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- clean up — make a place clean or remove a mess. We need to clean up the kitchen after dinner.
- take responsibility for — accept that you are responsible for an action, decision, or result. The company took responsibility for the error.
- calm down — become less upset, excited, or angry. Give him a few minutes to calm down before you talk to him.
- tell someone exactly what happened — give a clear and complete account of an event. The manager asked us to tell her exactly what happened.
