# Chapter 18 — An Adventure on the Tory Road

## Story Summary

Anne is still worried about Miss Barry’s broken blue willow platter when Diana sends an urgent signal from her bedroom window. Anne hurries to Orchard Slope and learns that Diana may have found a solution.

A woman in Carmody has told Diana that Sarah and Martha Copp, two elderly sisters who live on the Tory Road near Spencervale, own a blue willow platter that may be exactly like Miss Barry’s. If theirs is not the right one, another family in Spencervale may have a similar platter.

Anne decides to go the next day, and Diana comes with her. The journey is about ten miles. The weather is hot and windless, and the roads are dusty after weeks without rain.

When they finally reach the Copp home, everything looks extremely neat, but the window shades are down and nobody answers the door. Anne is frustrated because she does not want to wait for hours only to discover that the platter is the wrong kind.

Diana notices a small pantry window without a shade. She suggests that Anne climb onto the roof of an old duck house beside the wall and look through the window. Anne agrees because their purpose is only to identify the platter.

Anne carefully climbs up and sees exactly what she hoped to find: a blue willow platter that appears to match Miss Barry’s. In her excitement, however, she shifts her weight too quickly and crashes through the weak roof of the duck house.

Anne becomes firmly stuck. Her upper body remains above the roof while the rest of her is trapped inside. Diana tries to pull her free but cannot do so without hurting her.

They decide to wait for the Copp sisters to return. Before long, thunder begins in the distance and a heavy rainstorm moves in. Diana protects Anne with a parasol and takes shelter nearby.

Even while trapped in the roof, Anne finds something to occupy her mind. She imagines a conversation among the flowers in her garden and writes parts of it down on paper Diana brings from the buggy.

After the storm passes, Sarah Copp finally arrives. Once Diana explains the situation, Sarah gets an axe and cuts away enough of the old roof to free Anne. She is not upset about the damage because she has wanted the duck house removed for years.

Anne explains the reason for their visit and offers twenty dollars for the platter. Sarah asks twenty-five. Diana quietly signals Anne to bargain, but Anne is unwilling to risk losing the only suitable replacement she has found and agrees to the higher price.

Sarah then reveals that she needs money because she plans to marry Luther Wallace, a man she had wanted to marry many years earlier. Anne and Diana leave with the platter safely in Anne’s lap, relieved that their long, uncomfortable adventure has finally produced the result they needed.

## Conversation 1 — Searching for a Replacement

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Diana: We’ve spent days trying to **track down** a platter that matches the original.
Anne: If the Copps really have one, the trip will be worth it.
Diana: Since we’re already here, we should **check the place out** carefully before going anywhere else.
Anne: Agreed. If it isn’t the right one, we’ll **head over to** the second address.
Diana: I just hope we don’t **come all this way for nothing**.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- track something down — find something after making a deliberate effort to search for it. It took several calls to track down the missing document.
- check something out — examine or investigate something to learn more about it. We should check the apartment out before signing the lease.
- head over to — go to a particular place. I’m heading over to the main office after lunch.
- come all this way — travel a considerable distance or make a significant effort to reach a point. We’ve come all this way, so we might as well stay for the whole event.

## Conversation 2 — Waiting Out the Storm

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Anne: I can’t move without making the roof worse. I’m completely **stuck**.
Diana: If nobody comes soon, I’ll have to **go for help**.
Anne: Let’s **wait the storm out** first. It’s too dangerous to start walking now.
Diana: Fair enough. And when Sarah comes back, don’t accept the first price she gives you.
Anne: I know you want me to **hold out for** a better deal, but I’m not risking that platter.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- be stuck — be unable to move or unable to make progress in a situation. We were stuck in traffic for nearly two hours.
- go for help — leave in order to find someone who can help. Stay here while I go for help.
- wait something out — remain patient until a difficult or unpleasant situation ends. We waited the storm out inside a café.
- hold out for something — refuse to accept less than what you want or expect. They turned down the first offer and held out for a better price.
