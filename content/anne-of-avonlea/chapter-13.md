# Chapter 13 — A Golden Picnic

## Story Summary

In spring, Anne meets Diana near the brook below the Haunted Wood. Anne wants to celebrate her birthday even though it was actually in March. She says that if she had been able to choose, she would have preferred to be born in spring. Priscilla is coming to Avonlea for the weekend, Jane will be home, and Anne proposes that the four friends spend Saturday exploring the woods and fields.

Anne plans a picnic lunch that she thinks will suit the season, including small cakes, cookies, tarts, and sandwiches. Saturday arrives warm, bright, and breezy. Anne, Diana, Jane, and Priscilla set out with their baskets and decide that they will make the day memorable by looking for beautiful places and leaving school worries behind.

As they walk through the woods, they discover a narrow path and follow it to a shallow woodland pool surrounded by young birch trees and ferns. Anne wants to give the place a poetic name. Each girl suggests one, and they draw a name from Anne’s hat. Jane’s practical suggestion, “Crystal Lake,” is chosen, even though Anne would have preferred something more imaginative.

The girls continue through Mr. Sloane’s back pasture and into a lane lined with flowering wild cherry trees. They talk about what they would wish for if they could have one wish. Jane would choose wealth, beauty, and intelligence. Diana wants to be tall and slender. Priscilla wants to be famous. Anne says she would wish for spring to remain in people’s hearts and lives.

After climbing a steep hill, they discover an old, neglected garden surrounded by a broken stone wall. The garden is filled with blooming narcissi and flowering cherry trees. Diana recognizes it as Hester Gray’s garden and tells the others the story she has heard.

Hester Gray had lived there with her husband, Jordan. She was not strong and gradually became seriously ill. Jordan cared for her and often carried her into the garden she loved. Hester had hoped that when her life ended, she would be outside among her flowers, and that is what happened. The old house is gone, but the garden has continued to bloom for many years.

The girls spend the rest of the day in and around the garden. They eat their picnic, talk, explore, and gather narcissi. On the way home, all four agree that the day has been special. The next day, Anne takes some of the flowers from Hester’s garden to the cemetery and places them on Hester Gray’s grave.

## Conversation 1 — A Day in the Woods

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Diana: I’m glad we **set off** early. We have the whole day ahead of us.
Anne: And no school problems today. We’re going to **make a day of it**.
Priscilla: I’d like to **look around** places we usually pass without noticing.
Jane: Fine — as long as we don’t **wander off** so far that we can’t find our way back.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- set off — begin a journey or trip. We set off before sunrise to avoid the traffic.
- make a day of it — spend most or all of a day enjoying a particular activity or place. Since the museum is two hours away, let’s make a day of it.
- look around — examine or explore a place by looking at different parts of it. We had an hour to look around the old town.
- wander off — move away from a place or group without a clear plan or without noticing. Don’t wander off while we are waiting for the tour to begin.

## Conversation 2 — A Place Worth Remembering

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Priscilla: I can’t believe we **came upon** this garden by accident.
Anne: I want to **take it all in** before we leave.
Diana: We’ll probably **look back on** today for years.
Jane: Especially if we bring enough flowers home to remind us of it.
Anne: I’m glad we didn’t **rush through** the afternoon.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- come upon — find or meet something unexpectedly. We came upon a quiet beach while exploring the coast.
- take something in — look at, listen to, or experience something carefully so you can fully appreciate or understand it. We stood quietly for a moment and took in the view.
- look back on — remember and think about a past event or period. I look back on that trip as one of my favorite vacations.
- rush through — do something too quickly, often without giving it enough attention. Don’t rush through the instructions; read them carefully.
