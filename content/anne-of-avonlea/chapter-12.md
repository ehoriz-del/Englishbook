# Chapter 12 — A Jonah Day

## Story Summary

Anne’s bad day begins the night before with a painful toothache that keeps her awake. The next morning is bitterly cold, her face is swollen, and she goes to school already tired and irritable. The schoolroom is cold and smoky because the fire will not burn properly, and the children crowd around the stove.

Anne speaks more sharply than usual. When Barbara Shaw trips over the coal scuttle, scatters coal across the room, breaks her slate, and gets coal dust on her face, the other children laugh. Instead of comforting Barbara, Anne criticizes her for being awkward. Barbara is hurt because Anne has never spoken to her that way before.

St. Clair Donnell arrives half an hour late because he had to help his mother prepare food for company. Anne gives him arithmetic problems as punishment. A little later, she sees him pass a small blue-and-white parcel to Joe Sloane. Anne has recently forbidden the boys to bring Mrs. Hiram Sloane’s nut cakes into class, so she immediately assumes the parcel contains cakes.

Anne orders Joe to throw the package into the stove. Joe tries to explain, but Anne refuses to listen. The parcel actually contains firecrackers and pinwheels that Warren Sloane planned to use for a birthday celebration. The fireworks explode inside the stove, filling the schoolroom with smoke and noise. Some pupils scream, Prillie Rogerson faints, and Annetta Bell becomes hysterical.

Anne restores order, but she is deeply embarrassed and even more impatient for the rest of the morning. When she returns after dinner, she opens her desk drawer and a live mouse jumps out. Anne screams, and Anthony Pye laughs. She asks who put the mouse there, and Anthony admits that he did.

Already angry and humiliated, Anne loses her temper. She takes the heavy classroom pointer and strikes Anthony. She stops when she sees tears in his eyes and immediately regrets what she has done. She had repeatedly promised herself that she would not use physical punishment, and now she believes she has destroyed any chance of winning Anthony’s respect or affection.

At Green Gables that evening, Anne cries in the east gable room and tells Marilla about the entire day. Marilla does not consider the punishment a disaster and reminds Anne that everyone makes mistakes. She tells her that the day is finished and that tomorrow will be new.

During the night, snow covers Avonlea. On the way to school the next morning, Anne meets Anthony. To her surprise, he politely lifts his cap and offers to carry her books. Anne gives them to him, and the two walk to school together. Later, Mrs. Rachel Lynde tells Anne that Anthony now believes she is “some good” after all. Anne is surprised that the punishment she regrets has changed Anthony’s attitude toward her.

## Conversation 1 — A Bad Morning

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Marilla: You were tired and in pain, but you shouldn’t **take it out on** the children.
Anne: I know. I also **jumped to conclusions** about that package.
Marilla: You didn’t let Joe explain before you threw it into the stove.
Anne: And then everything **went off** at once — literally.
Marilla: Next time, try to **slow down** before you react.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- take something out on someone — treat someone badly because you are upset about something else. I had a terrible day, but I shouldn’t have taken it out on you.
- jump to conclusions — decide what happened before you have enough information. Don’t jump to conclusions until we hear both sides.
- go off — explode or suddenly make a loud noise, especially a device such as an alarm or firework. The smoke alarm went off in the middle of the night.
- slow down — reduce your speed or take more time before acting. Slow down and read the instructions before you make a decision.

## Conversation 2 — The Next Morning

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Anne: I still **feel bad about** losing my temper yesterday.
Marilla: You can regret it without letting it ruin today.
Anne: I suppose I need to **put it behind me** and do better.
Marilla: Exactly. And Anthony may **come around** sooner than you think.
Anne: If he gives me another chance, I want to **start fresh**.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- feel bad about something — feel sorry, guilty, or unhappy about something that happened. I still feel bad about missing her birthday.
- put something behind you — stop allowing a past problem or mistake to affect you. The argument is over, so let’s put it behind us.
- come around — gradually change your opinion or attitude. He disliked the idea at first, but eventually he came around.
- start fresh — begin again without allowing earlier mistakes or problems to control the new attempt. The first plan failed, so we decided to start fresh.
