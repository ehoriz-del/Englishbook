# Chapter 30 — A Wedding at the Stone House

## Story Summary

The last week of August arrives, bringing several changes at once. Miss Lavendar’s wedding is only a day away. Anne and Gilbert will leave for Redmond College in two weeks. Mrs. Rachel Lynde will soon move into Green Gables, and Mr. and Mrs. Allan are preparing to leave Avonlea because Mr. Allan has accepted a call to Charlottetown.

Anne feels both happy and sad as so many familiar parts of life begin to change. Before going to Echo Lodge, she visits the Harrisons to ask for yellow dahlias for the wedding decorations. Flowers are still less plentiful than usual because of the damage from Uncle Abe’s storm.

Mr. Harrison asks Anne about college. Anne says she is glad to be going, but she is also sorry to leave people she loves. She explains that she no longer cares as much about winning honors as she once did. What she wants from college now is a better understanding of how to live well and how to understand and help other people.

After tea, Anne and Diana drive to Echo Lodge with flowers from several gardens. The stone house is full of activity. Charlotta the Fourth is rushing from one job to another and worrying that something will go wrong. Anne and Diana work until late, helping with decorations, food, silver, packing, and other preparations.

The next morning looks gray and threatening. Charlotta wakes Anne very early because she is afraid it will rain and ruin the outdoor ceremony. Anne tries to stay hopeful. The rain never actually begins.

Even though the sky remains cloudy, the preparations continue. Anne and Diana arrange flowers through the stone house, and Charlotta changes into a stiff white dress with bright blue ribbons. Everyone is nervous about the weather, but the ceremony remains planned for the garden.

By noon the rooms are ready and the table is set. Miss Lavendar dresses for the wedding. Anne and Diana tell her she looks lovely. The wedding is small and informal. Mr. and Mrs. Allan are there, along with Paul, Mrs. Irving, Gilbert, Diana, Anne, Charlotta the Fourth, and several of Miss Lavendar’s relatives.

Miss Lavendar comes downstairs to meet Stephen at the foot of the stairs. Together they walk into the garden, where Mr. Allan is waiting under the honeysuckle arbor.

The guests stand wherever they wish. Anne and Diana remain near the old stone bench with Charlotta between them. Mr. Allan begins the ceremony.

At the exact moment Stephen and Miss Lavendar are pronounced husband and wife, the sun suddenly breaks through the gray clouds. Light pours into the garden. Anne is delighted by the timing.

After the ceremony, Anne, Diana, and Charlotta hurry inside to make sure the meal is ready. Charlotta is relieved that the marriage is safely completed, no matter what else may happen.

At half past two, Stephen and Miss Lavendar leave Echo Lodge. The guests throw rice, and Charlotta throws an old shoe with such poor aim that it hits Mr. Allan instead.

Paul gives the final farewell. He rings a large old brass dinner bell from the porch. As the sound travels across the river, the echoes answer from the hills and curves beyond the stone house.

The echoes sound to Anne like distant wedding bells. Miss Lavendar leaves Echo Lodge with Stephen for the next part of her life, while the house remains behind to wait for their summer visits.

## Conversation 1 — Preparing for a Big Day

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Charlotta: There’s still so much to do before tomorrow.
Anne: We’ll **get through** it if we work one thing at a time.
Charlotta: I’m worried the weather will **turn against us**.
Diana: We can’t control that, so there’s no point worrying all night.
Anne: Exactly. Let’s **focus on** what we can actually finish.
Charlotta: The silver still needs polishing, and the cake isn’t ready.
Diana: Then we’ll **take care of** those first.
Anne: By the time we go to bed, most of this will be done.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- get through something — complete or survive something difficult or demanding. We have a lot of work, but we’ll get through it.
- turn against someone or something — change in a way that creates difficulty or opposition. The weather turned against us halfway through the trip.
- focus on something — give your main attention to something. Let’s focus on the most urgent task.
- take care of something — deal with or complete a task or responsibility. I’ll take care of the reservations.

## Conversation 2 — Leaving After the Wedding

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Anne: It feels strange to see Miss Lavendar **move on to** such a different life.
Diana: She looks very happy.
Anne: I’m happy for her too, but I’ll miss having her nearby.
Diana: At least she and Stephen plan to **come back** every summer.
Anne: That makes it easier. Echo Lodge won’t be gone from our lives completely.
Diana: And you’ll soon be **heading off to** college yourself.
Anne: I know. Everyone seems to be starting something new.
Diana: We’ll all have to **keep in touch** while things change.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- move on to something — begin a new stage, activity, or part of life. After two years in the role, she moved on to a new position.
- come back — return to a place. We hope to come back next summer.
- head off to — leave in order to go somewhere. She’s heading off to college in September.
- keep in touch — continue communicating with someone. We promised to keep in touch after graduation.
