# Chapter 2 — Selling in Haste and Repenting at Leisure

## Story Summary

The next afternoon, Anne drives to Carmody to do some shopping, and Diana goes with her. On the way, the girls talk eagerly about the Avonlea Village Improvement Society. Diana suggests practical changes, such as repainting the hall and tidying neglected places around the village. Anne agrees, though she knows they will have to begin modestly and win people over little by little.

One idea especially appeals to them: a small piece of land where three roads meet could be cleared, furnished with a bench, and planted with flowers. They also discuss the deserted Boulter house, which Anne immediately fills with imagined memories and past happiness. Diana, being more practical, reminds her that the people who once lived there are now ordinary grown-ups with families of their own.

After a thunderstorm, the girls start back toward Green Gables. The fields and trees look fresh and beautiful in the clear light after the rain, but Anne’s pleasure vanishes the moment she notices a Jersey cow standing in Mr. Harrison’s oat field. Since she had promised only the day before that Dolly would never get in there again, Anne is horrified.

She and Diana climb out at once and chase the cow through the wet field. The animal keeps dodging them, and the pursuit turns into an exhausting, muddy struggle. At last they manage to drive her out onto the road. Just then Mr. Shearer appears with his son and repeats his earlier offer to buy Dolly.

Still flustered and embarrassed, Anne makes a rash decision. She agrees to sell the cow on the spot for twenty dollars. Mr. Shearer accepts, and his son takes the animal away to be shipped that very afternoon. Diana is doubtful, but Anne insists that the sale is reasonable because Dolly belongs to her and probably would not bring much more at auction.

Back at Green Gables, Marilla is out, and Anne has time to think the matter over. When Marilla returns, she says that perhaps it is just as well, although Anne should not have acted so quickly. Anne then goes to inspect the pen because she cannot understand how Dolly escaped in the first place.

There she makes a dreadful discovery: Dolly is still in the pen. In a moment Anne understands everything. The Jersey cow she and Diana chased from Mr. Harrison’s oats was not Dolly at all. It was Mr. Harrison’s own cow, recently bought with the farm, and Anne has accidentally sold it to Mr. Shearer.

Horrified, Anne rushes back into the house. Marilla tells her there is only one sensible course: go to Mr. Harrison, explain the whole matter, and offer either the twenty dollars or Dolly in exchange. Though Marilla offers to speak to him herself, Anne refuses. Since the blunder was hers, she feels bound to face him in person.

Before setting out, Anne notices a nut cake she had baked for the Improvement Society. Remembering that Mr. Harrison lives alone, she decides to take it along as a peace offering. Carrying the cake and the money, she crosses the fields toward his house, determined to explain everything.

## Conversation 1 — After Chasing the Cow

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Diana: Anne, you’re soaked. Can we slow down for a second?
Anne: Not yet. We have to **get this sorted out** before Mr. Harrison sees the field.
Diana: You can’t control everything that cow does.
Anne: I know, but I gave him my word. I don’t want to **go back on** it.
Diana: At least we finally **got her out**.
Anne: Yes, and if Mr. Shearer still wants her, I may **take him up on** that offer.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- get something sorted out — deal with a problem successfully so that the situation becomes clear or under control. Give me a few minutes to get this billing problem sorted out.
- go back on your word — fail to do something you promised to do. I told her I would help, and I don’t want to go back on my word.
- get someone or something out — cause a person or thing to leave a place. We finally got the dog out of the flower bed.
- take someone up on an offer — accept an offer or invitation that someone has made. If the offer still stands, I’d like to take you up on it.

## Conversation 2 — Anne Realizes Her Mistake

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Anne: Marilla, I really **messed up**.
Marilla: What happened?
Anne: Dolly is still in the pen. I sold Mr. Harrison’s cow.
Marilla: Then you’ll have to **own up to** it and explain what happened.
Anne: I know. I should have **thought it through** before I sold her.
Marilla: The longer you wait, the harder it will be.
Anne: I’ll go now. I need to **make it right** myself.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- mess up — make a mistake or cause something to go wrong. I messed up the reservation and booked the wrong date.
- own up to something — admit that you did something wrong or made a mistake. He eventually owned up to forgetting to send the email.
- think something through — consider a situation carefully before deciding what to do. Let’s think this through before we make a final decision.
- make it right — correct a mistake or unfair situation. The company sent the wrong order, but they promised to make it right.
