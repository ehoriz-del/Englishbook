# Chapter 17 — A Chapter of Accidents

## Story Summary

Anne is so excited about Mrs. Morgan’s visit that she wakes several times during the night to check the weather. The next morning is clear and beautiful, and Diana comes to Green Gables early to help with the final preparations.

Together they fill the parlor with flowers and arrange the dining table with Marilla’s best linen, china, glass, and silver. The meal has been carefully planned, and Anne wants everything to be ready before the guests arrive.

Davy has promised to behave. For a while, he sits quietly in the kitchen working with an old fishing net. Later, however, he climbs onto the pantry table to put away a ball of twine. Marilla has forbidden him to climb there because he has fallen before.

Davy loses his balance and lands directly on the two lemon pies Anne prepared for dessert. The pies are ruined. Marilla sends him upstairs, while Anne and Diana quickly decide to serve strawberry preserves and whipped cream instead.

Mr. and Mrs. Allan and Miss Stacy arrive, but Priscilla and Mrs. Morgan do not. As the planned dinner time passes, Anne repeatedly looks down the lane. She becomes increasingly anxious that something has happened.

While they wait, Miss Stacy asks to see Miss Barry’s valuable blue willow platter. Anne has borrowed it for an earlier church event and promised to take great care of it. After showing the platter to the guests, Anne places it temporarily on the second stair when a loud crash comes from the pantry.

At half past one, Marilla says they can wait no longer and the meal must be served. Anne and Diana try to hide their disappointment. Then Diana tastes the peas and discovers another problem: Anne, Diana, and Marilla have each added a spoonful of sugar because each assumed the others would forget. The peas are far too sweet to serve.

The dinner is not the success Anne imagined. The guests are kind, but Anne and Diana are too tired and disappointed to enjoy themselves fully.

As everyone is leaving, another crash comes from the staircase. Davy has accidentally knocked a large conch shell down the stairs, and it has struck Miss Barry’s platter. The valuable platter lies broken into pieces.

Anne knows she was responsible for leaving it in such a dangerous place. She immediately decides that she must replace it or repay Miss Barry.

Later, Marilla brings home a letter from Priscilla explaining that Mrs. Morgan badly sprained her ankle and could not make the trip. After all Anne’s careful preparation, the visit has fallen through completely, and she is left with a ruined dessert, an unsuccessful dinner, and a broken platter to deal with.

## Conversation 1 — When the Plan Falls Apart

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Diana: We prepared for every possible detail, and somehow everything still **went wrong**.
Anne: I know. Once the pies were ruined, we had to **make do with** whatever we had.
Diana: And waiting so long for Mrs. Morgan made the whole afternoon harder.
Anne: I tried to **put on a brave face**, but I was terribly disappointed.
Diana: At least the guests were kind about it.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- go wrong — develop a problem or fail to happen as planned. Several things went wrong during the event.
- make do with — manage using what is available even though it is not ideal. The printer broke, so we had to make do with handwritten signs.
- put on a brave face — appear calm or cheerful even though you are worried, disappointed, or upset. She put on a brave face during the meeting even though the news was difficult.
- fall apart — stop working successfully or lose organization and control. The plan began to fall apart when two people canceled.

## Conversation 2 — The Broken Platter

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Anne: The platter never should have been left on the stairs. I have to **take responsibility for** that.
Marilla: It was an accident, but yes, you’ll have to **sort it out**.
Anne: I’m going to find a replacement if I can.
Marilla: And if you can’t?
Anne: Then I’ll **make up for** the loss by paying Miss Barry back.
Marilla: That’s the sensible way to **deal with** it.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- take responsibility for something — accept that you are responsible for an action, mistake, or result. The company took responsibility for the error.
- sort something out — solve a problem or organize a confusing situation. We need to sort out the payment issue before the order ships.
- make up for something — do something that compensates for a mistake, loss, or disappointment. I worked late to make up for the time I had missed.
- deal with something — take action to handle a problem or difficult situation. She dealt with the complaint calmly and professionally.
