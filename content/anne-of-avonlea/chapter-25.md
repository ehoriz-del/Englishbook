# Chapter 25 — An Avonlea Scandal

## Story Summary

Two weeks after Uncle Abe’s storm, Avonlea is still recovering. Farmers have replanted some crops, and new growth is beginning to appear, but many flowers and trees were badly damaged. Anne is especially sorry that the white narcissi she usually takes to Matthew’s grave have been destroyed. She also visits Hester Gray’s old garden and finds that its narcissi are gone too.

One bright June morning, Anne is standing near the Green Gables gate when an express wagon comes up the lane. A small, neatly dressed woman gets out and asks where James A. Harrison lives. Anne points toward his house.

The woman then asks whether it is true that Mr. Harrison is planning to marry a woman in Avonlea. Anne quickly says no and explains that the newspaper item suggesting such a marriage was only a joke.

The stranger says she is glad to hear that because James Harrison is already married. She is his wife, Emily Harrison.

Anne is completely astonished. Everyone in Avonlea has assumed that Mr. Harrison is a bachelor. Recently, people had even connected him with Isabella Andrews because he often visited her brother to play checkers.

Emily also asks about Ginger. When Anne says the parrot is dead, Emily announces that everything may now be all right and continues toward Mr. Harrison’s house.

Anne tells Marilla what has happened. Marilla is equally surprised. Anne points out that Mr. Harrison never actually claimed to be unmarried; the neighbors simply took it for granted.

Mrs. Rachel Lynde soon hears the news and immediately goes to investigate. By Monday, several different versions of the story are circulating around Avonlea. Davy comes home repeating rumors that Emily left because Mr. Harrison smoked, because he had poor manners, or because she was always correcting him.

Mr. Harrison later asks Anne to visit. His kitchen is almost unrecognizable: the floor has been scrubbed, the stove polished, the walls cleaned, and his clothes repaired. Emily has also become friendly with Mrs. Lynde.

Mr. Harrison tells Anne the real story. Before coming to Prince Edward Island, he lived in New Brunswick. After his sister died, he married Emily Scott. She was a capable housekeeper and had been a schoolteacher.

Their marriage soon became difficult. Emily wanted everything extremely clean and tried to change many of James’s habits. She objected to his smoking, his grammar, his table manners, and his general untidiness. James admits that he responded by becoming stubborn and disagreeable instead of trying to compromise.

Their biggest conflict involved Ginger. The parrot had belonged to James’s brother, so James was deeply attached to it. Emily hated Ginger’s rude language.

The final quarrel came after Ginger swore loudly while two ministers and their wives were visiting for tea. Emily left a note saying James had to choose between her and the parrot. Angry and proud, James refused to follow her and later moved to Prince Edward Island.

After Ginger died, Emily received the newspaper clipping suggesting that James might marry someone else. She came to Avonlea herself. When James returned home and found her there, they finally talked properly.

They decide to live together again. James admits that both of them were partly responsible for their separation. Emily is grateful to the unknown “Observer” whose newspaper note brought her back. Anne does not reveal that she and Gilbert wrote it.

## Conversation 1 — A Surprise Nobody Expected

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Anne: Everyone in Avonlea **took it for granted** that Mr. Harrison was single.
Emily: Apparently nobody ever **asked him outright**.
Anne: That’s true. He never actually said he was unmarried.
Emily: And now the marriage rumor has **turned out to be** useful, even though it was false.
Anne: I imagine the real story will **get around** very quickly.
Emily: In a place this small, I’d be surprised if it hasn’t already.
Anne: People will probably add details that never happened.
Emily: Then perhaps James and I should tell the truth before the rumors grow.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- take something for granted — assume something is true without checking it. We took it for granted that the meeting was still scheduled.
- ask someone outright — ask someone directly and clearly. I finally asked him outright whether he planned to resign.
- turn out to be — eventually prove to be a particular thing or result. The rumor turned out to be false.
- get around — spread from person to person, especially news or information. News travels fast here; it will get around by tonight.

## Conversation 2 — Repairing a Relationship

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Mr. Harrison: Emily and I **fell out over** more things than I care to admit.
Anne: Did either of you try to **work things out** after she left?
Mr. Harrison: Not really. We were both waiting for the other person to change first.
Anne: So neither of you wanted to **give in**.
Mr. Harrison: Exactly. Pride made the whole problem last much longer than it should have.
Anne: What changed when she came back?
Mr. Harrison: We finally talked without trying to win the argument.
Anne: And now you’re willing to **start over**?
Mr. Harrison: Yes. We know each other’s faults better this time.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- fall out over something — have an argument that damages a relationship. They fell out over money and stopped speaking.
- work things out — solve a problem or repair a difficult relationship through discussion and effort. We disagreed badly, but we eventually worked things out.
- give in — stop resisting and accept another person’s position or request. Neither side was willing to give in.
- start over — begin again after a failure or difficult period. The first plan failed, so we decided to start over.
