# Chapter 20 — The Way It Often Happens

## Story Summary

The morning after Anne’s peaceful day with Paul and Gilbert, Marilla and Dora leave for town with Mrs. Lynde. Davy goes to Mr. Harrison’s, and Anne stays at Green Gables to finish several household jobs.

One task she has been postponing is transferring feathers from an old bed ticking into a new one. Anne puts on an old dress, covers her hair with a large handkerchief, and begins the messy work.

While passing a mirror, she notices the freckles on her nose. She remembers a homemade freckle lotion she has recently made and goes into the dark pantry to apply it.

Because the pantry is dim, Anne takes the wrong bottle. Instead of the freckle lotion, she spreads Marilla’s red dye over her nose without realizing it.

After working with the feathers for some time, Anne is covered in white down. At that exact moment, someone knocks at the kitchen door. She assumes the visitor is a local man coming to sell meat and answers the door without changing.

Anne is stunned to find Priscilla standing there with Mrs. Charlotte Morgan and Mrs. Pendexter. Mrs. Morgan’s earlier visit had been canceled because of her injured ankle, but her plans have changed and she has come to Green Gables unexpectedly.

Although Anne is embarrassed by her old dress and the feathers covering her, she controls herself and welcomes the guests. Priscilla helps with the horses while Anne tries to behave as calmly as possible.

Then Diana arrives and stares at Anne. Anne thinks Diana is reacting to the feathers, but Diana finally explains that Anne’s nose is bright red.

Anne rushes upstairs, discovers that she used red dye instead of lotion, and washes the color away. Her white muslin dress is not available, so she changes into another clean dress and returns to the guests.

There is no elaborate meal prepared this time. Diana goes home and brings back a cooked chicken. Anne adds bread, butter, cheese, fruit cake, and preserved plums, creating a simple lunch from what is available.

The meal goes extremely well. Mrs. Morgan is warm, humorous, and genuinely interested in other people. Anne and Diana soon stop worrying about the menu and enjoy listening to her stories.

After lunch, the group walks through several of Anne’s favorite places, including Lover’s Lane, Violet Vale, the Birch Path, the Haunted Wood, and the Dryad’s Bubble. Mrs. Morgan enjoys hearing how Anne named and imagined these places.

When the visitors leave, Anne realizes that the unexpected visit has been more relaxed and enjoyable than the carefully planned one might have been. That evening, she throws away the freckle lotion and decides that she has spent enough time worrying about the freckles on her nose.

## Conversation 1 — Completely Unprepared

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Diana: Mrs. Morgan really **caught you off guard** by arriving today.
Anne: Completely. I was covered in feathers when they **showed up**.
Diana: I would have panicked.
Anne: I nearly did, but I had to **pull myself together** quickly.
Diana: You did more than that. You **rose to the occasion** and made everyone feel welcome.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- catch someone off guard — surprise someone when they are not prepared. The question caught me off guard, so I needed a moment to answer.
- show up — arrive at a place, sometimes unexpectedly. Two extra guests showed up just before dinner.
- pull yourself together — regain control of your emotions after being upset, nervous, or surprised. She took a minute to pull herself together before going back into the meeting.
- rise to the occasion — perform well when a difficult or unexpected situation requires it. He was nervous, but he rose to the occasion and gave an excellent presentation.

## Conversation 2 — A Lunch That Worked Anyway

*A modern conversation inspired by this chapter.*

Anne: We didn’t have the meal I had imagined, so we just **threw something together**.
Diana: And somehow it all **came together** perfectly.
Anne: Mrs. Morgan seemed more interested in the conversation than the food.
Diana: Exactly. The afternoon **turned out** better than either of us expected.
Anne: I’m glad we were able to **make the best of** the surprise.

### Phrasal verbs & expressions

- throw something together — prepare or create something quickly using what is available. I threw together a quick dinner after work.
- come together — develop successfully as separate parts begin to work well as a whole. The event was chaotic at first, but everything came together by the afternoon.
- turn out — develop or end in a particular way. The trip turned out much better than we expected.
- make the best of something — handle an imperfect situation as positively and effectively as possible. The weather was poor, but we made the best of the day.
